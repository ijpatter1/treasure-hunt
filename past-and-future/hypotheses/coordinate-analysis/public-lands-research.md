## Treasure Location Criteria

From the author's postscript:

- **Within 3 miles of a road**
- **Not buried** - no digging required
- **Safe access** - no cliff scaling, water crossings, dangerous ledges

## Public Lands Near 39°N, 120°W

### 1. Van Sickle Bi-State Park (STRONG MATCH)

**Location:** 30 Lake Pkwy, South Lake Tahoe (38.9549°N, 119.9404°W)
- **Nation's first bi-state park** - straddles CA/NV border
- 542 acres of public land
- "Easy to moderate trails studded with **rock outcroppings**"
- Grand views of Lake Tahoe and peaks
- Short walk from Stateline casinos (road access)
- Connects to Tahoe Rim Trail via Rim Trail Connector
- Historic structures: 1914 log cabin, 1930s cabins
- Picnic tables in fir/pine forests

**Criteria Match:** Public land, rock features, scenic, near road, safe access

---

### 2. Spooner Lake & Backcountry State Park (STRONG MATCH)

**Location:** 39.1076°N, 119.9090°W (just off Highway 50/SR 28 junction)
- **12,000 acres** of forested backcountry
- **50 miles of trails** for hiking
- Features **granite outcrops** and alpine peaks
- Historic **Marlette Flume Trail** (relates to RAIL clue?)
- Snow Valley Peak, Marlette Peak
- Aspen groves and sub-alpine lakes

**Key Feature - Flume Trail:**
- 4.4-mile historic trail carved into granite
- Built 1870s for Comstock Lode mining water supply
- "Trail carved into the granite wall" with vista points
- Connects Spooner Lake to Marlette Lake

**Criteria Match:** Public land, rock formations, near road (Highway 50), safe trails, historic features

---

### 3. Cave Rock State Park

**Location:** Southeast shore of Lake Tahoe (Nevada side)
- **Volcanic dome** - 3 million year old volcanic vent remnant
- Caves carved by lake waves
- Short 0.8-mile trail, easy hike
- **CAUTION:** Sacred Washoe site (De'ek Wadapush)
- Climbing/some activities restricted
- Scramble to top is steep/jagged

**Criteria Match:** Public land, dramatic rock, near road, but cultural restrictions and steep scramble

---

### 4. Tahoe Rim Trail (Multiple Access Points)

- **165-mile loop** around Lake Tahoe
- Crosses CA/NV state line multiple times
- Passes through multiple state parks and national forests
- Access points near roads throughout
- Crosses Monument Pass at 8800 feet on state line

**Key Segment - Tahoe Meadows to Spooner:**
- Rich history (logging/mining)
- Multiple peaks
- Stunning views

---

### 5. Mount Rose Wilderness Area

**Location:** Northeast side of Lake Tahoe (Nevada)
- Mount Rose: 10,776 feet (3rd tallest in basin)
- Most popular hiking trail in Nevada
- 11 miles round trip, 2,500 ft elevation gain
- **Public land** - wilderness area
- Trailhead parking at top of Mount Rose Highway

**Criteria Match:** Public, spectacular views, but may be too strenuous for "leisurely afternoon"

---

### 6. Armstrong Pass / Freel Peak Area (NOTABLE)

**Location:** South Lake Tahoe area, accessed via Forest Service Rd 051
- **Armstrong Pass** at 8,700 feet - Tahoe Rim Trail junction
- Freel Peak: **Highest peak in Tahoe Basin** at 10,881 feet
- Sits on border between Lake Tahoe Basin and Humboldt-Toiyabe National Forest
- **Star Lake** - 3 miles from Armstrong Pass on TRT
- Surrounded by public land on all sides

**Notable:** ARMSTRONG contains "STRONG" - potential wordplay connection?

**Natural Features:**
- Western White Pine, Whitebark Pine, Sierra Juniper
- Wildflowers: Dwarf Phlox, Pygmy Fleabane, Pacific Hulsea, **purple lupines**
- Birds: Clark's Nutcracker, Brewer's Sparrow, Rock Wren
- Decomposed granite terrain
- Ancient Junipers and giant pines
- 360-degree panoramic views

**Trail Details:**
- 4 miles from trailhead to Armstrong Pass
- Trail crosses creek via wide footbridge
- Incredible vistas of Meyers, Tahoe Keys, Crystal Range
- TRT marker at Armstrong Pass junction

**Access:**
- 3.5 miles of dirt road (4WD/high clearance recommended)
- Gate usually closed Nov 1 - May 31
- Trailhead at end of Oneida Road

**Criteria Match:** Public land, rock features, scenic, but access requires effort
