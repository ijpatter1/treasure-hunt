### Stanza 3: Navigation

|------|------------------|-------------------|
| "more on your own" | Less obvious path from this point | Trail becomes fainter or requires navigation |
| "path is always drawing nigh" | Path stays close/approaches | Following a trail that winds toward destination |
| "compass points...towards the home" | Directional navigation | Compass bearing leads to specific landmark cluster |
| "point and tree and seeing eye" | Three landmarks | A point (peak? rock point?), a distinctive tree, and a "seeing eye" (viewpoint? eye-shaped feature?) |

**NM Interpretation:**
This suggests navigating toward a trio of landmarks. In NM mountains, "point" could be:

- A named rock formation
- Trail junction point

"Seeing eye" could be:
- A natural arch (eye-shaped)
- A viewpoint
- A rock formation resembling an eye

### Stanza 6: Final Instructions

```
So listen well and hear me all,
your efforts will be worth the high.
If you can mark warm water's halt,
you'll bring it back full weight and dry.
```

| Line | Fact (from poem) | NM Interpretation |
|------|------------------|-------------------|
| "worth the high" | Reward for effort; possibly elevation reference | High elevation location; significant elevation gain |
| "warm water's halt" | Where warm water stops | Hot spring that cools? Thermal feature? Confluence point? |
| "full weight and dry" | Box retrieved intact, dry | Not submerged; protected from elements |

**Critical "Warm Water's Halt" Interpretation:**
In Fenn's poem, "where warm waters halt" was the starting point. Here it's presented as the key to success. In NM, this could be:
- Ojo Caliente hot springs area (warm water halts/cools)
- A geothermal feature
- Where a warmer stream meets cooler water
- Metaphorical (where "warm" feeling stops?)
