## Chapter Summary

The author uses the Chavin de Huantar temple in Peru as an extended metaphor for confirmation bias. When Spanish explorers first encountered the 45-foot walls of this temple (built ~900 BC), they wrongly assumed it was made by giants or was an ancient fortress. The truth: it was a religious temple that united the Chavin culture for over a thousand years through spiritual influence, not military force.

The temple is described as "a gigantic puzzle box with no discernible entrance" - visitors were given psychedelics and led through a labyrinth of narrow tunnels featuring special effects (torchlight, embedded nails creating sparkling reflections, mirrors). The gold chalice in the treasure may have been used in ceremonies here.

The chapter transitions to a personal story: at age 4, the author (Jon) disrupted his father's Christmas Eve candlelight vigil by loudly singing "Here Comes Peter Cottontail" - an Easter song. His mother fled the 200-person congregation with him. The moral: even his "sweet mother" fell victim to confirmation bias.
