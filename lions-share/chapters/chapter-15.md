## Chapter Summary

This is the author's "favorite story in the book." Wilma Rudolph was born in 1940 in Clarksville, Tennessee, the 20th of 22 children. Premature at 4.5 pounds, she contracted polio at age 5 and was paralyzed in her left leg, fitted with a steel brace. Doctors said she might never walk again.

For four years, she took twice-weekly bus rides 50 miles to Nashville's Meharry Medical School for rehabilitation. At age 9, she walked into church without her brace; at 12, she removed it permanently. She then turned to basketball (Coach Clinton Grey) and later track (Coach Ed Temple at Tennessee State). After losing every race at her first competition in Tuskegee, Alabama, she trained with renewed determination.

At the 1960 Rome Olympics, despite spraining her ankle the day before competition, Wilma won gold medals in the 100-meter, 200-meter, and 400-meter relay - setting world records in all three events. She became the first American woman to win three gold medals in a single Olympics.

Muhammad Ali (then Cassius Clay, age 18) also won gold at the 1960 Rome Olympics. He allegedly threw his medal off the Second Street Bridge after being refused restaurant service. He received a replacement medal at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics.
