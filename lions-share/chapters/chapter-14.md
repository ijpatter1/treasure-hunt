## Chapter Summary

The chapter explores failure as a path to success through two stories: Louis Comfort Tiffany's eight years of experimentation to create Favrile glass (unveiled 1893), and Henry David Thoreau's accidental fire that burned 300+ acres in Concord, Massachusetts on April 30, 1844.

Tiffany (son of Charles Tiffany, founder of Tiffany & Co.) was a masterful painter before shifting to glass. President Chester Arthur commissioned him to decorate the White House in 1882. His enamel pieces were so prized that only 750 were ever made, and Tiffany kept 90 in his private collection.

Thoreau's fire may have led to his Walden Pond retreat a year later. His 24-year journal (2 million+ words) became the source for all his public writings. Two pages from 1851 containing his first "art of life" writings are in the treasure.

The author shares his personal struggles with fear of failure - wanting parental approval, avoiding rejection, stressing about money as a child in a family of five. The book "Failing Forward" by John Maxwell changed his perspective.
