## Chapter Summary

Alexander the Great became king of Macedonia in 336 BC, ruling for 13 years until his death at 32. He conquered 3,000 miles from Macedonia to India, renamed 20+ cities "Alexandria," and his conquests brought vast gold back to Greece, spurring goldsmithing.

The author acquired four gold items from this era. Each connects to Greek mythology: olive/oak leaves (Zeus, wisdom), goat/ibex (Dionysus, fertility), snakes (Asklepios, healing - now AMA symbol), and the Club of Hercules (protection). Amethyst originated from a Dionysus myth and was believed to prevent drunkenness.

The chapter concludes with a plea against modern divisive myths - that race, religion, culture, or politics make people fundamentally different. "We and our enemies, if we have them, are mostly the same."

The chapter closes (p.188) with a large aerial/overhead photograph of a crowd arranged to form a heart shape, and the chapter-closing line: "Our next chapter is a love story." (foreshadowing Ch. 22, Picasso's Pendant - "A Love Story").
