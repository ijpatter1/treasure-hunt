## Chapter Summary

The author shares two perspective shifts about items he initially excluded from the treasure. First, he found only dark smokey quartz as a child while digging for crystals (Chapter 4 callback), which made him associate it with "disappointment and unrewarded toil." When he saw Mark Oros's cut stone without knowing what it was, he finally appreciated smokey quartz's beauty.

Second, at age 9, the author learned his January birthstone was garnet - the same red rock used in asphalt on his street. While his father and sister had rubies, his friend had emerald, and his baby brother had aquamarine, he felt cheated. After 40 years of disgust, he discovered the rare green tsavorite garnet (discovered by Campbell Bridges in Tanzania after a buffalo charged him into a ditch).

The author walked through a "massive rose garden" (several acres) at a local park with his wife Kimberly. He uses roses as a metaphor for perspective - a single flower at ground level can't see the garden's beauty the way someone standing can.
