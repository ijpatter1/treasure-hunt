## Chapter Summary

The chapter tells the dramatic story of Captain Felicio de Fonseca and the Nuestra Señora de la Luz (Our Lady of the Light), which sank July 2, 1752 near Montevideo during a violent pampero storm. The ship carried 12,500 gold coins for King Ferdinand VII of Spain (50/50 split contract) plus a hidden cargo in the powder hold: 200,000 pesos in gold coins and 40 gold bars/disks that the King didn't know about.

The overloaded ship (185 passengers, 12 cannons, animals, rations) was anchored in the deadly Rio de la Plata - "one of the deadliest shipwreck graveyards in the world" with over 1,000 ships lost. When the pampero struck with 180 km/h winds, Fonseca couldn't reach his ship. No passengers survived. Captain Fonseca recovered over 90% of the official cargo within a year, but the hidden treasure wasn't found until 1993 when Ruben Corella discovered the wreckage 50 miles from Montevideo.

The moral: greed sank the La Luz - the captain's avarice led to an overloaded ship that couldn't survive the storm.
