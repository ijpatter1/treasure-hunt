## The best STORY in full (the "Make It Make Sense" deliverable)

Start with the author's own instruction. Jon Collins-Black titled his **confirmed**
solve-guidance chapter (Ch21) *Antiquities of Alexander -- Make It Make Sense* and said to
**look beyond just ancient myths and legends and consider more**. The prior adversarial
passes read "Antiquities of Alexander" as Alexander-the-Great mythology, found the myth a
dead end, and threw the chapter out as a "name coincidence." That is precisely the move
JCB warned against. *Look beyond the myth.* What is left when you set the Macedonian king
aside is the bare **word Alexander** -- and on a US map that word is a **county in western
North Carolina**. The faith-lens calls this its single strongest leap, and it is the
method JCB *handed* us: stop chasing kings, look at the map for the same word.

The same key turns a second lock. The book is titled *There's Treasure Inside*. Inside
Alexander County sits **Hiddenite** -- don't romanticize "hidden treasure," go to the town
literally named **Hidden**-ite. It is the site of Emerald Hollow, the only public
emerald-digging mine in North America, and the place JCB **grew up hunting treasure from
the ground** (Ch4 roots him "some miles up the road" from it). A hidden-treasure book, by
a gem-digging author, pointing at the word *Alexander*, which contains a town named
*Hidden*. That is not one wink; it is the same wink delivered three times -- wordplay,
myth-decoy, and biography all closing on one county. This is the *faith* move the pass
exists to make: act on the reading before it can be proven, because the *story* is too
clean to be accident.

The gem thread repeats and locks the corridor. Ch4 is the **Chivor emerald** (-> Hiddenite,
Alexander); Ch18 is **Carnegie's emerald** -- the real "Carolina Emperor" emerald found in
NC -- anchored to **Statesville/Iredell**, the next county over, whose name "States-ville"
even winks at one-box-per-*state*. Two emerald chapters, two adjacent western-NC counties,
the same green stone that put Hiddenite on the map. "Blaze the Path" (Ch4's subtitle) reads
almost literally: blaze a trail to the emeralds.

Now the landform itself answers to the name. The public natural area in this county is
**Rocky FACE Mountain** -- and the *Lion's* Share, the king of beasts, wants a lion's face.
"Rocky FACE" is a name-and-shape double: a granite-faced monadnock standing alone above the
Piedmont. It is the one botg site already attempted, which the failure passes treated as
*eliminated* -- but only a *fraction* of its ~318 acres was searched. The right read is not
"falsified" but "right mountain, wrong acre."

The terrain-intuition lens then tells us *where on that mountain*, and it converges hard.
Read as pure sensory choreography, Joy's Serenade walks you UP through shade and water and
then "the view opens onto lower country" -- the exact bodily feeling of an **isolated front
range** (you only get "lower country opening" on a monadnock standing alone, not deep in
the Blue Ridge interior where higher peaks surround you). Five separate intuition reads
pile onto this one massif: the escarpment "lip" (L1), the oak-and-outcrop ridgecrest (L5),
"pike are rather all around" = standing on an open dome ringed by distant peaks (L13), the
rural dark-sky notch between the Statesville and Hickory light-domes (L11), and the quiet
contemplative overlook where "the haiku curls a little further on" (L12). Critically, two
of the sharpest reads say *don't take the summit*: the poem's paradox -- "if you don't go
DOWN you may never know how HIGH" -- reads as a real body-instruction to step down off the
wooded top onto a **sub-summit shelf** where the height finally hits and a sit-rock waits
(L14); and the gentler "cove" read pulls to a **sheltered draw with a streamlet and a
seating boulder** at the foot of the Brushies near Hiddenite/Stony Point, with a *framed*
view-gap rather than a grand panorama (L9). Both resolve the apparent shade-vs-view tension
the same way: the box is one bend *past* where treasure hunters stop -- exactly JCB's
hide-in-plain-sight-but-overlooked instinct ("Joy Is in the Details," Ch3).

So the abductive best-story: **the Lion's Share sits on Rocky Face Mountain in the Hiddenite
district of Alexander County** -- the one place where the wordplay (Alexander -> Hiddenite
-> "there's treasure inside" -> Rocky FACE/lion), the mineralogy (emeralds, the author's
boyhood craft), the literal-engine #1 ranking, and *five* independent sensory-intuition
reads all make the same sense at once, on public, road-near, snow-getting, walkable county
land that is ~80% unsearched. Nine of nine lenses close here. Nothing else in the entire
pass comes close.

## Honest self-check

**Genuinely compelling:**
- The **Alexander -> Hiddenite -> *There's Treasure Inside* -> Rocky FACE** wordplay stack
  is the strongest single thread in the whole hunt. It is multi-layered, it is *exactly*
  the "look beyond the myth at the word" move JCB's own guidance chapter endorses, and it
  needs no cipher -- only association, which this pass explicitly admits.
- The convergence is real and broad: **9 of 9 lenses** land here, from completely different
  starting points (wordplay, myth, author-psychology, terrain-feel, item-metaphor), and it
  is **externally corroborated by JCB's biography**. That is the discipline this pass cares
  about, satisfied more emphatically than anywhere else in the corpus.
- The terrain-intuition *micro*-reads are unusually concrete and mutually reinforcing
  (sub-summit shelf, cove-one-bend-past, "pike all around" on the open dome), which turns a
  ~318-acre area into a testable search pattern rather than a vague pin.

**Where it's a stretch / the load-bearing leaps (what we're betting on):**
1. **Load-bearing leap #1:** that "Antiquities of Alexander" means **Alexander County, NC**
   rather than Alexander-the-Great. The entire pinpoint rests on this. It is the most
   author-coherent read and JCB's own stated method, but it is still a read.
2. **Load-bearing leap #2:** that the cluster's center of gravity (Hiddenite/Rocky Face) is
   also the *hide site* and not merely a thematic flag while the box sits elsewhere in the
   corridor. The convergence proves the **corridor and the mountain**; the *acre* is carried
   by the intuition lens (cove-vs-crest), which is the softest evidence type.
3. **The "right mountain, wrong acre" comfort is double-edged.** It neatly explains the prior
   near-miss -- but a story that can absorb its own past failure without updating is exactly
   where motivated coherence hides. Treat the unsearched 80% as a genuine test, not a reward.
4. **Motivated-coherence risk, named:** NC was already the prior favorite, so a 9/9 sweep
   partly reflects that several lenses share one origin (the author's NC home ground). The
   convergence synthesis itself down-weights shared-origin stacking; even so, the creative
   pass cast a wide national net and *failed to surface any non-NC cluster above 2 lenses*.
   That can mean NC is genuinely strongest -- or that the net, run by NC-primed lenses, was
   not truly independent. Both are live. The honesty move is to keep the fresh leads warm.
5. **Cluster 4 (Statesville/Iredell)** and the Chapel Hill thread are weak as *sites* -- they
   are personality/origin fingerprints that pull the center toward Hiddenite, not pins.

Bottom line: **high confidence in the corridor and in Rocky Face Mountain; moderate
confidence in the specific acre.** The bet is that the Alexander/Hiddenite wordplay is
intentional and that the prior search simply missed the right shelf or cove.

## Concrete NEXT TEST for the top reading (faith -> action)

**Primary target -- the unsearched majority of Rocky Face Mountain Recreational Area,
Hiddenite, Alexander County, NC.** Within hard constraints (public county land, <=3 mi from
a road, not buried, no cliff/ledge/water-crossing, single-day walkable, snow-getting),
search in this priority order, driven by the converging intuition reads:

1. **Sub-summit shelves (L14):** step *down* off the wooded summit onto the exposed granite
   shelves/benches just below the crest where the view first opens -- gentle shelves only,
   no ledge danger. The poem's "don't go down -> how high" maneuver.
2. **One bend *past* each overlook (L2, L12):** at every developed overlook / natural
   sit-rock, keep going one meander further -- "the haiku curls a little further on." Check
   the natural seating boulder just beyond where hikers stop.
3. **Sheltered cove / streamlet draw near Hiddenite-Stony Point (L9, L15):** a tender,
   enclosed hollow with a seating boulder, a small creek that shimmers in midday sun, oak
   shade, and a *framed* view-gap (not a grand panorama). Look on the cooler north/east
   flank. (Rocky Face is documented to have creeks + oak woodland.)
4. **Open-dome summit ring (L13):** if testing the crest read, stand on the bare granite and
   find where "pike are rather all around" (the peak ring) is most complete; check sit-rock
   crevices/outcrops there.

**Constraint guardrail:** aim every Hiddenite-area lead at **public, accessible parcels
ADJACENT** to the emerald belt -- **never the private pay-to-dig mines themselves** (that
would break the public-land rule). On any Brushy-spine extension, stay on confirmed public
land (Stone Mountain SP, Pores Knob if public, Carolina Thread Trail segment); **Love
Valley and Hibriten Mtn are private/legally closed** -- excluded.

**What would confirm the reading (the faith-payoff):** an on-the-ground feature that
re-states the wordplay at a public, road-near, snow-getting, walkable spot -- an
"Alexander," "hidden," emerald/green, or lion/face motif (named rock, interpretive sign,
distinctive sitting boulder) at the cove or shelf. That is the author winking back.
