## The biography-counted picture: NC vs. CA on DISTINCT (non-same-fact) anchors

With biography now a legitimate pointing channel, the decisive contrast is between North Carolina and California — and it is **not close** once the independence rule is applied honestly.

**North Carolina — THREE distinct, separately-sourced anchors that stay SEPARATE:**
1. **Hiddenite / Alexander County** — JCB's childhood gem-dig (Ch4 p.48). Biography. *Distinct fact.*
2. **Chapel Hill / UNC** — JCB's college years, via the Daily Tar Heel (Ch2 p.28), reinforced (not double-counted) by Jordan/UNC item-provenance (Ch13). Biography. *Distinct fact, different life-stage, different place.*
3. **Bakersville / Mitchell County** — Seth Gould forged all five boxes there (Ch3 p.40, "forty miles NE of Asheville," with a bearing-and-distance — the strongest single region-pointer in the book). Item-provenance + author-relationship. *Distinct source.*

What COLLAPSES within NC (correctly, anti-inflation): the NC dossier merges the Hiddenite childhood-dig with the unnamed pastor-father/Waldenbooks hometown into ONE childhood anchor, because they are almost certainly the same childhood location described from two angles. That is a conservative, honest move — it keeps NC at 3, not an inflated 4. (Validator note: there IS in fact a *direct* textual Statesville signal the dossier wrongly dismissed — Ch18 p.161, "they helped start and lead a new church in Statesville, NC" — a separately-sourced biographical anchor. Counting it would arguably push NC toward 4 distinct anchors; the dossier's caution is honest in spirit but factually under-counts. We keep the validated figure at the conservative **3**.)

**California — ONE anchor, no matter how many times it appears:**
- Every LA mention across Ch1/Ch2/Ch3/Ch12/Ch18/Ch19 ("called California home for over twenty years"; "not far from my home, just to the northeast" of Mt Wilson; "living near a large city") is the **same single residence fact**. Repeated mentions of one fact collapse to one point. CA = **1** distinct biographical anchor.
- Everything that makes CA *look* dominant — the Gold Rush nugget (item-provenance), Loloma murals / Alcatraz / Bowers / Getty (exhibition-display), Apple/Jobs (story-color) — is NOT biography and NOT hide-pointing. It collapses out entirely.

**Net:** NC has **3 distinct anchors** (genuine convergence); CA has **1** (a home, the weakest possible hide-locator). The biography ruling *helps* NC far more than CA, because NC's anchors are genuinely separate facts while CA's are one fact restated. (Integrity correction carried from validation: the NC dossier's supporting prose contains a **fabricated author preference** — "I am not a fan of the cold" attributed to Intro+Postscript does NOT exist in the ledger — and a **misquoted poem line** ("Begin where waters meet"; the real line is "There may be magic in the water"). ## The discriminator layer (Group P/Q — region-agnostic terrain, flora, access)

The region-agnostic signals — water ("magic in the water"), shady oaks, sit-rock, elevation/vista where the view opens onto lower ground, blue-and-pink spring wildflowers, dark sky, plus the hard-filters (≤3 mi from road, public land, not buried, no dangerous water, snowy winters, bear/snake habitat) — are **comparators, not pointers.** They can fail to exclude a region or tip a tie; they cannot pick one.

- **Best terrain fit: North Carolina Blue Ridge.** It scores well on water/confluence, oak-hickory cove forest, granite/gneiss outcrops, an eastern-escarpment "view opens onto the lower country" landform that maps cleanly onto the Ch4 vantage-as-key teaching, Catawba rhododendron (pink) + bluets (blue) spring bloom, accessible Pisgah-NF/Roan public land within 3 mi of roads, and native black bear + timber rattlesnake. This is a strong *compatibility* result.
- **CA mountains** also satisfy nearly every discriminator — but only in the Sierra/San Gabriel high country, NOT the LA/Bay lowlands where its one anchor sits; the dark-sky and snowy-winter filters push any CA hide UP and AWAY from the residence into generic montane terrain with no independent CA pointer. Net edge: none.
- **AZ high desert, MA flat Concord, the urban Bay/TI/Santa Ana/NYC** each fail one or more emphasized discriminators (water/oaks fail the desert; elevation-vista and dark-sky fail flat/urban Concord and the Bay).

**Hard-filter note on "snowy winters":** the Postscript "Do not search in winter… bitter cold and snow" is a *searchability* hard-filter, and it actually IMPLIES at least one box is in a region that gets snow — which is compatible with the snowy NC Blue Ridge and does NOT favor a warm-region read. (This corrects the dossier's now-struck "not a fan of cold" prose.)

**Caveat (load-bearing):** NC's excellent terrain fit is a **tiebreaker that reinforces an already-leading region — it is NOT itself a pointer.** The southern Appalachians broadly (NC/TN/GA/VA) and parts of the Western US satisfy the same cues. Terrain confirms NC is *plausible*; it does not prove NC is *the* place.

## THE KEY VERDICT — Is the region DERIVABLE from the in-text signals, or is the ledger REGION-SPARSE?

**Honest answer: PARTIALLY derivable to the STATE/sub-state level, but the ledger is genuinely REGION-SPARSE and does NOT yield a hide pinpoint. The specific spot must be teased out by external Phase-B research.**

Reasoning:

1. **What the convergence engine CAN derive:** The in-text signals do produce one real result — North Carolina (Blue Ridge gem belt) is the single region with genuine multi-source convergence (3 distinct anchors), and it is the only region above "thin." Every competitor collapses to one source or zero. So the ledger *does* discriminate: it ranks NC clearly first. To the extent the question is "which region does the book point at hardest," the answer is derivable, and it is NC.

2. **Why it is NOT a pinpoint, and why that is structural, not a failure of effort:**
   - The strongest *named* NC place (Bakersville) is **craftsman-provenance** — where the boxes were *made*, with no statement anything was *hidden* near it. Lone-association risk on the very anchor that looks strongest.
   - The other two NC anchors are **biography** (where the author is *from*), which a hider may use OR deliberately avoid — undecidable from the text.
   - One NC anchor (Hiddenite/Alexander → Rocky Face Mtn) had a prior BOTG attempt — but Rocky Face is ~320 acres and only a small sub-area was checked. "Not where I looked" ≠ "not there." This anchor remains FULLY LIVE; the convergence is NOT spent.
   - MODERATE is the ceiling: NC clears multi-anchor convergence but cannot reach "strong" because no signal says "hidden near here."

3. **The region-sparsity is real and broad:** Across 937 signals, the overwhelming majority are role (e) item-provenance (object origins, mostly abroad or decoy), role (b) region-agnostic discriminators, and role (d) method teachings. Genuine role (a) region-pointing-at-the-HIDE signals are scarce — which is exactly why NYC's "5 groups" collapse to 0 hide-pointers and why ten regions land at THIN/collapses. The book was built so that *frequency ≠ pointing*; the convergence engine has now extracted essentially all the hide-pointing value the literal text contains.

4. **Therefore the next narrowing must come from Phase B — external RESEARCH, not more in-text counting.** The literal ledger has been mined to its useful limit. The remaining leverage is in: (a) **item-provenance-to-ground** — chasing the real-world geography behind the treasure objects to a specific accessible public site; (b) **the children's book** (a separate JCB artifact that may carry geography the main book withholds); and (c) **topic-tangents** the author flagged ("research captivating topics further"), pursued to ground truth.

**Bottom line: the region is research-needed.** The text gets you to "North Carolina Blue Ridge, lean Bakersville/Mitchell (un-refuted) over Hiddenite/Alexander (failed)," but the actual hide location is not derivable from the ledger as it stands. Convergence-counting has done its job and reached its limit.
