## STANZA 2 CONNECTIONS

> "Our conductor, as with a trace of a baton through the air, gives direction. While the coda is incomplete, there's a flutter of excitement around."

### 2A. "CONDUCTOR" — Chapter 11 (La Luz / Shipwreck)

**The word "conductor" has three primary meanings:**

1. **Musical conductor** — directs an orchestra with a baton, "gives direction"
2. **Railroad conductor** — directs a train along tracks
3. **Electrical conductor** — carries current

**Ch 11 Connection (La Luz):** The ship's captain "directs" the vessel, like a conductor directs music. The ship name "Nuestra Senora de la Luz" means "Our Lady of the Light" — light/direction. But the geographic references are all South American (Montevideo, Rio de la Plata), with no NC relevance.

**Alexander Railroad Connection (STRONG):**
The Alexander Railroad (the "June Bug Line") ran through Alexander County, connecting Statesville to Taylorsville, passing through the Hiddenite area. A railroad conductor literally "gives direction" along these tracks. The railroad's route points toward Rocky Face Mountain.

**Additional "conductor" connection — Chapter 5 (Art Smith):**
Art Smith's jewelry career was launched through **Talley Beatty**, a **choreographer** (someone who "conducts" dancers). Beatty worked with **Duke Ellington**, a famous bandleader/conductor. The connection chain: conductor -> Ellington -> Beatty -> Art Smith -> jewelry in treasure.

### 2B. "BATON" — What traces through the air?

A conductor's baton traces an arc through the air. "Baton" in French means "stick" or "staff." This connects forward to Stanza 3's geometric language ("turning square or any arc").

This matches the author's statement in Ch 8: "I decided to leave an extra clue or two within my children's book about the location of our treasure." The coda (ending/resolution) requires the children's book "Our Unbreakable Thread" to complete.

## STANZA 3 CONNECTIONS

> "See how far to go. Use will's straight edge, as the turning square or any arc may align at a proper point. Never despair, never doubt. This is no imaginary wonderland. Now look."

### 3A. "WILL'S STRAIGHT EDGE" — Chapter 4 (Hiddenite/Emerald)

**William Earl Hidden** (1853-1918) was the mineralogist who discovered the mineral hiddenite in 1879 in what is now Alexander County, NC. The town of Hiddenite is named after him. The author states he grew up "only some miles up the road" from the Hiddenite emerald mine (Ch 4, p.48).

**"Will's straight edge" = William Hidden's prospecting line/direction.**

Hidden was sent to North Carolina by Thomas Edison to search for platinum. He prospected systematically, following mineral veins in straight lines through the terrain. His "straight edge" could refer to:
1. The direction of the mineral vein he followed
2. A literal compass bearing from Hiddenite toward Rocky Face Mountain
3. The concept of following a direct, unswerving path

**CRITICAL VISUAL FINDING: The explorer silhouette illustration (p.54).** The figures walk from left to right toward mountains. In the text, Restrepo follows a single clue (the vista) to find the Chivor mine. The parallel is explicit: follow a single directional clue to find the treasure. The mountains in the illustration have jagged peaks — "pike" in dialect = peaked hills. The figures are moving TOWARD the peaks, matching the poem's instruction "don't go down" and "how high you ever want to be."

### 3B. "TURNING SQUARE" — Chapter 3 (Puzzle Box)

**A turning square is a drafting/surveying tool** — also called a try square or set square. It creates right angles. In combination with a straight edge and arc (compass), these are the fundamental tools of geometric construction.

**Ch 3 (Puzzle Box) Connection:**
- Japanese puzzle boxes require sliding panels in specific sequences — geometric movements
- The Lion's Share box requires "seven actions" to open
- Seth Gould's box design uses "a variety of dynamics to entice and challenge"
- The puzzle box's geometric mechanisms echo the geometric language of Stanza 3

**Ch 3 Visual Clues:**

| Page | Image | Analysis |
|------|-------|----------|
| p.38 | Japanese Himitsu-Bako puzzle box | The box surface has **geometric wood-inlay patterns** — diamonds, crosses, zigzag lines in brown/cream/dark tones. These patterns include what appear to be **star patterns** (hexagonal) and **diamond/square patterns rotated 45 degrees** — literally "turning squares." The pattern is called Yosegi-zaiku, made by cutting cross-sections of composite wood blocks at angles. |
| p.41 | Lion's Share treasure box | Metal box with clean geometric lines. Yellow brass sides, steel top edge, textured iron center. The box is rectangular — a straight-edged form. |

**FINDING:** The Japanese puzzle box's Yosegi-zaiku pattern literally contains "turning squares" — geometric patterns created by rotating square cross-sections at various angles. The instruction "use will's straight edge, as the turning square or any arc may align at a proper point" could be decoded as:

1. Start with a straight line from William Hidden's location (Hiddenite)
2. Apply a right-angle turn (turning square) or follow a curve (arc)
3. These geometric operations will "align at a proper point" = the treasure location

### 3C. "ANY ARC MAY ALIGN AT A PROPER POINT"

An arc is a portion of a circle's circumference. In surveying, you use a straight edge (ruler), a square (for right angles), and an arc (protractor or compass) to triangulate a point.

**Possible geometric decoding:**
- Draw a straight line (Will's straight edge) from Hiddenite
- Apply a turn (turning square) at some angle
- Measure an arc (distance along a curve)
- The intersection = "a proper point" = marker 318 on Rocky Face trail

## CHAPTER 21: "ANTIQUITIES OF ALEXANDER" — The Hidden Connection

Chapter 21 is titled "Antiquities of **Alexander**" with the subtitle "**Make It Make Sense**." The author explicitly states: "If you've been thoughtful, you may have considered that a chapter titled 'Make It Make Sense' might also be a reference to figuring out the solution for the location of the treasure I have hidden."

Alexander = **Alexander County, NC** = where Hiddenite and Rocky Face Mountain are located.

The gold wreath in Ch 21 features **oak and olive leaves with acorn accents** — "shady oaks" from Joy's Serenade Stanza 5. The treasure items in this chapter (gold wreath with oak leaves) echo the physical environment of the treasure location (chestnut oaks on Rocky Face).

The instruction to "look beyond just ancient myths and legends and consider more" means: don't just read about Alexander the Great — consider that "Alexander" points to Alexander County, NC.
