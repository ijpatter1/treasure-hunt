# Joy's Serenade as a Trail Guide to Rocky Face Mountain

## The Navigational Reading

The poem reads as a sequential set of directions from the Hiddenite area UP Rocky Face Mountain.

---

### Stanza 1-2: The Setup
> A note, the molten tone of a tune, rests inside all of us who want a song to follow, a treasure to hold; we may find music in a poem.

"A song to follow" = directions to follow. "A treasure to hold" = the Lion's Share. "We may find music in a poem" = the clues are IN this poem.

> Our conductor, as with a trace of a baton through the air, gives direction.

"Conductor gives direction" = the Alexander Railroad ("June Bug Line") runs from Statesville toward Taylorsville/Hiddenite. A quarry spur once reached Rocky Face itself. Follow the railroad's direction. Also: the poem itself is your conductor, giving you direction.

> While the coda is incomplete, there's a flutter of excitement around.

"Coda incomplete" = more clues elsewhere in the book. "Flutter" = butterflies (Coral Hairstreak at Rocky Face).

---

### Stanza 3: Navigation Instructions
> See how far to go. Use will's straight edge, as the turning square or any arc may align at a proper point.

"Will's straight edge" = William Earl Hidden's prospecting path. Follow the direction Hidden blazed through this area. Use mapping tools (straight edge + square + arc) to identify the exact point on Rocky Face.

> Never despair, never doubt. This is no imaginary wonderland. Now look.

This is a REAL place. Look around you.

---

### Stanza 4: The Pivot — From Streams to Peaks
> You can take your own path, an X leads the way, or simply follow shimmering circles of gold.

Navigate by trail markers (X at intersections) or follow the shimmering mica-gold water pools on the granite surface.

> There may be magic in the water, but the pike are rather all around.

**THE KEY LINE.** "Magic in the water" = the gem-bearing streams of the Hiddenite area (Emerald Hollow Mine, creeks with mica and gems). "BUT" = a redirect. "The pike are rather all around" = look UP at the peaked hills surrounding you (Pores Knob, Brushy Mountains, Grandfather Mountain visible on clear days).

**Meaning: Don't stay in the streams. Go UP to the peaks. The treasure is on the mountain, not in the water.**

---

### Stanza 5: The Ascent
> If you get warm, you may find shady oaks.

As you climb Rocky Face, you enter the **chestnut oak forest** that dominates the Piedmont Monadnock Forest community. The oaks provide shade on the trail. "Getting warm" = you're getting closer (hot/cold game) AND physically warming from the climb.

> When close, the haiku curls a little further on where few have seen it.

The back cover poem (5-7-6 near-haiku) provides the final clue. The treasure is slightly further than where you think — "curls a little further on." It's in a spot "where few have seen it" — off the main trail, in a hidden nook of the granite.

> And if you don't go down, you may never know how high you ever want to be.

**Don't descend.** Stay high. Keep climbing toward the summit. The treasure is at elevation, not in the valleys.

---

### Stanza 6: The Summit Experience
> The sky smiles on you. Dancers are on the land.

Open sky at the exposed granite summit. **Coral Hairstreak butterflies** ("dancers") in the oak habitat on the mountain.

> And if you stay to see the night, it will ignite with the flame of starlight.

You can **camp on the summit** (Hollow Rock campsite). From the granite dome in rural Alexander County, the night sky blazes with stars — just as the author saw growing up nearby.

---

### Stanza 7: At the Treasure
> Now that you're here, all that is left remains for you.

You've arrived. Everything remaining ("all that is left") is yours. Possible directional: "left" = go left at a trail junction?

> And if you spy others warming with the sunlight, or sat upon a rock, allowing nature's wide embrace to sustain their bliss, maybe ask them to assist you.

Other hikers are sitting on the **granite surfaces** of Rocky Face in the sunshine. The park gets 200,000 visitors/year — people ARE there. You might need help finding or retrieving the box (it could be in a crevice or hidden nook in the granite).

> You can sing harmonies as you travel home.

You'll be joyful on the drive home. "Harmonies" = multiple voices singing together, just as the multiple clue threads have harmonized to bring you here.

## The Back Cover Poem as Rocky Face Description

"Be solid, have grit; sparkle even as you pine. Here lies a joy divined."

| Word | Rocky Face Connection |
|------|---------------------|
| Solid | Granite — solid rock |
| Grit | The quarry literally produced crushed granite = grit |
| Sparkle | Hiddenite minerals — mica, quartz, emeralds sparkle |
| Pine | Table Mountain pine, Virginia pine, shortleaf pine on the mountain |
| Here lies | The treasure rests here |
| Joy | The treasure ("joy") AND the experience of finding it |
| Divined | Discovered through intuition — like William Hidden divined gems here |

## The Complete Story

The author grew up near Hiddenite, NC, digging in dirt for crystals. "Some miles up the road" was the Emerald Hollow Mine, where William Earl Hidden discovered the mineral hiddenite in 1879. The author's love of gems, nature, and treasure began HERE.

Decades later, he created a treasure hunt. He hid his most personal box — the Lion's Share, with "pieces of my soul" — where it all started. On Rocky Face Mountain, a granite dome in Hiddenite, surrounded by the peaked hills (pike) of the Brushy Mountains, shaded by chestnut oaks, covered in sparkling mica, with pine trees on its slopes.

The poem guides you: start at the streams (magic in the water), look up at the peaks (pike all around), climb through the oaks (shady oaks), reach the granite summit (sat upon a rock), and find what few have seen — a hidden treasure on a mountain in a town named for a man who found hidden treasure.

William Hidden found hidden gems.
You will find hidden treasure.
Here lies a joy divined.
