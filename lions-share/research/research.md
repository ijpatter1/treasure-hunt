| Species | Colors | Habitat | Notes |

| **Southern Blue Flag (Iris virginica)** | Light blue to violet with white markings | Moist/boggy areas, wet meadows | 2-2.5 ft tall |

**Key Details:**
- Dwarf Crested Iris is "arguably one of the most beautiful perennials native to North Carolina"

- Over 3,000 wildflower species in western NC mountains

**Pink Flowers:** Pink Lady's Slipper (Moccasin Flower) is native to NC mountains - NC Significantly Rare species

**Location Relevance:** SUPPORTS NC - Native blue/purple irises grow naturally in western NC mountain woodlands and rocky areas, matching author's description

### 2.2 Hiddenite, NC Emerald Mines

**Research Goal:** History, public access, connection to author's childhood

**Findings:**

**Emerald Hollow Mine:**
- **Only emerald mine in the world open to the public**
- Located in Hiddenite, NC (Brushy Mountains foothills)
- 70 acres for prospecting
- Open year-round, 7 days/week, 8:30 AM to sunset

**History:**
- Emeralds first found during plowing in 1874
- Town named for mineralogist **William Earl Hidden** (1879)
- Hidden was sent to NC by **Thomas Edison** looking for platinum
- Mike Watkins opened Emerald Hollow Mine to public in 1986
- Currently owned by Jason Martin and Dottie Watkins

**Notable Discoveries:**
| Year | Find | Value |
|------|------|-------|
| 1969 | Stephenson Emerald (1,438 carats) | Largest in North America at time |
| 2003 | Jamie Hill emerald (1,869 carats) | Over $1 million |
| - | **"Carolina Emerald"** | **Owned by Tiffany & Co.**, valued over $100,000 |

**Gems Available:** Emeralds, aquamarine, sapphire, garnet, amethyst, hiddenite (only found in NC)

**CRITICAL CONNECTION:** The **Carolina Emerald is owned by Tiffany & Co.** - and a Tiffany item (Favrile vase) is in the Lion's Share treasure. This creates a direct link between Hiddenite and the treasure items.

**NC State Facts:** Emerald is NC's official state precious stone (since 1973)

**Location Relevance:** STRONGLY SUPPORTS NC - Author's childhood gem-hunting location, Tiffany connection
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Sources:**
- [Emerald Hollow Mine](https://www.emeraldhollowmine.com/)
- [NC DNCR - Hiddenite](https://www.dncr.nc.gov/blog/2024/01/17/hiddenite-m-29)
- [Wikipedia - Hiddenite Gem Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiddenite_Gem_Mines)

### 4.2 Grand Central Station + Onassis

**Research Goal:** Her preservation work - does "full circle" theme apply?

**Findings:**

**The Preservation Fight (1975-1978):**
- Penn Central planned 55-story tower above Grand Central
- Pennsylvania Station demolished in 1963 - preservationists wanted to prevent repeat
- Jackie joined Municipal Art Society's "Committee to Save Grand Central Terminal"

**Jackie's Role:**
- Became public face of preservation campaign after 1975 court ruling
- Famous quote: "If we don't care about our past we can't have very much hope for our future"
- Organized "Landmark Express" train trip to DC for Supreme Court hearing
- June 26, 1978: Supreme Court ruled 6-3 in favor of NYC's Landmark Law (first preservation case)

**Legacy:**
- Main entry foyer on East 42nd Street renamed "Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis Foyer"
- MAS presents "Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis Medal" for preservation work
- Called "First Lady of Historic Preservation"

**"Full Circle" Theme:** The chapter mentions her bringing things "full circle" - she preserved the past for the future, which mirrors the treasure hunt's emphasis on history and legacy.

**Location Relevance:** NEUTRAL - NYC location, thematic but not geographic clue
**Confidence:** HIGH
**Sources:**
- [NYPAP - Jackie Kennedy Onassis](https://www.nypap.org/preservation-history/jacqueline-kennedy-onassis/)
