## TASK 1: RESEARCH OF SPECIFIC CANDIDATE LOCATIONS

### A. Garden of the Gods

**Overview:**
- 1,367-acre National Natural Landmark, owned by the City of Colorado Springs
- Donated by Charles Elliott Perkins' children in 1909 with stipulation it remain "free to the entire world"
- Elevation range: 6,210 ft (low) to 7,067 ft (high point on Rampart Range Road)
- Approximately 6 million visitors per year
- FREE admission, no fees, open to public year-round

**Trails:**
- **Central Garden Trail** — most visited, paved, ADA accessible
- **Palmer Trail** — runs nearly 2 miles along the western/northern side; winds through Gambel oak shrubland; less visited than Central Garden; offers views of formations from less-crowded angles
- **Scotsman/Buckskin Charlie Trail** — 1.9-mile moderate loop in southwestern portion of park; less popular, offers solitude; ascends through Gambel oak
- **Chambers/Bretag/Palmer Loop** — 3-mile route navigating the edge of the park; "fewer visitors choose this route, offering a more private and peaceful experience"
- **Siamese Twins Trail** — SW corner near Balanced Rock; "crooks and crannies for kids to explore"; views of Pikes Peak through rock window
- **Cabin Canyon Trail** — shares trailhead with Siamese Twins at parking lot P14
- **Niobrara Trail** — less crowded south-side option

**Tree Species Confirmed:**
- **Gambel oak (Quercus gambelii)** — dominant shrubland vegetation, especially along Palmer Trail and Scotsman Trail areas
- **Ponderosa pine** — present in park
- **Juniper** — present

**Rock Formations:** World-famous; hundreds of red sandstone formations including Balanced Rock, Cathedral Spires, Siamese Twins, Kissing Camels, Gateway Rocks, North/South Gateway. Many have nooks, crevices, backsides where visitors rarely go.

**Wildlife:**
- Black bears, mule deer, elk, bighorn sheep, coyotes, bobcats
- Rattlesnakes, bull snakes, garter snakes, fence lizards
- 170+ bird species

**Picnic Areas:**
- Scotsman Picnic Area: 11 tables, BBQ grills
- South Spring Canyon Picnic Area: 13 tables, BBQ grills
- First-come, first-served basis

**Stargazing:**
- Hosts regular "Star Party" events with Colorado Springs Astronomical Society
- "Starlight Spectacular" — annual nighttime cycling/walking event through the park (25+ year tradition, though cancelled in 2024)
- Park is "perfectly sheltered from city light pollution"
- Park closes at 10 PM

**Distance from Roads:** Roads run throughout the park. Every point in the park is within 1 mile of a road.

**Aspen Trees:** NOT confirmed within Garden of the Gods proper. Elevation (6,210-7,067 ft) is at the very lowest range for aspen (6,500+ ft). No specific reports of aspen in the park.

### C. Section 16 / Palmer Trail Area

**Overview:**
- Section 16 refers to a historic land ordinance parcel (originally 640 acres portioned for schools under Thomas Jefferson's presidency)
- **Permanently protected by Palmer Land Trust in 2010**
- Managed by City of Colorado Springs, Parks, Trails & Open Space
- FREE access, no parking fee at Section 16 trailhead
- The trail crosses multiple land jurisdictions: city parks, Pike National Forest, and Bear Creek Canyon Park

**Access:**
- Section 16 Trailhead on Gold Camp Road
- From US-24 (Cimarron), turn south on 26th Street, through two switchbacks, right onto Gold Camp Road, trailhead parking ~0.9 miles
- Connected to Manitou Springs via Intemann Trail (~7 miles one way)

**Trail System:**
- Palmer Trail/Section 16 Loop: 5.5-5.9 miles, 1,200-1,305 ft elevation gain, moderate difficulty
- Trail starts through **scrub (Gambel) oak**, then transitions into **ponderosa pine forest** as elevation increases
- Starting elevation ~6,300 ft, climbing to ~8,000 ft at ridge
- Counterclockwise provides spectacular Pikes Peak views

**Key Features:**
- **Finger Rock** — distinctive rock formation near Intemann Trail intersection, resembling "a closed hand with a raised index finger" (an "X" pointing the way?)
- **Picnic Rock** — flat sandstone rocks with vista, shaded by junipers and pines
- Red rock outcroppings, meadows, stream canyons with tiny waterfalls
- **Hunter Run Creek** crossing with bridge
- Views of Garden of the Gods and Red Rock Canyon from ridge

**Vegetation Confirmed:**
- **Gambel oak** — dominates first mile of trail
- **Ponderosa pine** — dominates above first mile
- **Wildflowers** — pasque flowers in spring, "bevy of colorful wildflowers" in summer
- **Butterflies** confirmed in spring

**Trail Intersections:**
- Section 16/Intemann Trail intersection at Finger Rock (~0.75-0.8 mi from Section 16 trailhead)
- Palmer Trail/Section 16 3-way intersection at summit ridge (~1.6 mi)
- Connector to Red Rock Canyon Open Space trails
- **Waterfall Spur** — 0.32 mi from Section 16/Intemann intersection
- **Meadow/Red Rock Link** — 0.65 mi from intersection

## TASK 4: IRISES

### Do Irises Grow in the Colorado Springs Area?

**YES.** *Iris missouriensis* (Rocky Mountain iris / Blue Flag) is native to Colorado and grows throughout the region:

- **Color:** Pale blue to lavender to white flowers with purple veins and faint yellow markings — matches "blue and pink irises" from the book
- **Elevation range:** Low valleys to 9,000-11,000 ft — the 6,000-7,000 ft Colorado Springs foothills are well within range
- **Habitat:** Wet meadows, dry steppes, open woodland — all present in the candidate areas
- **Bloom time:** Late spring to early summer
- **Colorado native status:** YES

The book says "blue and pink irises...perhaps you'll pass by similar flowers along your way." Rocky Mountain irises are blue-purple (matching "blue") and the book's mention of "pink" could refer to the pinker tones some flowers display, OR to other wildflowers (prickly pear cactus blooms pink in the area, as do various other wildflowers).

**Assessment:** Rocky Mountain irises ARE native to the Colorado Springs foothills and could plausibly be encountered along trails in the candidate areas, especially in meadow sections and near stream crossings. This clue is COMPATIBLE with the Colorado hypothesis.

## TASK 7: "WHERE FEW HAVE SEEN IT" CONSTRAINT

### The Problem
Garden of the Gods gets 6 million visitors/year. How can something be hidden "where few have seen it"?

### Resolution

**1. The treasure is likely NOT in Garden of the Gods proper, but in the adjacent, far less-visited areas.**

Red Rock Canyon Open Space and Section 16 are described as:
- "Not nearly as visited" as Garden of the Gods
- "Fewer crowds"
- "More private and peaceful"
- Section 16 is a conservation area with moderate hiking traffic
- Contemplative Trail in Red Rock Canyon is specifically designed for solitude

**2. Even within Garden of the Gods, the south/west trails see dramatically fewer visitors.**
- Palmer Trail: "not the most popular"
- Scotsman Trail: "less foot traffic"
- Chambers/Bretag: "fewer visitors choose this route"
- The vast majority of 6M visitors stay on the Central Garden paved trails and drive-through roads

**3. Off-trail nooks and crevices.**
- Rock formations have backsides, crevices, and hidden alcoves
- Most visitors stick to established trails and viewpoints
- The poem says "where few have seen it" — not "where no one goes." A spot 50 feet off a moderate trail, behind a rock formation, could be visited by millions of people yet seen by very few.

**4. The poem's own framing supports this.**
- "And if you spy **others** warming with the sunlight, or sat upon a rock" — OTHER PEOPLE ARE NEARBY
- The treasure is in a place where people are around (sitting on rocks, enjoying nature) but the specific spot has been seen by few
- This is consistent with a hidden nook near a popular-ish trail in a less-visited area

### Assessment
**Section 16/Intemann Trail area** or **Red Rock Canyon Open Space** best satisfies this constraint. Both have moderate visitation (enough for "others" to be around) but far less than Garden of the Gods, with numerous rock formations and off-trail spots that most hikers walk past without exploring.
