# NC Foothills Hypothesis: Comprehensive Investigation

## Purpose
Thorough investigation of the NC foothills (Statesville / Hiddenite / Alexander County) as a BACKUP hypothesis to Colorado Springs for the Lion's Share treasure location.

---

## THE 4 "FATAL FLAWS" — CAN THEY BE RESOLVED?

### FLAW 1: "PIKE ARE RATHER ALL AROUND"

**Research Findings:**

1. **No "Pike" place names in Alexander, Iredell, Burke, Catawba, or Caldwell counties.** Exhaustive search found NO Pike Road, Pike Creek, Pike Mountain, or any other pike-named feature in the immediate area around Hiddenite/Statesville.

2. **Pikeville, NC exists but is in Wayne County** (eastern NC, ~200 miles away). Named after Nathan Pike who ran a tavern. No connection to the foothills region.

3. **Pike Nurseries** exists on Statesville Road in Cornelius (~30 miles south). Modern business, not relevant.

4. **Turnpike history in NC is minimal.** NC had a brief turnpike era (early 1800s) but major turnpikes were in western mountains (Buncombe Turnpike) and Piedmont (Salisbury-Lincolnton-Rutherfordton). No notable turnpike documented through Alexander or northern Iredell County.

5. **Pike fish (Esocidae) in the Catawba basin:** North Carolina has 3 pike family species:
   - **Redfin Pickerel** — records from "the western Piedmont in the Catawba basin"
   - **Chain Pickerel** — native east of the Mountains, present in NC waters
   - **Muskellunge** — Tennessee drainage basins

   Pickerel are present in the Catawba River system (which includes Lake Hickory bordering Alexander County), but they are NOT "rather all around" — they are an uncommon species in these waters. The dominant fish in Lake Hickory are largemouth bass, striped bass, bluegill, crappie, and catfish.

6. **"Pike" as peaked hills (English dialect):** The Brushy Mountains ARE a chain of peaked knobs — Pores Knob (2,680 ft), Hickory Knob (2,560 ft), Fox Mountain, Click Mountain, etc. Rocky Face Mountain itself is a dome-shaped granite peak rising 600 ft above surroundings. The South Mountains have Buzzard Roost (2,980 ft), Benn Knob (2,894 ft), Chestnut Knob, Sawtooth — all peaked. However, the term "pike" for peaked hill is a Northern England/Lake District dialect term — it is NOT used in Appalachian or NC dialect. There is no evidence this term was ever used locally for these features.

**VERDICT: NOT RESOLVED.** The NC foothills have peaked knobs but nobody calls them "pikes." There are no Pike place names nearby. Pickerel exist but are uncommon. The Colorado interpretation (Pikes Peak, Pike National Forest, Pike-everything) remains overwhelmingly stronger.

**Strength: 2/10 for NC vs 10/10 for Colorado.**

### FLAW 2: "MAGIC IN THE WATER"

**Research Findings:**

1. **Davis White Sulphur Springs (Hiddenite):** A fashionable health resort operated ~1905-1925 on Sulphur Springs Road in Hiddenite. Sulfur-saturated water believed to have healing/health properties. "Well-to-do people" arrived by train from out of state. The resort burned; nothing remains today except the road name.

2. **All Healing Springs (Taylorsville):** Another mineral springs resort in nearby Taylorsville, also ~turn of century. Burned (possibly 1925). Named "All Healing" — literally claiming magical healing properties. Road name (All Healing Springs Rd) survives. Springs existed "in some form as late as the 1980s."

3. **1908 North Carolina mining publication** documented the mineral content of both springs.

4. **Emerald Hollow Mine (Hiddenite):** The only emerald mine in the world open to the public. Visitors sluice through creeks, washing dirt in screen-bottomed boxes. "Quartz and mica veins indicate that emeralds may lie hidden in the dirt." Mica flakes in the water create golden sparkle. Every rain washes fresh minerals into the streams. The experience of finding gems in water could easily be described as "magical."

5. **Lake Hickory:** Borders Alexander County. Public boat ramps and fishing access at Wittenburg and Dusty Ridge. Standard reservoir, no "magical" water reputation.

6. **Catawba River:** Major river corridor. No special mineral springs reputation.

**VERDICT: PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** The Hiddenite area had TWO mineral springs resorts with healing/magical water reputations — Davis White Sulphur Springs and All Healing Springs. The names survive in road names. Plus, the Emerald Hollow Mine offers a literal "magic in the water" experience (gems appearing from creek washing). However, neither springs facility exists anymore, which weakens the argument — the poem uses present tense ("there may be magic in the water"). The Manitou Springs interpretation for Colorado is stronger because those springs are still actively flowing, still have mineral content, and the name "Manitou" literally means "Great Spirit" (supernatural/magical).

**Strength: 5/10 for NC vs 9/10 for Colorado.**

### FLAW 3: "SHIMMERING CIRCLES OF GOLD"

**Research Findings:**

1. **Quaking Aspen: NOT present in the NC foothills.** Aspen grows only in high-elevation NC mountains (zone 6 or higher) — isolated populations at elevations far above the Brushy Mountains or South Mountains. The foothills at 1,800-3,000 ft are too low and too warm. This eliminates the strongest Colorado interpretation (aspen groves = shimmering circles of gold).

2. **Tulip Poplars (Yellow Poplars):** Abundant in NC foothills. Turn "golden yellow" in fall. NC Extension says they show "superb fall color of yellow to golden yellow." One description: "a few of her leaves will shimmer, golden in the sun." Found "throughout North Carolina" especially "in deep, moist soils along streams and lower mountain coves." BUT — tulip poplar leaves are NOT round/circular. They are distinctively four-lobed and pointed.

3. **Shagbark Hickory:** "Wonderful texture as it matures, offers great golden color in the fall." Common in NC foothills. Hickory leaves are compound (elongated leaflets), not circular.

4. **Mica in streams:** HIGHLY RELEVANT. Mica is abundant in Alexander County geology. "Quartz and mica veins" are characteristic of Hiddenite-area geology. When sunlight hits mica flakes in stream water, it creates "shimmering, golden" flashes. "Pyrite and yellow-colored mica are frequently mistaken for gold." Mica flakes are flat and roughly circular when they float on water. In the context of Emerald Hollow Mine: "miners wash handfuls of dirt in screen-bottomed boxes hoping that among the remaining flecks of mica and quartz they'll find an emerald." Mica's specific gravity makes it float and catch light — creating what could genuinely be described as "shimmering circles of gold" on water surfaces.

5. **Gold coins from the Charlotte Mint:** William Julius Alexander (Alexander County namesake) was superintendent of the Charlotte Mint (1846-1851), which produced gold coins — literal shimmering circles of gold. He directed the striking of the first gold dollars. This is an intellectually interesting connection but feels too abstract/historical for a physical location clue.

6. **NC Gold Belt:** Alexander County is adjacent to the Carolina gold-bearing regions. Gold panning is possible in the Catawba River and South Mountain streams. Burke County is noted as "one of the richest counties for panning along the Catawba River."

**VERDICT: PARTIALLY RESOLVED.** The mica-in-streams interpretation is the strongest NC candidate — geologically accurate for the area, visually matches "shimmering circles of gold," and directly ties to the Hiddenite gem-mining heritage. However, it requires the searcher to be looking at a specific stream at the right angle in sunlight. The Colorado aspen interpretation (entire mountainsides of shimmering golden circles visible from miles away) is more visually dramatic and obvious.

**Strength: 4/10 for NC vs 9/10 for Colorado.**

### FLAW 4: "WILL'S STRAIGHT EDGE"

**Research Findings:**

1. **William Julius Alexander (1797-1857):** Alexander County namesake. Speaker of the NC House of Commons. Superintendent of the Charlotte Mint (1846-1851). Lawyer, politician. NO connection to surveying, straight lines, or "straight edges" found. He was a legal/political figure, not an engineer or surveyor.

2. **William Sharpe (1742-1818):** Lawyer, surveyor, land speculator, and militia officer in Rowan County (later Iredell County). He created a famous 1773 map of the Fourth Creek Congregation in the Statesville/Iredell area using "his surveying skills." He divided territory into quadrants showing resident locations in an eleven-mile radius. THIS IS A SURVEYOR who used straight lines and measurements to draw maps of the exact area where JCB grew up. However, connecting "will" (lowercase in the poem) to "William Sharpe" is a stretch — the poem says "will's" not "William's" or "Sharpe's."

3. **"Will" as common noun:** "Will" can mean determination, intention, resolve. "Will's straight edge" could mean "determination's straight path" — i.e., stay on course, don't deviate. This is a motivational reading, not a geographic one.

4. **"Straight edge" as geometry tool:** The poem follows "will's straight edge" with "the turning square or any arc may align at a proper point" — classic compass-and-straightedge geometry language. In Euclidean construction, a straightedge draws lines and a compass draws arcs/circles. "Turning square" could be a set square (drafting tool) or a right angle. This may be map navigation instructions rather than a location clue.

5. **William Jackson Palmer (Colorado):** Founded Colorado Springs. Railroad builder — railroads are literally straight edges across landscapes. Palmer Trail, Palmer Park, Palmer Land Trust all bear his name in Colorado Springs. The "conductor...gives direction" line supports Palmer as railroad conductor.

**VERDICT: NOT RESOLVED.** William Sharpe the surveyor is the best NC candidate, but the connection is tenuous (lowercase "will," no "straight edge" directly associated with Sharpe, requires knowledge of an obscure 1773 map). The Colorado interpretation (William Jackson Palmer, railroad founder, city builder) is vastly more natural and well-known.

**Strength: 2/10 for NC vs 8/10 for Colorado.**

## ADDITIONAL NC LOCATION RESEARCH

### Rocky Face Mountain Recreation Area

**Location:** 3451 Rocky Face Church Rd, Hiddenite, NC 28636. Northern Alexander County. Considered the southern edge of the Brushy Mountains. ~5 miles north of Taylorsville.

**Physical Features:**
- 318-acre county recreation area (public land)
- Dome-shaped granite mass (granitic gneiss), 1,800 ft elevation
- Rises ~600 ft above surrounding terrain
- Extensive exposed rock formations (exfoliation/bornhardt)
- Wetland area at old quarry site with boardwalks
- Rocky Face Branch stream (two tributaries)

**Trees/Vegetation:**
- Dense hardwood forests
- Table Mountain Pine (Pinus pungens)
- Red Cedar-Hardwood Woodland
- Piedmont Monadnock Forest community
- Magnolia, Sumac, Rhododendron, Beautyberry
- NO confirmed oaks specifically listed (though likely present in hardwood forest)
- Rare species: Keever's Onion, Keever's bristle moss, Appalachian Sandwort

**Trails (6 miles total):**
- Buzzard Loop (0.8 mi, purple blaze)
- Walking Track (0.3 mi, black)
- Hollow Rock (2.5 mi round trip, red blaze)
- Grindstone (1 mi, green blaze)
- Prison Camp (1 mi, orange blaze)
- Vertical Mile Challenge (2.2 mi loop, blue blaze)

**Amenities:** Picnic shelter, restrooms, 58 rock climbing routes, camping, outdoor classroom, adventure playground, wildlife observation decks, amphitheater

**Poem Match Assessment:**
- "Sat upon a rock" — STRONG (granite dome with extensive rock outcrops)
- "Shady oaks" — UNCERTAIN (hardwood forest present, oaks not confirmed)
- "Nature's wide embrace" — MODERATE (318-acre natural area)
- "Where few have seen it" — MODERATE (local park, not heavily touristed)
- "Magic in the water" — WEAK (small stream only)
- "Pike are rather all around" — WEAK (dome shape, not peaked hills)
- "Pine" (back cover) — PRESENT (Table Mountain Pine)
- "Grit" (back cover) — STRONG (granite)
- "Don't go down / how high" — MODERATE (1,800 ft, climbable summit)

## THE ALEXANDER RAILROAD — A CONDUCTOR CONNECTION

A significant discovery: The **Alexander Railroad** runs 18 miles from Statesville to Taylorsville, serving both Iredell and Alexander Counties. Originally built in 1887 as the Statesville & Western.

- Nicknamed "The June Bug Line" — after Romulus Linney's remark about Hiddenite emeralds being so valuable "a well-wintered junebug could fly away with enough of the mineral tied to its hind leg to pay for the whole railroad"
- Currently operates freight service
- Has conductors who literally "give direction" between Statesville and Taylorsville

The poem says: "Our conductor, as with a trace of a baton through the air, gives direction."

This could describe the Alexander Railroad conductor directing the train from Statesville toward Taylorsville/Hiddenite. However:
- The railroad serves freight, not passengers
- "Trace of a baton through the air" suggests an orchestral conductor, not a railroad one
- The Colorado interpretation (William Jackson Palmer as railroad conductor/city founder) is more historically prominent

**Assessment: INTERESTING but WEAKER than Colorado.** The Alexander Railroad exists and has a conductor, but the Palmer/Colorado Springs connection is more compelling.

## THE WILLIAM JULIUS ALEXANDER — CHARLOTTE MINT THREAD

An intriguing connection: William Julius Alexander, the man Alexander County is named after, was superintendent of the Charlotte Mint (1846-1851). The Charlotte Mint struck gold coins — literal "shimmering circles of gold."

This creates a potential triple connection:
- "Will's straight edge" = William Julius Alexander's work at the Mint (coin dies use straight edges in production?)
- "Shimmering circles of gold" = the gold coins produced under his supervision
- "Alexander" = the county name, the chapter title ("Antiquities of Alexander"), and the historical figure

However, this interpretation requires:
- Knowing that Alexander County's namesake ran the Charlotte Mint
- Interpreting "straight edge" as a minting tool (coin dies are stamped, not cut with straight edges)
- A very abstract reading of "circles of gold" as coins rather than a physical feature at the location

**Assessment: INTELLECTUALLY CLEVER but IMPRACTICAL.** This feels like the kind of deep-cut historical connection the author might appreciate, but the poem describes a physical location you visit. "Circles of gold" should describe something you SEE at the site.

## FINAL ASSESSMENT

### Can the 4 Fatal Flaws Be Resolved?

1. **"Pike are rather all around"** — **NO.** No credible NC interpretation comes close to matching the Pikes Peak/Pike NF cluster in Colorado. The Brushy Mountain knobs are peaked hills, but nobody calls them pikes. Pickerel are present but scarce.

2. **"Magic in the water"** — **PARTIALLY.** The historical sulfur springs at Hiddenite and "All Healing Springs" at Taylorsville provide a legitimate connection, and the Emerald Hollow Mine creek panning adds a "magical" water experience. But the springs are gone and the mine is a tourist attraction, not a natural feature near a public park.

3. **"Shimmering circles of gold"** — **PARTIALLY.** Mica in streams is a legitimate interpretation for the geology of the area. However, it requires being at a specific spot at the right time, whereas Colorado's aspen groves are unmissable.

4. **"Will's straight edge"** — **NO.** No strong NC interpretation exists. William Sharpe the surveyor is tenuous. William Julius Alexander had no "straight edge" connection.

### The Bottom Line

**Colorado Springs remains the clear winner for the poem-based clues.** The NC hypothesis resolves 0 of 4 fatal flaws completely and only partially resolves 2 of 4. Meanwhile, Colorado resolves ALL major poem clues convincingly.

NC's strengths are:
- The deepest possible author connection (born, raised, formative memories)
- Confirmed dwarf crested irises matching the "flowers along your way" clue
- Excellent rock formations and oak/pine forests
- "Nature's wide embrace" at South Mountains State Park

NC's fatal weaknesses are:
- No "pike" connection
- No strong "magic water" that still exists
- No natural "circles of gold" phenomenon matching aspen groves
- No "Will" figure with a "straight edge"

### Confidence Level

**Colorado Springs: 75% confidence** as the correct location
**NC Foothills: 10% confidence** as the correct location
**Somewhere else entirely: 15% confidence**

The NC hypothesis would only be correct if:
- "Pike" means something we haven't discovered
- The author deliberately used misleading geographic-sounding clues that actually mean something else
- The emotional/personal connection outweighs the geographic specificity of the poem

None of these seem likely given the author's stated approach of "no complex cipher or grand secret code" and "subtly placed" but real clues.

**RECOMMENDATION: Maintain Colorado Springs as the primary hypothesis. NC remains a worthy backup only if Colorado field searches are exhaustively eliminated.**
