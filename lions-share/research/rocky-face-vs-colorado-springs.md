# Head-to-Head: Rocky Face Mountain vs. Colorado Springs

## Rocky Face Mountain, Alexander County, NC
- 318 acres, county park, free admission
- Granite dome (bornhardt), 1,800 ft summit, 600 ft above surroundings
- IN Hiddenite, NC — 4 miles from Emerald Hollow Mine, ~20 miles from author's childhood home
- Opened as park 2012; prior to that an abandoned quarry/dump
- ~200,000 visitors/year

## Section 16 / Intemann Trail, Colorado Springs, CO
- Palmer Land Trust + City + Pike NF, free access
- Red sandstone, Gambel oak, ponderosa pine, 6,300-8,000 ft
- No stated author connection; author searched for Fenn treasure in Rockies
- Gold Camp Road access follows historic railroad

## THE CASE FOR ROCKY FACE MOUNTAIN

**What it gets right:**
1. **The back cover poem maps perfectly to Hiddenite geology.** "Solid" = granite. "Grit" = quarry grit. "Sparkle" = Hiddenite minerals. "Pine" = confirmed pines. This isn't a coincidence — every word in the back cover poem has a natural, unstrained connection to this specific landscape.

2. **"Sat upon a rock" is as strong as it gets.** The mountain IS a rock. Named "Rocky Face." Granite dome with flat sitting surfaces at the summit. Picnic tables on bare rock.

3. **Author connection is the strongest possible.** The treasure box maker (Seth Gould) is in nearby Bakersville. The author grew up exploring this area. The book is saturated with NC references. The faience necklace ("most personal item") was assembled by the author and his wife.

4. **The Alexander Railroad is a real "conductor."** A literal railroad with conductors runs from Statesville through Taylorsville toward Hiddenite. A quarry spur once reached Rocky Face itself.

5. **Oaks are dominant.** Chestnut oak (largest acorns in eastern US) dominates the Piedmont Monadnock Forest community at Rocky Face.

6. **Accessibility is perfect.** Leisurely afternoon outing, picnic facilities, family-friendly, 200K visitors/year, free county park.

7. **Endemic botanical species.** Keever's Onion grows ONLY in the Brushy Mountains of Alexander and Wilkes counties. This is a botanically significant Natural Heritage Area. The author's advice to "go research it further" could lead here.

**What it struggles with:**
1. **"Pike"** remains the biggest challenge. The Middle English etymology is valid, and the Brushy Mountain knobs ARE visible from the summit, but they're typically described as "rounded knobs" not pointed pikes. The argument requires the searcher to learn an archaic meaning AND apply it to modestly peaked hills.

2. **"Magic in the water"** is weak at the mountain itself. The historical mineral springs are gone. The Emerald Hollow Mine is 4 miles away. You have to extend the clue area beyond the mountain.

3. **"Shimmering circles of gold"** has no obvious match. No aspen. Mica-in-streams requires finding the right stream at the right angle. Gold coins from the Charlotte Mint is too abstract.

4. **"Will's straight edge"** has no strong candidate. William Sharpe the surveyor is obscure.

5. **The park opened in 2012.** Before that it was a quarry/dump. The author didn't explore Rocky Face as a child — he explored the Emerald Hollow Mine and the broader countryside.

## THE CASE FOR COLORADO SPRINGS

**What it gets right:**
1. **"Pike" is perfect.** Pikes Peak, Pike National Forest, Pike everywhere. "Rather all around" is literally true.
2. **"Magic in the water" is perfect.** Manitou Springs = sacred healing waters, "breath of the Great Spirit."
3. **"Shimmering circles of gold" is perfect.** Quaking aspen confirmed in the area.
4. **"Will's straight edge" is strong.** William Jackson Palmer, city founder, railroad builder.
5. **Gold Camp Road is multilayered.** Gold + railroad + Palmer + path to trailhead.
6. **Lion Trail exists.** In Red Rock Canyon, echoing "The Lion's Share."
7. **14/18 poem clues match at STRONG+.**

**What it struggles with:**
1. **No stated author connection.** This is the single biggest problem. Why would the author hide his most personal treasure somewhere he has no stated connection to?
2. **The Fenn box may be in Colorado too.** 25% chance of conflict.
3. **No treasure items connect to Colorado.**
4. **Irises are weaker.** Rocky Mountain iris vs. NC's native dwarf crested iris.
5. **The back cover poem maps less naturally.** "Grit" as sandstone works, but "sparkle" doesn't have a specific local connection the way Hiddenite does.

## UNRESOLVED QUESTIONS

1. **Can "pike" work for the Brushy Mountains?** If you accept the Middle English definition, and the Brushy Mountain knobs/summits are visible "rather all around" from Rocky Face, this could work — but it's the weakest of the four challenged clues.

2. **Is "magic in the water" about the AREA or the MOUNTAIN?** If the clue describes the broader Hiddenite area (including Emerald Hollow Mine's gem-bearing streams), it works better. If it must be at the exact hiding spot, Rocky Face's small streams don't qualify.

3. **Could "shimmering circles of gold" describe mica?** In the right light, mica flakes in water DO create golden shimmer. But they're more "specks" than "circles," and the effect requires specific conditions.

4. **Is the author's personal connection more important than poem specificity?** The prime directive says "where the author would choose to hide his most important, most personal treasure." NC wins on personal connection. But the poem is the primary clue vehicle.

## REVISED CONFIDENCE (Updated After First-Hand Observations)

Three critical corrections from someone who has visited Rocky Face summit:

### 1. PIKE — RESOLVED
Pointed peaks (pikes) ARE visible in every direction from the summit. First-hand observation confirms this. "Pike are rather all around" describes the view from Rocky Face Mountain. The Middle English etymology (pike = pointed/peaked hill) is the correct reading. **Upgraded from 2/10 to 7/10.**

### 2. WILL — RESOLVED: WILLIAM EARL HIDDEN
The most obvious candidate was overlooked: **William Earl Hidden** (1853-1918), the mineralogist who discovered hiddenite, the namesake of the town where Rocky Face sits. Sent by Thomas Edison in 1879, he prospected this exact area. His surname is literally "HIDDEN" — the thematic parallel to hidden treasure is unmistakable. The author's story of discovering gems near Hiddenite mirrors Hidden's own discovery. "Will's straight edge" = William Hidden's prospecting/surveying tools, or the straight mineral veins he followed, or the direct path he blazed to discovery. **Upgraded from 2/10 to 7/10.**

### 3. CAMPING ON SUMMIT — STARLIGHT CONFIRMED
You can camp overnight on Rocky Face's summit. "And if you stay to see the night / it will ignite with the flame of starlight" is not hypothetical — campers on this granite dome in rural NC see the Milky Way, exactly as the author did growing up nearby. **Upgraded from 5/10 to 7/10.**

### Remaining Challenge: "Shimmering Circles of Gold"
Still the weakest NC clue. No aspen trees. Best interpretations:
- Mica flakes in gem-bearing streams creating golden shimmer
- Sunlight on water
- Connection to Carolina Gold Belt / Charlotte Mint gold coins
**Still 3-4/10.**

### Remaining Challenge: "Magic in the Water"
Stronger than initially assessed when considering the AREA not just the mountain:
- Emerald Hollow Mine (4 mi): finding gems in water IS magic — this is the author's origin story
- Historical mineral springs (Davis White Sulphur, All Healing) — gone but once present
- Gem-bearing creeks throughout the Hiddenite area
**Upgraded from 3/10 to 5/10.**

### Updated Confidence (Revision 2 — After Site-Specific Research)

Additional findings from park website and hiker reports:

**CORAL HAIRSTREAK BUTTERFLY documented at Rocky Face** (park Animal Life page). Same Lycaenidae family as Colorado Hairstreak. Hairstreak butterflies perform the same darting, territorial "dancing" flights. "Dancers are on the land" now works for Rocky Face. **Upgraded from 4/10 to 6/10.**

**SUMMIT VIEWS confirm peaks in all directions** (MyHikes.org trail report):
- NW: Pores Knob (2,660 ft, highest Brushy Mountain peak)
- NE: Range continuing toward Wilkesboro
- W: Grandfather Mountain on clear days
- SW: Brushy Mountains toward Lenoir; Black Mountains in distance
"Pike are rather all around" = surrounded by peaked mountains. **Confirmed at 7/10.**

**SHIMMERING CIRCLES OF GOLD — RESOLVED via water pools on mica-bearing granite.** Rain/runoff collects in circular depressions on the exposed granite dome. The granitic gneiss contains mica flakes. Sunlight on these pools creates shimmering golden circles. The poem says "or simply FOLLOW shimmering circles of gold" — you follow the pools across the granite surface as you hike. This is a direct physical observation, not abstract. You can't "follow" aspen trees the same way. **Upgraded from 3/10 to 7/10.**

**BOTANICAL SIGNIFICANCE confirmed** (UNC NC Botanical Garden article):
- Table Mountain Pine confirmed
- Keever's Onion: endemic to Brushy Mountains ONLY (Alexander + Wilkes Counties)
- Keever's bristle moss: state endangered, holotype from Rocky Face
- 215 of 821 Alexander County herbarium specimens from Rocky Face area
- NC Natural Heritage Area designation
