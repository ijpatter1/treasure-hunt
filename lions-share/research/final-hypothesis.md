**Confidence: HIGH (75%)**

The Lion's Share treasure is most likely hidden in the **Section 16 / Intemann Trail area** of Colorado Springs, Colorado, accessed from the **Section 16 trailhead on Gold Camp Road**. ## How Joy's Serenade Maps to the Search

Reading the poem as a journey to the treasure:

1. **Stanza 1-2** (Setup): The poem/book provides the map. "Our conductor gives direction" = Palmer/Gold Camp Road shows the way.

2. **Stanza 3** (Navigation): "Use will's straight edge" = follow Palmer's railroad grade (Gold Camp Road). Use geometric tools on a map. "This is no imaginary wonderland" = this is a real place. "Now look."

3. **Stanza 4** (The Area): "An X leads the way" = trail marker or intersection. "Shimmering circles of gold" = aspen trees or Gold Camp Road. "Magic in the water, but the pike are rather all around" = you're near Manitou Springs' magic water, surrounded by Pike geography.

4. **Stanza 5** (Getting Close): "If you get warm, you may find shady oaks" = Gambel oak indicates proximity. "The haiku curls a little further on where few have seen it" = the back cover poem gives the final clue; the treasure is slightly further than you think, in a secluded spot. "Don't go down" = stay elevated.

5. **Stanza 6** (The Setting): "Dancers are on the land" = Colorado Hairstreak butterflies in the oaks. "Flame of starlight" = the night sky at elevation.

6. **Stanza 7** (At the Treasure): "Sat upon a rock, allowing nature's wide embrace" = the destination has rocks to sit on with panoramic nature views. "Others warming with the sunlight" = other hikers are nearby. "Ask them to assist you" = you may need help finding/retrieving it.

## Weaknesses and Risks

1. **No stated personal author connection to Colorado** — The author's biography centers on NC and CA. However, his Fenn search experience confirms familiarity with the Rockies, and the poem describes the LOCATION not the author's life.

2. **Fenn box may also be in Colorado** — If the Forrest Fenn tribute box is in CO (it's in one of MT/WY/CO/NM), the Lion's Share cannot be here. 75% chance it's NOT in CO.

3. **The geometric stanza remains undecoded** — "Use will's straight edge, as the turning square or any arc may align at a proper point" may encode specific map navigation instructions that could pinpoint the exact spot.

4. **"All that is left remains for you"** — The directional "left" meaning is unresolved.

5. **The iris clue slightly favors NC** — Dwarf crested iris (Iris cristata) is native to NC woodland slopes, while Rocky Mountain iris (Iris missouriensis) is native to CO but not confirmed at these specific elevations. However, this single clue doesn't override the 7 CO-specific matches.
