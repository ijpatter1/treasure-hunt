# Search Plan: Rocky Face Mountain, Hiddenite NC

## Target Area
Hidden exposed granite surface between trail markers 321 and 322, accessed by leaving the Hollow Rock Trail and descending below the trail.

## Route
1. Park at Rocky Face Mountain Recreation Area (3451 Rocky Face Church Road, Hiddenite, NC 28636)
2. Take Hollow Rock Trail (red blazes) toward summit
3. Pass summit marker 319, continue to picnic area at 320
4. Follow the sharp curve ("the haiku curls") between 320 and 321
5. Continue past 321 toward 322
6. Leave the trail and descend to the hidden granite surface below

## What to Look For

### The Treasure Box
- The Lion's Share box is yellow brass sides, steel top edge, textured iron center locking panel (Ch 3)
- "Substantially larger" than the four smaller boxes
- Not buried — sitting on or concealed at ground level
- Would be covered by snow in winter — ground-level placement
- May be in a granite crevice, under an exfoliation shelf, or wedged in a rock gap

### Physical Environment at the Exact Spot (from back cover poem)
- **Solid granite** surface with **grit** texture
- **Mica sparkling** in the rock ("sparkle")
- **Pine trees** nearby or at the edge of the granite ("as you pine")
- Look specifically for the **transition zone** where exposed granite meets pine

### From Joy's Serenade
- "Sat upon a rock" — large enough to sit on
- "Nature's wide embrace" — panoramic natural setting
- "Where few have seen it" — off-trail, hidden from casual view
- "Others warming with the sunlight" — the main trail with other hikers is visible/nearby above

## Search Strategy

### Systematic Grid
- Map the hidden granite surface mentally into sections
- Search each section thoroughly: every crevice, every exfoliation gap, every rock shelf
- Check underneath overhanging granite sheets
- Look in gaps where granite meets the tree line
- Check behind any boulders or rock piles at the edges

### Key Poem Reminders
- "The haiku curls a little further on" — if you think you've covered the area, push a little further. The treasure is always "one step beyond" where you expect
- "All that is left remains for you" — consider going LEFT when you have a choice
- "Maybe ask them to assist you" — bring a friend, the box may be heavy or awkward to retrieve

## What to Bring
- Sturdy hiking boots (granite can be slippery)
- Water and snacks (leisurely afternoon outing)
- Phone with GPS (know your location — postscript advice)
- A friend (author recommends searching with a loved one; also useful for carrying the box)
- Bright clothing (hunting seasons — postscript advice)
- Copy of the back cover poem and Joy's Serenade
- Flashlight (for peering into dark crevices)
- Camera (document your adventure — Ch 17 advice)

## Timing
- **Best season**: Late spring / early summer (May-June)
  - No snow on ground
  - Dwarf crested irises blooming (March-May) — "flowers along your way"
  - Coral Hairstreak butterflies active — "Dancers on the land"
  - Comfortable hiking temperatures
  - Long daylight hours
- **Avoid**: Winter (snow covers ground-level items), extreme heat (exposed granite gets dangerously hot)
- **Park hours**: April-Sept 8am-8pm; check current hours

## Safety
- Watch for rattlesnakes and copperheads (confirmed at Rocky Face)
- Exposed granite is very slippery when wet — avoid after rain
- Stay aware of your footing when leaving the trail and descending
- Tell someone where you're going and when you expect to return
- Bring phone, stay charged

## If You Find It
- The box requires 7 actions to open (Ch 3) — instructions are inside
- Document everything before moving anything
- The box contains items worth millions — secure transportation planned
- Contact information should be in the book's postscript or website
