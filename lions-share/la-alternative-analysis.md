# Alternative Location Analysis: Steelmanning Los Angeles

## Executive Summary

This document presents the strongest possible case for the **Los Angeles area** as the Lion's Share treasure location. The core premise: **It makes practical sense that JCB would place the largest treasure close to where he lives.**

**Key Finding:** While the main book text evidence still leans toward North Carolina, the LA hypothesis has underappreciated strengths. JCB explicitly lives in LA, explicitly mentions a "massive rose garden" at a "local park" that matches Exposition Park (7 acres), and has Mount Wilson "not far from my home." These are **specific, identifiable locations** worthy of investigation.

## The Case FOR Los Angeles

### Argument 1: Practical Proximity

**The strongest argument for LA:**

JCB has lived in California for **over 20 years**. He currently lives in the LA area (Mount Wilson is "not far...to the northeast"). For the **largest treasure box** ($100k+ in value):

- **Monitoring**: He can visit the location regularly to ensure it's undisturbed
- **Maintenance**: If the box is compromised, he can respond quickly
- **Emotional connection**: His adult life, family, and success are built in LA
- **Security**: Harder to monitor a treasure 2,500 miles away in NC

### Argument 2: The Rose Garden Clue (Chapter 20)

JCB explicitly writes (p.177-178):
> "walked through a **massive rose garden**...covering **several acres**...at a **local park** with my wife Kimberly"

This describes a **specific, identifiable location**:

**Exposition Park Rose Garden** matches exactly:
- **Size**: 7 acres (matches "several acres")
- **Scale**: ~15,793 rose bushes, 200+ varieties (matches "massive")
- **Public**: Free to enter (matches "local park")
- **Location**: Los Angeles (matches "local" to JCB)
- **Historic**: National Register of Historic Places (1991)

**No other LA rose garden fits this description.** The Huntington Gardens has only 3 acres. Descanso Gardens is smaller.

### Argument 3: Mount Wilson Observatory Reference (Chapter 18)

JCB writes (p.164):
> "One of the hundreds of science projects Andrew Carnegie funded was the **Mount Wilson Observatory**. It is located **not far from my home, just to the northeast**."

This pinpoints JCB's home:
- Mount Wilson is in the San Gabriel Mountains
- "To the northeast" means JCB lives **west or southwest** of Mount Wilson
- This places him in: Pasadena, Glendale, Los Feliz, Hollywood Hills, or central LA

**Why include this geographic detail if not significant?**

### Argument 4: California Gold Rush Nugget (Chapter 12)

The treasure includes a **4+ ounce California Gold Rush nugget** found near Sutter's Mill.

- This is the ONLY treasure item explicitly from California
- JCB emphasizes his 20-year California residence in the same chapter
- Could this be a location signifier like the Tiffany-Hiddenite connection?

### Argument 5: "Treasure Island" Name (Chapter 8)

Charles Loloma painted murals on **"Treasure Island"** in San Francisco Bay. JCB specifically notes Alcatraz is "less than three miles" away.

While potentially coincidental, the "Treasure Island" name appearing in a treasure hunt book deserves consideration.

### Argument 6: Joy's Serenade Could Apply to LA

| Clue | LA Interpretation |
|------|-------------------|
| "Solid, have grit" | Quartz diorite bedrock at Mount Wilson |
| "Sparkle" | Granite/quartz reflection in San Gabriel Mountains |
| "Pine" | Pine trees in Angeles National Forest |
| "Shady oaks" | Coast Live Oaks throughout Griffith Park |
| "Dancers on the land" | Western Tiger Swallowtails documented in Griffith Park |
| "Sat upon a rock" | "Echo Rock" viewpoint near Mount Wilson |

### Argument 7: Children's Book Publisher Location

"Our Unbreakable Thread" publisher: **Marble Press LLC, Palo Alto, CA**

While publishers are often chosen for business reasons, a California publisher for a book with "extra clues" is notable.

## The Critical Weaknesses of LA Hypothesis

Despite strong practical arguments, LA faces significant obstacles:

### 1. NO 318 Encoding

The haiku math (3 lines + 18 syllables = 318) is the **most compelling single clue** in the book:
- Rocky Face Mountain Marker 318 exists
- Rocky Face Mountain is exactly 318 acres
- No equivalent found in any LA location

This triple confirmation is extremely difficult to dismiss.

### 2. NO "Alexander" Connection

Chapter 21 "Antiquities of **Alexander**" is explicitly stated to be a treasure clue:
> "A chapter titled 'Make It Make Sense' might also be a reference to figuring out the solution for the location of the treasure"

Alexander County, NC contains both Hiddenite and Rocky Face Mountain. There is no Alexander equivalent in LA.

### 3. NO "Will's Straight Edge" in LA

Joy's Serenade: "Use **will's** straight edge"

William Earl Hidden discovered the hiddenite mineral in 1879. The town of Hiddenite, NC is named after him. There is no equivalent figure or wordplay for LA.

### 4. "Hiddenite" is EXPLICIT

Chapter 4 explicitly states:
> "only some miles up the road was the **Hiddenite emerald mine**"

This is not coded - it's a direct location statement about the author's childhood. LA has no equivalent explicit mention.

### 5. Five NC Geographic References Cluster

| Chapter | NC Location |
|---------|-------------|
| Ch 2 | Chapel Hill (college) |
| Ch 3 | Bakersville (family near Seth Gould) |
| Ch 4 | Hiddenite (childhood) |
| Ch 13 | "I grew up in North Carolina" |
| Ch 18 | Huntersville (parents' church) |

All five cluster in **western North Carolina** - the same region as Rocky Face Mountain. LA has no equivalent clustering.

## How LA Could Still Be Correct

For LA to be the treasure location, we would need to accept that:

1. **Hiddenite/Alexander references are misdirection** - The author deliberately planted NC clues to mislead while the treasure is actually near his home

2. **318 has a different meaning** - The haiku math points to something other than a trail marker

3. **Emotional connection evolved** - The author's deepest emotional connection is now to LA, not his NC childhood

4. **The rose garden is the key clue** - The "massive rose garden...several acres...local park" is more significant than we recognized

5. **Practical trumps emotional** - The author made the rational choice to hide the treasure where he can monitor it
