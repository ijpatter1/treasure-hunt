## Left Page (163) - Carnegie's Philanthropy

### Content
- 1901: Carnegie sold steel companies to J.P. Morgan
- **Became wealthiest man in world**
- Peak fortune equivalent: **$310 billion today**
- Rest of life focused on philanthropy
- **Carnegie Corporation formed: 1911**
- Revolutionary idea: **charitable endowment** (first of its kind)
  - Compounding principal contributions
  - Designed to last in perpetuity

### Carnegie's Libraries
- Funded building of library if local government matched funds for land and books
- Partnered with local communities
- **In U.S. alone: funded almost 1,700 libraries**
- **Over 800 still operate today**

### Carnegie's Endowments
- Carnegie Endowment for International Peace
- Carnegie Institution of Science
- Carnegie Hero Fund
- Carnegie Foundation for the Advancement of Teaching
- Carnegie Mellon University
- Endowments have **more funds today than when chartered 100+ years ago**

### Funded Creations
- Discovery of insulin
- Production of Sesame Street
- Development of Pell Grants

### Carnegie's Total Giving
- **$350 million** (historic dollars)
- Upon death: wife and children received only home and estate
- "Almost everything else went to fund his endowments"

### Author's Reflection
- Emerald "may not be the fanciest or most expensive object"
- Has "significant meaning" as connection to Carnegie
- "Catalyst for change and joy in the world"
- Wonders what finders will do with treasure items

### Potential Clues
- Carnegie's model: give almost everything away
- Libraries as community institutions
