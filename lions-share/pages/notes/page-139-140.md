## Left Page (139) - Rome Olympics & Personal Struggles

### 1960 Rome Olympics Injury
- Day before competition: Sprained ankle stepping in hole
- Ankle swelled and turned blue
- Rumors spread she would withdraw
- **Competed anyway** - won all qualifying races

### Gold Medal Results
- **100-meter:** Gold
- **200-meter:** Gold
- **400-meter relay:** Gold
- **World records in all three events**
- "From polio survivor to fastest woman in world"

### Personal Struggles (from autobiography)
- Brace was "psychologically devastating"
- Felt unlovable due to loneliness and teasing
- "I lived in mortal fear of being disliked"
- Afraid to beat teammates - feared losing friendships
- In 1956 trials, pulled up to let friend Mae Faggs win

### Racism Experiences
- Mother cleaned homes of wealthy white people
- Not allowed to speak up in white grocery stores
- Rode in back of buses
- Woman in Hawaii: "What are you nativoes doing out on the street?"
- Man spat on her in bus station
- Bus driver refused to drive track team to Olympic trials

### Economic Struggles
- $70/week at Tennessee State jobs
- Couldn't afford childcare
- Sister Yvonne helped care for daughter Yolanda
- Drove through night to St. Louis to retrieve Yolanda

## Right Page (140) - Legacy & Muhammad Ali Connection

### Post-Olympic Life
- Fought for racial equality
- Worked to support black athletes
- **Led to full racial business integration in Clarksville, TN**

### **AUTHOR'S TREASURE HUNTING REFLECTION:**
- "I imagine if Wilma Rudolph were alive today and wanted to, she might find all the treasures I have hidden by her sheer will alone."
- "She might simply have looked everywhere, leaving no stone unturned."
- **"What I enjoy most about Rudolph's life story is that it shows how much can be accomplished with determination."**
- "The next time I find myself wanting to give up, it'll be harder to do so."

### Muhammad Ali Connection
- **Cassius Clay** (later Muhammad Ali) won boxing gold at 1960 Rome Olympics
- Age 18 at Rome
- Lost his medal - allegedly threw it off **Second Street Bridge** after being refused service at restaurant
- 1996 Atlanta Olympics: Ali lit Olympic torch, received replacement medal

### Ali's Brother Rahman's Account
- "And when we got to the Second Street Bridge, my brother tossed his beloved medal in the river."
