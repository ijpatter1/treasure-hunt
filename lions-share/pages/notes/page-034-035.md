## Right Page (35) - Khmer Burial, Champa Culture

### Content
- Khmer believed body is a shrine - wearing gold was worship
- Gold worn as pendants, on fingers, even on toes
- Khmer dressed in gold for afterlife - jewelry destroyed in cremation
- **"Rings like ours that survive today were discovered mostly in hidden caches, secreted away within temple walls"**
- **Khmer reign**: 8th century until 1431, defeated by Siams
- Drought and flooding followed, Angkor abandoned, jungle overtook city
- Angkor "hidden from outside world" for three centuries
- **Champa**: Maritime culture on eastern shore of Vietnam
  - Dating back to 2nd century AD
  - Self-governing, independent, resilient regions
  - Prioritized independence, dissociated from invaders
  - Successfully defended against Khmer for most of their reign
  - 1145: Part of Champa succumbed to Khmer rule (lasted only 2 years)
  - Champa navy attacked and defeated Angkor, controlled it for 4 years

### Potential Clues
- **"Hidden caches, secreted away within temple walls"** - describes how rings survived
  - Could be metaphorical hint about treasure hiding place?
- **"Hidden from outside world"** - theme of concealment
- **Champa's revenge** - unexpected twist, underdog victory
- **Maritime culture** - coastal connection
