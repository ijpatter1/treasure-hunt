## Left Page (60) - Art Smith's Career

### Content
- Art got job teaching kids at art center in **Harlem**
- Met **Winifred Mason** - African-American jewelry artist
- Winifred had just opened jewelry store in **Greenwich Village**
- Art inspired to open his own shop
- 1940s: Difficult to find landlord who would rent to black man
- Partnered with white man from Winifred's shop to get lease
- Brick smashed through store window, insults shouted - Art remained resolute
- Art befriended **Talley Beatty** - dance choreographer who worked with **Duke Ellington**
- Talley commissioned Art to design jewelry for dancers
- Talley wanted jewelry that was bold, striking from close and far
- **"Many of Art Smith's most iconic jewelry pieces were originally worn in these large performances"**
- Art Smith quote: "A piece of jewelry is in a sense an object that is not complete...until you relate it to the body"
- Art's work featured in New York magazines
- **Art Smith became America's very first prominent African-American jewelry designer**
- Signature: asymmetrical forms of **copper, silver, brass** that contour the body
- Influences: **ancient Egypt, tribal Africa, surrealism**
- **"Art Smith became the forefather of today's leading African American jewelry artists"**
- Work featured in over a dozen museums
- **"Art Smith's jewelry pieces are now very rare...These objects do not often change hands"**

### Potential Clues
- **Harlem, New York** - where Art taught
- **Greenwich Village, New York** - where Winifred's store was
- **Duke Ellington** connection - famous jazz musician
- Art Smith pieces are **very rare** - significant treasure value

### Geographic References

### Names
