## Left Page (74) - Amelia's Poetry & Solo Flight

### Content
- Amelia loved poetry for making subtle commentaries

### Amelia's Poems:
1. **"From an Airplane"** - bird's eye view perspective
   - "Even the watchful purple hills / That hold the lake / Could not see so well as I..."
   - "Nor the round, yellow eyes of the hamlet / Growing filmy with mists"

2. **"Courage"** - why she wanted to explore
   - "How can life grant us boon of living... / Unless we dare"

3. **"Only Baggage"** - about 1928 Atlantic flight where she was just a passenger
   - "Precious freight," "child-eyed wonder," "maiden spirit," "mother-courage"
   - "Atalante of the Air" (reference to Atalanta, Greek mythological figure)

### 1928 Flight Details:
- First woman to fly across Atlantic (as passenger)
- **Wilmer Stultz** and **Louis Gordon** actually piloted
- Amelia sat in back doing nothing - this bothered her

### **Solo Flight - May 20, 1932:**
- **"Her flight lifted off the ground on Friday, May 20th, 1932"**
- She was the only person aboard
- Plane: Vega 5B

### Potential Clues
- **"Purple hills that hold the lake"** - landscape description
- **"Yellow eyes of the hamlet"** - village lights imagery
- **May 20, 1932** - specific date
- **"Atalante of the Air"** - Greek mythology reference

### Geographic References
- None specific (poetic descriptions of landscapes)

### Final Flight (May 21, 1937):
- **Amelia Earhart and Fred Noonan**
- Attempted to be first to fly around the world
- "Five years and one day later" (from solo Atlantic flight)
- Flew 22 days of planned 30-day trip
- Aircraft never discovered
