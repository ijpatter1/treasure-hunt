## Null Hypothesis

This theory would be **DISPROVEN** if:

1. Author explicitly names a different state or region as the Lion's Share location
2. Clue analysis reveals the treasure must be in a non-mountainous region
3. "Pike" is definitively proven to reference a location-specific feature not present at Stone Mountain (e.g., pike fish, a road named Turnpike)
4. Physical search of all accessible areas yields no treasure and eliminates all plausible hiding spots
5. "X leads the way" is proven to reference a specific marker system not present in this park

## Confidence Assessment

**Overall:** Medium

**Justification:**
- **Strong matches:** Oak trees, granite dome/rock, waterfalls, elevation, "grit" from haiku, pine trees
- **Moderate matches:** Butterflies as "dancers", multiple peaks as "pike", secluded trails
- **Weak/unclear:** No pike fish, no X markers, unclear gold circles reference, unclear haiku physical feature
- **Biographical fit:** Same state as author's childhood, mountain/woodland setting similar to 20-acre family land, but further than "miles up the road" from Hiddenite

The hypothesis merits active investigation. Multiple strong poem-to-feature alignments exist, particularly for the haiku ("grit" = granite, "pine" = white pine) and elevation/rock references. The main uncertainty centers on the "pike" interpretation and "X leads the way" clue.

## Key Areas of Interest

1. **Stone Mountain Summit** - 600-ft granite dome, sitting spots, panoramic views
2. **Wolf Rock** - "Extremely flat and massive", underrated trail, views of Blue Ridge escarpment
3. **Cedar Rock** - Views of Stone Mountain, less crowded
4. **Stone Mountain Falls Area** - 200-ft slide waterfall, water feature
5. **Hutchinson Homestead Area** - Historic site at mountain base, butterfly viewing location
