# Evidence Log: Stone Mountain State Park

Chronological record of evidence discovery for the Stone Mountain hypothesis.

---

## 2026-01-10

### Initial Research - Park Overview

**Source:** NC Parks website, Wikipedia, NCpedia
**Finding:** Stone Mountain State Park is a 14,353-acre park featuring a 600-foot granite dome designated as a National Natural Landmark (1974). Located in Alleghany and Wilkes counties, ~60 miles NW of Winston-Salem.
**Impact:** Supports
**Notes:** The granite dome provides a strong match for "sat upon a rock" and the haiku's "grit" reference. The dome is the largest plutonic monadnock in North Carolina.

---

### Initial Research - Geological Composition

**Source:** NC Geology, Wikipedia
**Finding:** The granite is composed of quartz, alkali feldspar, plagioclase feldspar, and biotite/muscovite mica. The rock is 400 million years old (Devonian age). Quartz content means the rock literally contains "grit" and can "sparkle."
**Impact:** Supports
**Notes:** Direct match for haiku line "Be solid, have grit." Quartz crystals sparkle in sunlight, matching "sparkle even as you pine."

---

### Initial Research - Flora

**Source:** NCpedia, Sherpa Guides
**Finding:** Chestnut oak forests dominate the slopes. Scarlet oak and black oak also present, along with red maple, hickory, white pine, and dogwood. Understory includes blueberry, rhododendron, and mountain laurel.
**Impact:** Supports
**Notes:** Direct match for "shady oaks" from Joy's Serenade. Multiple oak species confirmed. White pine presence matches "pine" from haiku.

---

### Initial Research - Water Features

**Source:** NC Parks, AllTrails, ncwaterfalls.com
**Finding:** Park contains multiple waterfalls: Stone Mountain Falls (200-ft slide waterfall), Middle Falls, Lower Falls, and Widow's Creek Falls. Multiple trout streams (Garden Creek, Widow's Creek, Big Sandy Creek) with wild trout.
**Impact:** Supports
**Notes:** Strong match for "magic in the water." The 200-foot waterfall is a major feature.

---

### Initial Research - Trail System

**Source:** NC Parks, AllTrails, hiking blogs
**Finding:**
- Stone Mountain Loop Trail: 4.5 mi, strenuous, orange circle blazes
- Wolf Rock Trail: 1.5 mi, moderate, red square blazes
- Cedar Rock Trail: 1.0 mi, moderate, red circle blazes
- Middle/Lower Falls Trail: 1.0 mi, moderate, blue circle blazes
- Mountains-to-Sea Trail: 11.32 mi segment passes through

**Impact:** Neutral
**Notes:** Trail blazes use circles and squares, not X markers. Wolf Rock and Cedar Rock described as "underrated and often underpopulated" - potential match for "where few have seen it."

---

### Initial Research - Rock Formations

**Source:** NC Parks, Wikipedia, hiking blogs
**Finding:** Multiple rock formations in addition to main dome: Wolf Rock, Cedar Rock, Little Stone Mountain, Buzzard Rock, First Flat Rock, Hitching Rock. Wolf Rock described as "extremely flat and massive, extending a few hundred feet out and over a thousand feet wide."
**Impact:** Supports
**Notes:** Multiple "pike" (peaked formations) "all around" if using the Northern England interpretation. Wolf Rock's flat surface is ideal for sitting.

---

### Initial Research - Wildlife (Butterflies)

**Source:** NC Parks, wildlife blogs
**Finding:** Swallowtail, monarch, and fritillary butterflies documented in park. Park offers guided butterfly hikes to Hutchinson Homestead wildflower fields.
**Impact:** Supports
**Notes:** Butterflies = "Dancers are on the land" based on Hopi tradition (book Chapter 8 connection). The park actively promotes butterfly viewing.

---

### Initial Research - Fish Species

**Source:** NC Angler forums, fishing guides
**Finding:** Park streams contain only trout species: rainbow trout, brown trout, and brook trout. No pike fish documented. Pike are cold-water lake fish not native to NC mountain streams.
**Impact:** Challenges
**Notes:** Weakens the "pike" = fish interpretation. Must rely on alternative: pike = peaked hills.

---

### Initial Research - Historic Sites

**Source:** NC Parks, Blue Ridge Heritage
**Finding:**
- Hutchinson Homestead: Established 1855 by John and Cidney Jane Hutchinson. Log cabin, barn, blacksmith shop, corn crib, smokehouse. Restored 1998.
- Garden Creek Baptist Church: Established 1897, still holds seasonal services.

**Impact:** Neutral
**Notes:** Historic context. Church established in 1897 is interesting given author's father is a pastor, but no direct connection identified.
