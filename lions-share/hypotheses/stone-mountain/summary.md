# Stone Mountain State Park - Summary

*Last updated: 2026-01-10*

---

## Current Status

Stone Mountain State Park in Alleghany/Wilkes County, NC presents a **compelling candidate** for the Lion's Share location. The 600-foot granite dome provides a literal match for "sat upon a rock" and "grit" from the haiku, while oak-dominated slopes and multiple waterfalls align with "shady oaks" and "magic in the water."

**Investigation Phase:** Initial research complete; detailed analysis in progress.

---

## Strongest Evidence

1. **Granite dome = "grit" and "sat upon a rock"**
   - 600-foot granite dome, largest plutonic monadnock in NC
   - Composed of quartz, feldspar, biotite mica - literal "grit"
   - Wolf Rock described as "extremely flat and massive" - natural sitting spot
   - Classification: Direct, High weight

2. **Oak trees = "shady oaks"**
   - Chestnut oak forests cover the slopes
   - Scarlet oak and black oak also present
   - Mountain laurel and rhododendron in understory
   - Classification: Direct, High weight

3. **Waterfalls = "magic in the water"**
   - Stone Mountain Falls: 200-foot slide waterfall
   - Middle Falls and Lower Falls on Big Sandy Creek
   - Widow's Creek Falls (popular swimming hole)
   - Multiple trout streams (Garden Creek, Widow's Creek, Big Sandy Creek)
   - Classification: Direct, High weight

4. **Elevation = "don't go down" / "how high you ever want to be"**
   - Summit at 2,305 feet elevation
   - 600 feet above valley floor
   - Spectacular views from summit, Wolf Rock, Cedar Rock
   - Classification: Direct, High weight

5. **Pine trees = "sparkle even as you pine"**
   - White pine present in woodlands
   - Small pines and cedars fringe the rock outcrops
   - Pine-Oak Heath natural community documented
   - Classification: Direct, Moderate weight

6. **Butterflies = "Dancers are on the land"**
   - Swallowtail, monarch, and fritillary butterflies documented
   - Park offers guided butterfly hikes to Hutchinson Homestead
   - Hopi tradition interprets butterflies as "dancers" (book Ch 8 connection)
   - Classification: Derived, Moderate weight

---

## Biggest Challenges

1. **No pike fish**
   - Park streams contain only trout (rainbow, brown, brook)
   - Pike are cold-water lake fish, not found in NC mountain streams
   - Must rely on "pike = peaked hills" interpretation
   - Multiple peaks exist: Wolf Rock, Cedar Rock, Little Stone Mountain, Buzzard Rock

2. **No "X" markers documented**
   - Trail blazes use circles and squares (orange, red, blue, white)
   - No evidence of X-shaped trail markers or crossings
   - "X leads the way" remains unresolved

3. **"Shimmering circles of gold" unclear**
   - No obvious feature identified
   - Could be: sun reflection on water? Golden lichen on rocks? Circular rock features?
   - Requires physical reconnaissance

4. **Distance from Hiddenite**
   - Author describes Hiddenite as "only some miles up the road" from childhood home
   - Stone Mountain is ~50-60 miles from Statesville/Hiddenite area
   - Not disqualifying, but doesn't match the "miles up the road" proximity
