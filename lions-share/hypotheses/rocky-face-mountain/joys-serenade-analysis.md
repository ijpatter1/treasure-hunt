## Author Connection to Hiddenite

Jon Collins-Black was born in 1973 at Iredell Memorial Hospital in Statesville, NC—just 17 miles from Hiddenite. His father served as minister at Grace Baptist Church in Statesville, and the family lived on 20 acres of woodland where his father built a log cabin by hand. Collins-Black explicitly states he visited **Emerald Hollow Mine in Hiddenite** as a child, "sifting through creeks and dirt searching for hidden gems."

This biographical connection—a childhood spent literally hunting for treasure in this specific location—makes Rocky Face Mountain a compelling candidate for the Lion's Share treasure.

### Etymology of "Pike"
The word "pike" has meant "sharp, pointed mountain or summit" since c. 1400, derived from the same root as "peak." This is not a reference to fish—it describes the panoramic view of pointed mountains visible from Rocky Face's summit.

### The Butterfly Connection
Rocky Face Mountain is documented habitat for **Giant Swallowtail** butterflies—the largest butterfly species in North America. The park's official plant documentation lists **Hop-tree** (*Ptelea trifoliata*), a primary host plant for Giant Swallowtails. Additional documented species include Common Buckeye and Coral Hairstreak. The "dancers on the land" and "flutter of excitement" clues both point to these butterflies.

## Search Protocol

### Getting There
1. Park at Rocky Face Mountain Recreational Area (3451 Rocky Face Church Road, Hiddenite, NC 28636)
2. Take the **Hollow Rock Trail** (red blazes)
3. Follow markers sequentially: 313 → 314 → 315 → 316 → 317 → **318**

### At Marker 318
1. **Identify the curve** in the trail at this point
2. **Look for oak trees** providing shade nearby
3. **Locate the granite outcrop** visible in satellite imagery

### The Critical Step
4. **LEAVE THE TRAIL AND GO DOWN**
   - Descend the slope from marker 318
   - The treasure is **below trail level**
   - Look "where few have seen it"—off the beaten path

### What to Search
5. Examine the **rock formation** for:
   - Natural crevices or hiding spots
   - Underhangs or sheltered areas
   - Base of the rock
   - Any spot a container could be wedged or placed

### Remember
- Treasure is **"not buried"** per the book—look in natural features, not underground
- It's on **public land**, within **3 miles of a road**, searchable in a **single day**
- No water crossings or cliff scaling required
- The area should have **shimmering mica** in the granite when sunlight hits it
