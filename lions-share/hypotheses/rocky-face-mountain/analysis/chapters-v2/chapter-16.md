## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 16 tells the story of the Nigerian soccer team's improbable gold medal victory at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics. The Nigerian national team, formed in 1949 (86 years after England's first team), had never won an Olympic game in 47 years, being outscored 22-7 in three prior Olympic appearances. The team was plagued by tribal conflicts, a corrupt and intrusive Nigerian Football Association (NFA), and financial crises -- coach Jo Bonfrere's salary went unpaid, and the team trained in a cockroach-filled two-star motel in Tallahassee, Florida with no money for transportation. Bonfrere attempted to resign but was persuaded to stay by his players.

Despite these obstacles, Nigeria beat Hungary 1-0 (first ever Olympic win), Japan (two goals in final 8 minutes), and upset Mexico 2-0 to reach the semifinals. Against Brazil (world #1), they trailed 3-1 at halftime. When Ronaldo was substituted out with 15 minutes remaining, Nigeria scored twice in the final minutes (Kanu Nwankwo) to tie 3-3, then Kanu scored the winner 4 minutes into overtime. In the gold medal match against Argentina, Nigeria again trailed early (1-0 at 3 minutes, later 2-1), but Daniel Amokachi equalized at the 74th minute, and Emmanuel Amunike scored the winner with under a minute remaining. Nigeria became the first African team to win Olympic soccer gold.

The author provides direct encouragement to treasure hunters: "If you begin to question whether you can find the treasure I have hidden, I invite you to return to this chapter. It's here to help you. Maybe it can provide you just the direction you need." He confirms five treasure boxes and reflects on his own family (wife, two children born in "middle years") and that the treasure hunt "would have seemed implausible just six or seven years ago."
