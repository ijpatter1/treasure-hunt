## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 6 centers on rubies as inspirational gemstones and introduces two contemporary jewelry designers who created bespoke pieces for the treasure. The author discusses ruby symbolism across cultures: in Burma, rubies represent "the inextinguishable flame of immortality" and warriors literally inserted rubies under their skin; in Thailand's Noppakao system, the ruby corresponds with the sun; in Hindu traditions, offering rubies to Krishna could grant better reincarnation. Three ruby items are in the treasure: Lauren Harwell Godfrey's ruby pendant necklace ($45,000+, from her "Cleopatra's Vault" collection), Angie Marei's wing-shaped ruby earrings ($26,000+), and a 16th-century Sukhothai Thailand ruby ring with pigeon's blood color from Tuyet Nguyet's collection.

The author reveals significant personal background: his father was a pastor who traded pulpits with local black ministers; as a child, he couldn't afford posters or comic books, so he copied quotes from Waldenbooks posters onto his bedroom wall. His childhood heroes were Martin Luther King Jr. and Mahatma Gandhi. He describes being profoundly inspired by his first visit to Rev. James Henderson's black Baptist church "down the street." As an adult, he finds inspiration in fatherhood, writing children's books, and meditation (practiced for 10 years).

The chapter climaxes with direct advice to treasure hunters: "Will you need inspiration to find the treasure boxes I have hidden? I imagine you might." And the critical sentence: "Just like peering behind a rock to find a treasure box, inspiration can appear almost anywhere. Just feel for where its breeze nudges you."

### SPECIAL ANALYSIS: "Peering Behind a Rock"

This is the single most important phrase in the chapter for any location hypothesis. Detailed decomposition:

**Full sentence:** "Just like peering behind a rock to find a treasure box, inspiration can appear almost anywhere."

**Grammatical structure:** The treasure box finding is presented as a **simile** for inspiration -- "Just like X, Y." The author is comparing finding inspiration to finding a treasure box behind a rock. This means:
1. **Literal reading:** He is describing the actual method of finding the treasure (behind a rock) and using it as a comparison.
2. **Metaphorical reading:** "Behind a rock" is just a vivid image for "in an unexpected place."

**Which is more likely?** The literal reading is strengthened by:
- The author said "almost every chapter has at least one important detail" for the Lion's Share
- The phrase "treasure box" is used (not "treasure" generically) -- this matches the physical treasure boxes
- The preceding paragraph asks "Will you need inspiration to find the treasure boxes I have hidden?" -- directly addressing hunters, then immediately using the rock metaphor
- Stanza 7: "sat upon a rock" -- confirms rock is a key physical feature

**What "behind" means geometrically:**
- Behind = the side facing AWAY from the approaching viewer
- If approaching from the trail, "behind" = the side of the rock facing away from the trail
- This means the treasure is on the DOWNHILL or OFF-TRAIL side of a rock formation
- The searcher must walk AROUND a rock to its far side

**Does the chapter give directional clues about WHICH side?**
- "Breeze nudges you" -- wind direction is variable but prevailing winds at Rocky Face come from the west/southwest. The trail approaches Zone C from the south (clockwise). "Behind" from the trail approach would be the NORTH side of a rock formation.
- "Go down" (Stanza 5) combined with "behind" suggests: the treasure is on the downslope side of a rock, not visible from the uphill trail approach.
- The Emerson quote's "what lies within us" could stretch to "within" a rock cavity, but this is speculative.

**Assessment:** The "behind a rock" phrase has HIGH HRT relevance because Rocky Face Mountain is defined by granite rock formations. However, it has equally HIGH relevance for any rock-featuring location hypothesis. The phrase tells us HOW the treasure is hidden (behind a rock), not WHERE the rock is. It narrows the search method, not the search location.

## Confirmation Bias Audit

### What Would Disprove This Chapter's HRT Connections?
- If "peering behind a rock" is purely metaphorical, the chapter's primary HRT value collapses. The phrase IS a simile ("just like..."), which grammatically introduces a comparison, not a statement of fact.
- If the treasure is NOT behind a rock at all (e.g., in a tree hollow, under a bridge, in a man-made structure), then the most compelling phrase in the entire book is misleading -- which the author explicitly said he would not do ("no explanation is necessary" to one with faith).
- If the wind/breeze metaphor is purely about inspiration (its literal context in the chapter), then the physical wind interpretation is projection.
