## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 1, "The 120 Carat Sapphire," is subtitled "A Plan That Changed the World." It opens with quotes from Eleanor Roosevelt and Mary Oliver about planning and purpose. The author recounts his 3.5-year-old son making a "treasure map" using a street map of Los Angeles, illustrating that the first step in any pursuit is having a roadmap.

The narrative then tells the founding story of Apple Computer. Two Steves (Jobs and Wozniak) met through Bill Fernandez, started the Homebrew Computer Club in Menlo Park, California in 1975, and were joined by investor Mike Markkula and first CEO Mike Scott. Scott's business plan drove Apple's early growth, leading to a $1.7 billion IPO in 1980. After leaving Apple in 1981, Mike Scott assembled one of the world's most valuable rare gem collections (2,600 specimens), including the 120-carat raw sapphire from Ratnapura, Sri Lanka now in the treasure. His collection was displayed at the Bowers Museum near Los Angeles.

The chapter closes with the author reflecting on parenting values (kindness, generosity, integrity) he shares with wife Kimberly, and states the lesson: "the first step in treasure hunting is to formulate a map, the likely second step is one of readying ourselves." He then introduces Tuyet Nguyet as the subject of Chapter 2.
