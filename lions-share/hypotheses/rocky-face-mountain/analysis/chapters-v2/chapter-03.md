## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 3, "A Puzzle Box, a Magnifying Glass, & the Mysterious Egg," is subtitled "Joy Is in the Details." It opens with quotes from Henri Cartier-Bresson (on how small details become great subjects) and Gosho Aoyama ("With a keen eye for details, one truth prevails!"). The author describes his late 2018 trip to Japan after creative burnout in the music industry. He and wife Kimberly fell in love with Tokyo, then traveled to Hakone, famous for its mineral baths and traditional wooden puzzle boxes (Himitsu-Bako). They bought several puzzle boxes as gifts.

The narrative then pivots 6,000 nautical miles east to Bakersville, North Carolina, "forty miles northeast of Asheville," where blacksmith Seth Gould lives and works. Seth's masterwork "Coffer" resides at the Smithsonian American Art Museum -- a nearly two-foot metal puzzle box forged from iron, brass, and steel that took three years to build. Seth, who also visited Hakone and was inspired by Japanese puzzle boxes, was commissioned to create all five treasure boxes. The Lion's Share box requires "seven actions" to open and combines design elements from the other four boxes (yellow brass sides, steel top edge, textured iron center locking panel). The author explicitly states: "I made sure he knew I'd grown up in North Carolina and that I had lots of family who lived not too far from him."

The chapter also introduces Michael Perkhin, head workmaster at Faberge, who created a gold and jade magnifying glass (valued over $25,000) now in the treasure. Perkhin designed more than half of the 54 Imperial Faberge eggs. The Third Imperial Faberge Egg story illustrates the chapter theme: lost for 87 years, it sat on a scraps metal dealer's kitchen counter for 8 years before a tiny engraving ("Vacheron Constantin") was finally noticed, revealing its $33 million value. The Third Imperial Egg is NOT in the treasure (sidebar explicitly states this -- the story illustrates the importance of details). The chapter closes: "These men blazed their own paths," transitioning to Chapter 4 and Rob Lavinsky.

## New Research Questions
1. **Where exactly does the author's family live in western NC?** "Not too far" from Bakersville could mean Mitchell, Avery, Burke, or Caldwell County -- or Alexander County (where Rocky Face is). This specificity matters.
2. **Is "Coffer" (as architectural term for recessed panel) connected to "turning square" in Stanza 3?** Coffered ceilings use square recesses. Worth exploring.
3. **Does the number 18 appear significantly more often than other numbers in the book?** Three appearances in Chapters 2-3 (Perkhin goldsmith at 18, Seth Gould 18 months, haiku 18 syllables) could be pattern or coincidence.
4. **Was Seth Gould's Hakone visit before or after the author's?** If after, Seth may have been sent by the author; if before, it is an independent coincidence.
5. **Does the phrase "no discernable way to open" recur in other chapters?** If it appears in Ch 9 or other mapped chapters, it strengthens the cross-chapter hiding-methodology pattern.
