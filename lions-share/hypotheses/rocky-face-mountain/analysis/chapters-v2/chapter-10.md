## Chapter Summary (Factual)

Chapter 10 tells the story of Jackie Kennedy Onassis through the lens of resilience. The author opens with a personal memory of crying in a parking lot at age 11 after losing a football playoff game, and his father kneeling down to comfort him. This speech inspired the author's only tattoo -- Sanskrit on his left forearm translating to "I welcome the good with the bad, as they both come from the one true source." The Jackie Onassis brooch (sapphires and diamonds inlaid around rock crystal, offered for $150,000) is his father's favorite item in the entire treasure.

Jackie's biography is told with emphasis on her resilience through tragedy (JFK assassination November 22, 1963 in Dallas, RFK assassination, miscarriage, stillborn, infant Patrick's death). After marrying Aristotle Onassis for privacy, she later became the first former first lady to return to a traditional workplace at Viking Press, then Doubleday for 16 years. She fought to save Grand Central Station and created the White House Historical Association.

The chapter closes with critical treasure hunt framing: "This hunt is designed to bring you joy, not suffering" and "Searching and not finding the treasure will most likely only leave you with happy memories of spending time outdoors with a loved one or with the delightful sights, smells, and sounds of nature around you."
