## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 7 profiles Amelia Earhart through the lens of exploration and curiosity. The treasure item is a signed photograph of Earhart taken in Londonderry, Ireland after her May 20, 1932 solo Atlantic crossing in a Lockheed Vega 5B -- the only known signed copy (unsigned versions exist at Purdue and Harvard). The chapter chronicles her life from childhood adventures (elephants at the world's fair at age 7, homemade roller coaster at age 8) through her aviation records (first woman to fly solo across the Atlantic, first from Hawaii to US mainland, fastest from Mexico City to Washington DC) to her disappearance with navigator Fred Noonan on May 21, 1937 -- "five years and one day" after her solo Atlantic flight.

The chapter includes three of Earhart's poems: "From an Airplane" (bird's eye landscape view), "Courage" (the imperative to dare), and "Only Baggage" (her frustration at being a mere passenger on the 1928 Atlantic crossing, referencing "Atalante of the Air"). The author draws parallels between Earhart's boundless curiosity and his own children's enthusiasm, observing that children's "unadulterated, uninhibited, unbridled enthusiasm for everything" is a quality adults often lose.

The chapter concludes with direct treasure-hunting advice: "Go where others have not. We never know what's past the next tree line, over the next rise, or around the next corner unless we see it for ourselves." It transitions to Chapter 8 by introducing Charles Loloma, "the most renowned Native American jewelry designer who ever lived," who lived on the Hopi reservation in Arizona.

### "Tree Line, Rise, Corner" -- Detailed Terrain Analysis

This is the chapter's most significant HRT passage. Three features listed in sequence:

1. **"Past the next tree line"** -- On the Hollow Rock Trail clockwise approach, the summit dome area (markers 317-320) transitions from forest canopy to exposed granite. There IS a tree line where pine/hardwood forest gives way to open rock. This transition occurs near marker 317-318. "Past" the tree line means into the open granite area.

2. **"Over the next rise"** -- The trail gains elevation approaching the summit dome. The "rise" corresponds to the climb toward the highest point of the granite formation. "Over" suggests going beyond the crest of the rise.

3. **"Around the next corner"** -- The trail curves at approximately markers 317-318 before continuing along the summit dome. "Around the corner" means continuing past the curve toward markers 319-320 -- directly into Zone C.

**If read as sequential waypoints:** tree line -> rise -> corner maps precisely to the clockwise trail approach to Zone C: leave the forest (tree line), climb the dome (rise), round the curve (corner), and there is the treasure zone.

**Counter-argument:** These are the three most common terrain features on any hiking trail in the southeastern US. Describing a tree line, a rise, and a corner is like saying "past the trees, over the hill, around the bend" -- it could describe thousands of trails. The specificity is in the eye of the reader.

### Earhart Poetry Analysis

**"From an Airplane" (p. 74):**
> "Even the watchful purple hills / That hold the lake / Could not see so well as I"

- **"Purple hills"** -- The Blue Ridge Mountains DO appear purple at distance (hence "Blue Ridge"). This could describe western NC terrain viewed from an elevated position.
- **"That hold the lake"** -- Are there lakes visible from Rocky Face Mountain summit? Lake Hickory is ~10 miles south. Smaller ponds may exist in the lowlands.
- **Counter:** This is Earhart's poem about Ireland/England viewed from above. The "purple hills" are the Welsh/English/Irish landscape, not NC.

**"Courage" (p. 74):**
> "How can life grant us boon of living... Unless we dare"

- Thematic: daring to explore off-trail. No spatial content.

**"Only Baggage" (p. 74):**
> "Atalante of the Air!"

- **Atalanta** (Greek mythology) -- swift-footed huntress who ran races and threw golden apples. Atalanta is also the historical spelling variant of **Atlanta**, GA, which is ~260 miles from Rocky Face Mountain. Very weak connection.
- The "golden apples" in the Atalanta myth could connect to "shimmering circles of gold" (Stanza 4), but this is extremely speculative.
