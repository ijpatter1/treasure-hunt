## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

The front matter spans the cover through page 17 and establishes the book's fundamental framework. The back cover contains a three-line poem ("Be solid, have grit; / sparkle even as you pine. / Here lies a joy divined"). The dedication names the author's family (Kimberly, Aiden, Londyn) and includes a Saint Augustine quote linking treasure, heart, and happiness. The table of contents reveals 23 chapters organized loosely into two parts, plus front and back matter sections.

The introduction (pp. 9-14) announces the treasure hunt: five custom boxes hidden in five US locations. Four smaller boxes (six-figure value each) have dedicated chapters in Part Two. The fifth "Lion's Share" box is substantially larger and its clues are scattered throughout the entire book -- "almost every chapter in Part One offers at least one important detail." The author describes his motivation as a personal quest for joy, his background as a full-time dad writing children's books during Covid, and enlisting five researchers for archival work.

"How To Read This Book" (pp. 16-17) provides explicit guidance: read thoughtfully, note anything that triggers intuition ("Spidey sense tingle"), research captivating topics further, and attend to details. The author explicitly disclaims complex ciphers: "I'm not a genius, so creating some complex cipher or grand secret code is beyond my scope." Physical search ("boots on the ground") is required. The section closes with Cesare Pavese's quote: "The only joy in the world is to begin."

## Confirmation Bias Audit

### What Would Disprove This Chapter's HRT Connections?
- If the back cover poem's syllable count were shown to be irrelevant to the author's design (e.g., confirmed as a standard haiku form with no numerical encoding intent)
- If "grit" and "sparkle" were shown to be purely motivational language (as they are commonly used) with no geological subtext
- If the park acreage of 318 were found to be a coincidence (many parks have 3-digit acreages)
- If the 5-7-6 syllable pattern were shown to be an imperfect haiku rather than an intentional deviation encoding information

## Confidence Assessment
First Pass: HIGH -> Second Pass: MODERATE-HIGH | Reason: The back cover haiku encoding (3+18=318=park acreage) remains the strongest single piece of front-matter evidence. However, the individual word-level connections ("grit," "sparkle," "pine") are each individually weak and rely on clustering to achieve significance. The front matter deliberately avoids geographic specifics, so no direct location confirmation is possible from these pages alone. The encoding argument is notable but rests on concatenation (3 || 18) rather than a more conventional mathematical operation, which introduces arbitrariness. Overall, the front matter provides a suggestive but not definitive foundation for the HRT hypothesis.

## New Research Questions
1. **Can the topographic map fragment on the title page be identified?** Higher-resolution analysis might reveal if it matches any specific terrain.
2. **Is the 5-7-6 syllable structure intentional or accidental?** A true haiku is 5-7-5; the extra syllable in line 3 could be deliberate encoding or simply imperfect form.
3. **Does Cesare Pavese have any connection to treasure hunts, geology, or North Carolina?** His rural Italian themes may be purely motivational.
4. **What is the printer's mark on the copyright page?** The "small decorative symbol" at bottom right may be standard or may be custom.
5. **Are there other 3-digit acreage parks near the author's childhood home?** If Rocky Face is the only 318-acre park in Alexander County, the encoding is more significant; if several parks have matching acreages to various derivations, the significance decreases.
