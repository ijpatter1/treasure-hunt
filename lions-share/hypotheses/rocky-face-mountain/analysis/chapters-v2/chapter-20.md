## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 20, "The Six-Figure Birthstone," features two treasure items: a 201-carat smokey quartz cut in the "Imperial Brilliant" design by Mark Oros (from Rio de Janeiro state, Brazil) and a 12-carat tsavorite garnet worth six figures. The chapter centers on the author's two perspective shifts regarding gemstones he previously dismissed. As a child, he found only dark smokey quartz while digging for crystals (a direct callback to Chapter 4), which created a lasting association with "disappointment and unrewarded toil." Encountering the Oros-cut stone in a private collection without knowing its identity broke that forty-year bias.

The second shift involved garnet, the author's January birthstone. At age nine, he learned his birthstone was the same red rock used in the asphalt on his street, while his father and sister had rubies, his friend had emerald, and his baby brother had aquamarine. After "nearly forty years" of disgust, he discovered tsavorite garnet, found by Campbell Bridges in Tanzania after a buffalo charged him into a ditch revealing green stone in a rock outcrop. Bridges searched seven more years before making his official discovery. Tiffany & Co. introduced tsavorite to mainstream jewelry.

The chapter concludes with the author walking through a "massive rose garden" (several acres) at a "local park" with wife Kimberly, using roses as a metaphor for perspective -- a flower at ground level cannot see the garden's beauty the way someone standing can. He previews Chapter 21: "four items from antiquity, all forged as a result of the conquests of Alexander the Great."

## Confirmation Bias Audit

### What Would Disprove This Chapter's HRT Connections?

1. If "lion atop a mountain" is simply a humorous simile (which the full context -- "mountain of decaying catnip" -- strongly suggests), the flagship connection from this chapter collapses.
2. If the Steve de Shazer quote is generic therapy advice (which it is -- de Shazer is the founder of Solution-Focused Brief Therapy), the "vantage point" reading is imported, not inherent.
3. If the author's childhood crystal digging (Ch 4 callback) was in Statesville rather than near Hiddenite specifically, the geographic anchor weakens.
4. The "outcrop of rock" describes Tanzania, not North Carolina. Drawing a connection requires treating any rock-related language as HRT-relevant, which is circular.
