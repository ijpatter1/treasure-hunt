## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 22, "Picasso's Pendant," subtitled "A Love Story," features a 23-karat gold pendant titled "Jacqueline au chevalet" (Jacqueline at the Easel) designed by Picasso in 1956, produced by goldsmith Francois Hugo of Hugo Ateliers in France. Francois was the great-grandson of Victor Hugo. Only 39 such pendants were ever minted, with only 20 available to the public. The author acquired the pendant directly from Francois's grandson Nicolas, who still runs the family workshop.

The chapter tells the story of Picasso (born 1882, died at age 91) and Jacqueline Roque, whom he met at his ceramic studio in Madoura, France. She was 25 and he was 47 years older. Picasso drew a chalk dove mural on her house, then brought her a single red rose every day for six months before she finally accepted dinner. They spent his last twenty years inseparable -- she was "wife, lover, muse, manager, accountant, caretaker, agent, and best friend." He painted her in over 400 works. Jacqueline was by his side when he died in April 1973, and she lived thirteen more years "mostly in solitude" before joining him in death.

The author parallels this with his own love story: he met Kimberly in 2003, started a family in 2017, and was "married only a few months ago" as of writing. It took him "over forty years" to find her. The treasure hunt project took "close to five years" and cost "millions of dollars." Kimberly's favorite song is "Make Your Own Kind of Music" by Cass Elliot, the last song played at their wedding. The chapter closes with treasure-hunting advice: "Picasso was persistent and brave...taking the first step out your door might be the bravest action required of you here."

## Confirmation Bias Audit

### What Would Disprove This Chapter's HRT Connections?

1. If "sparkle" is just a common word used to describe Jacqueline's personality (which it is), the back cover haiku connection is vocabulary cherry-picking.
2. If "fortune favors the brave" is just a standard Virgil quote chosen for a chapter about Picasso's boldness (which it is), the "have grit" connection is thematic at best.
3. If the "over forty years" timeline doesn't cleanly match a 1973 birth year (author met Kimberly in 2003, so "over forty years" of searching means born before 1963, which contradicts 1973), this is actually a timeline PROBLEM, not a confirmation.

**The "Over Forty Years" Problem:** If the author was born in January 1973 and met Kimberly in 2003, he was approximately 30 years old when they met. "Over forty years to find her" would mean he began "searching" before birth -- or the author is rounding up generously, or he was born earlier than 1973. This creates tension with the birth year hypothesis.

**Possible resolution:** The author may count from before his own birth (his parents' marriage or childhood) or is using "forty years" as a rounded/symbolic number rather than a precise count. The "forty years" appears so frequently across multiple chapters that it may be an intentional thematic marker rather than a precise measurement.
