## Chapter Summary (Factual)

Chapter 11 narrates the dramatic story of Captain Felicio de Fonseca and the Nuestra Senora de la Luz (Our Lady of the Light), a ship that sank July 2, 1752 near Montevideo, Uruguay during a violent pampero storm. The ship carried 12,500 gold coins for King Ferdinand VII of Spain on an official 50/50 split, plus hidden contraband: 200,000 pesos in gold coins and 40 gold bars and disks concealed in the powder hold. The ship was dangerously overloaded with 185 passengers, 12 cannons, animals, and rations.

When the pampero struck with 180 km/h winds, Fonseca was on shore and could not reach his ship anchored three miles out in the Rio de la Plata. No passengers survived. Fonseca recovered over 90% of the official cargo within a year, but the hidden treasure remained lost until 1993 when Ruben Corella discovered the wreckage 50 miles from Montevideo while searching for a different shipwreck (El Preciado).

Three La Luz items are in the treasure: a gold bar (22+ oz, $50,000+, XXVI marking), a gold disk (22+ troy oz, .975 purity/23.4 carats, XIV marking), and an 1750 eight-escudo coin (Lima mint, MS 63, one of only 15 rated). The author explicitly states "I placed all our boxes in safe locations" and directs readers to the postscript as "a must read."
