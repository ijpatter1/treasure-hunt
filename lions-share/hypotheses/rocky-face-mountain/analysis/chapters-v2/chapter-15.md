## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 15 opens Part Two of the book and tells the story of Wilma Rudolph, born in 1940 in Clarksville, Tennessee, the 20th of 22 children. Rudolph contracted polio at age 5, was paralyzed in her left leg, and wore a steel brace for years. After twice-weekly rehabilitation trips 50 miles to Nashville's Meharry Medical School, she walked into church without her brace at age 9 and removed it permanently at 12. She discovered track through basketball coach Clinton Grey, trained under Ed Temple at Tennessee State College, and qualified for the 1956 Melbourne Olympics at age 16, winning a bronze in the relay.

At the 1960 Rome Olympics, despite spraining her ankle the day before competition, Rudolph won gold medals in the 100-meter, 200-meter, and 400-meter relay, setting world records in all three. She became the first American woman to win three golds in a single Olympics. The chapter also covers her personal struggles with racism, poverty, and loneliness, and her post-Olympic work for racial equality in Clarksville. The author reflects: "She might simply have looked everywhere, leaving no stone unturned."

The chapter concludes with the story of Cassius Clay (Muhammad Ali), who also won gold at the 1960 Rome Olympics at age 18. Ali allegedly threw his medal off the Second Street Bridge after being refused restaurant service, and received a replacement at the 1996 Atlanta Olympics. This bridges directly to Chapter 16.

## Confirmation Bias Audit

### What Would Disprove This Chapter's HRT Connections?

If the treasure were hidden anywhere requiring effort and persistence (i.e., any location at all), these themes would still be relevant.
