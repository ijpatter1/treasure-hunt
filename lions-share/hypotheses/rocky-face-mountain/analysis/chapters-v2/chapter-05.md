## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 5 tells the story of Art Smith (Arthur George Smith), America's first prominent African-American jewelry designer, whose mother Mary Elizabeth Williams was born in 1879 in Morgan's Pass, Jamaica, and whose family emigrated from Cuba to New York City on July 21, 1920. Art earned a full scholarship to Cooper Union, won first prize in the New York World's Fair Corporation design contest, but rejected a civil engineering career to pursue art. He was mentored by Winifred Mason in Greenwich Village, opened his own shop despite racial hostility, and broke through when choreographer Talley Beatty (who worked with Duke Ellington) commissioned bold jewelry for his dance performances.

Art Smith's signature style featured asymmetrical forms of copper, silver, and brass influenced by ancient Egypt, tribal Africa, and surrealism. Four of his pieces are in the treasure: a bronze/brass cuff bracelet, a modernist brass brooch, a copper/brass brooch, and a free-form sterling silver ring. The chapter frames Art Smith's story through the theme of faith -- "undeniable knowing" rather than mere hope -- and the author uses this to deliver direct advice to treasure hunters.

The chapter's final pages contain the author's most explicit meta-commentary about the hunt: he predicts online forums and YouTube speculation will emerge and advises hunters to "value your own [instincts]. Trust in what you know. Have faith." He describes faith as "an ethereal treasure map guiding each step of our way" and transitions to Chapter 6 by previewing ruby jewelry pieces.
