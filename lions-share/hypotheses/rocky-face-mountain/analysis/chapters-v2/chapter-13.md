## Chapter Summary (Factual)

Chapter 13 profiles Michael Jordan through the treasure item of a 1986 Fleer Jordan Rookie Card (#57, PSA 9 MINT, serial 26670045) sourced from PWCC broker Brent Huigens for $20,000-$75,000. The card is described as "best of its class PSA 9" that "shows better than most PSA 10s."

Jordan's statistics are presented: 6 NBA championships, 5 MVP awards, 22 trophies (most ever), scoring leader 10 of 13 seasons, highest career PPG average. Obama called him "the Michael Jordan of greatness" when presenting the Presidential Medal of Freedom.

The author provides critical biographical details: "I grew up in North Carolina" as a passionate UNC Tar Heels fan. He was 6 years old when Jordan was a UNC freshman (1981-82), placing his birth around 1975-76. He met Jordan at a local high school autograph signing and his father erected a basketball hoop in the driveway when he was 8.

The chapter's centerpiece is the author's "most embarrassing moment" -- at age 13, making the junior-high basketball team as the last pick (12th of 12), sitting on the bench for 2/3 of the season, then in his first game appearance (2:34 remaining, 28-point lead), grabbing a rebound and scoring a layup for the opposing team by running the wrong direction.

The moral: "Don't be like I was, overexcited and out of control, running off with no sense of direction. Be like Mike." The author advises treasure hunters to "take a breath. Reframe and relax. Remaining calm will better suit your efforts."

Jordan's own failure is noted: at 15, he failed to make his varsity high school team in Wilmington, NC, was relegated to JV, then worked harder than anyone and became a top national recruit.
