## Chapter Summary (Factual)

Chapter 12 tells the story of the California Gold Rush through the lens of "Make Good Choices." Opening with Daniel Kahneman's quote about human irrationality, the author traces California's history from Native American lands through Spanish naming (1542, after Queen Calafia), Mexican independence (1821), and US purchase for $15 million (1848). Nine days before the purchase, James Marshall discovered gold at Sutter's Mill, 40 miles east of Sacramento along the American River.

The Gold Rush brought 90,000 people in 1849, another 90,000 in 1850, and by 1852 over 1% of the US population had relocated to California. Sam Brannan became California's first millionaire by selling supplies, not mining. 750,000 pounds of gold were eventually found, financing US industrialization, the transcontinental railroad, and the Northern Army in the Civil War.

The author reveals he has lived in California for over 20 years and shares a personal detail about experiencing financial hardship ("grab a muffin or two in a grocery store"). He states "material possessions without joy are just clutter" and reassures hunters that "at least one of our treasure boxes rests not too far from you" and can be found during "a leisurely afternoon outing in which you enjoy a picnic with a loved one."

The treasure item is a California gold nugget weighing over 4 ounces, found near the same farm where the Gold Rush discovery was verified.
