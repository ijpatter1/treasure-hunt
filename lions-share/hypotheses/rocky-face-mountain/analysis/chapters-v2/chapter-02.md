## Chapter Summary (Factual, 2-3 paragraphs)

Chapter 2, "The 100 Gold Rings of Tuyet Nguyet," is subtitled "Don't Wing It." It opens with quotes from Mark Twain and Jackie Joyner-Kersee about preparation. The author recounts his first job after college -- finding a sales position through The Daily Tar Heel (UNC Chapel Hill's newspaper), then failing embarrassingly at a water filter demonstration for a blind elderly man. The lesson: don't wing it; prepare properly.

The chapter then introduces Tuyet Nguyet, a Vietnamese investigative journalist who transitioned to art collection. In 1970, she co-founded Arts of Asia magazine with husband Stephen Markbreiter. Over 40 years, she amassed 100 ancient gold rings from Southeast Asia, considered "the best of their kind in existence." Twelve rings plus a gold betel nut box from her collection now reside in the treasure. The narrative covers Hindu and Buddhist gold symbolism (Brahma's golden embryo, the lotus flower, the blue lotus as "path to enlightenment where secrets are revealed"), the Khmer Empire (Angkor -- "the largest city in the world," size of Los Angeles, over 2,000 temples), and the Champa maritime culture.

Key details include: "golden lions guarded golden bridge" (describing Khmer imagery), "rings like ours that survive today were discovered mostly in hidden caches, secreted away within temple walls," and the chapter concludes with direct advice to hunters -- "Don't rush through these pages," "Get to know these stories," "Consider their content," "Don't dismiss any details." Seth Gould is introduced as the man who "with metal and fire, forged each of our five treasure boxes" over "eighteen months."

## Confidence Assessment
