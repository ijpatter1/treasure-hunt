# Narrowing Analysis: Three Lines of Evidence Point to Zone C (Marker 320)

## Summary

After two expeditions (Feb 14-15, 2026), the search was broadened from a single corridor (317-320) to four zones spanning the Hollow Rock Trail loop. This analysis synthesizes three independent evidence lines that converge on Zone C (markers 319-320), with the Hopi calendar potentially narrowing to marker 320 specifically.

**Key field insight:** The granite gneiss dome sounds hollow when tapped throughout the summit. "Hollow Rock" describes the *geology* of the entire summit, NOT a single identifiable feature. The "find the Hollow Rock" strategy is eliminated.

## 1. The Stanza Ordering Argument

The poem's stanza order vs. the trail's marker order reveals that Stanza 5 (the treasure stanza) and Stanza 6 (the campsite stanza) describe *different locations* — and the order is reversed relative to a clockwise trail walk.

### The Evidence

**Stanza 5** contains ALL treasure-finding language:
- "where few have seen it"
- "go down"
- "the haiku curls a little further on"

**Stanza 6** describes the 314-315 campsite:
- "stay to see the night" (official campsite exists at 314-315)
- "flame of starlight" (ABSOLUTELY NO FIRES at this campsite)

### The Reversal

On the clockwise trail, the campsite (314-315) comes **BEFORE** the curve area (317-318). But in the poem, Stanza 5 (the curve/treasure stanza) comes **BEFORE** Stanza 6 (the campsite stanza).

This reversal indicates the poem describes a **return journey**: find the treasure past the curve (Zone C), then retrace back past the campsite (Zone A).

### The Directional Clue

"A little further on" from the 317-318 curve points **forward** (clockwise) toward markers 319-320 — Zone C. The poem tells you to go past the curve, find the treasure, then describes the campsite you'd pass on the way back.

### Counter-Arguments

- **Stanzas might not be sequential waypoints.** Possible, but the campsite validation in Stanza 6 proves that at least SOME stanzas map to real locations. If Stanza 6 maps to 314-315, treating Stanza 5 as a separate physical location is reasonable.
- **Could the campsite proximity elevate Zone A instead?** The campsite is where you stay, not where you search. Stanza 5 is the treasure stanza and it describes the curve area, not the campsite. Stanza 6 describes what you do *after* finding the treasure.

**Confidence: MODERATE-HIGH.** The stanza reversal is clean and the campsite validation grounds the argument in an official park document.

## 3. The Hopi Calendar Encoding (Ch 8, p. 85)

Chapter 8 features a Hopi ceremonial calendar diagram on page 85. Five of the twelve Hopi ceremonial months have ceremonies whose vocabulary connects directly to Joy's Serenade.

### The Five Connected Months

| Month | # | Ceremony | Poem Connection |
|-------|---|----------|-----------------|
| January | 1 | Winter Social Dances | "Dancers on the land" |
| February | 2 | Bean Dance | Loloma = Snake Priest in Bean Dance; Ch 8 = Stanza 6 in chapter mapping |
| April | 4 | Plaza Dances/Footraces | Spring search season; "warm" |
| May | 5 | Plaza Dances/Footraces | Butterflies active; "dancers" |
| August | 8 | Snake/Flute Ceremonies | "Sing harmonies"; snake = "go down" |

### The Math

**Product:** 1 x 2 x 4 x 5 x 8 = **320**

**Sum:** 1 + 2 + 4 + 5 + 8 = 20 --> 300 + 20 = **320**

Both operations yield marker 320.

### Why These Five Months

The months *excluded* (3, 6, 7, 9, 10, 11, 12) have ceremonies that do NOT connect to poem vocabulary:
- Month 3: Women's Society ceremonies
- Month 6: Men's Initiations
- Month 7: Home Dance (Niman)
- Month 9-12: Soyal, various winter ceremonies

The selection was made by identifying ceremonies with vocabulary that appears in the poem — not by choosing months that produce a desired number first and justifying connections afterward.

### Confirmation Bias Risk

Chapter 8 itself explicitly warns: "Confirmation bias is a treasure hunter's most dreaded foe." This encoding carries bias risk:
- The month selection was based on poem-vocabulary matching, which could involve circular reasoning
- The author may not have intended mathematical operations on month numbers
- The "300 + 20" step in the sum interpretation adds an assumption

However, the fact that both product AND sum yield 320 through independent operations reduces the probability of pure coincidence.

**Confidence: MODERATE.** The math is clean but requires month selection choices. The double-operation convergence (product and sum both yielding 320) strengthens the argument.

## 5. Hollow Rock Clarification

### Field Observation (Feb 15, 2026)

The granite gneiss dome sounds **hollow when tapped** throughout the summit area. This is a geological property of the exfoliated granite formation, not a characteristic of a single rock.

### Implication

"Hollow Rock" is a geological description of the summit, not a single identifiable formation to find. The strategy of "finding the Hollow Rock" is **eliminated**.

### Impact on Priorities

The "Hollow Rock Namesake Feature" priority (previously Priority 4 in the field checklist) is eliminated as a separate search task. The geological finding subsumes it.

## 6. Confirmation Bias Acknowledgment

Chapter 8 explicitly warns: "Confirmation bias is a treasure hunter's most dreaded foe." This analysis carries inherent bias risk:

### Specific Risks

1. **Stanza ordering assumption:** The argument assumes the stanzas are sequential waypoints describing physical locations in order. They could be thematic rather than geographic.

2. **Necklace counting direction:** The bead-to-marker mapping hasn't been tested in reverse. If the orange bead maps from the opposite direction, it could point to a different zone.

3. **Hopi calendar month selection:** The five months were chosen by matching ceremonial vocabulary to poem text. This creates a circular reasoning risk — the selector (poem vocabulary) is also the validator.

4. **Convergence could be coincidental:** Three independent methods pointing to the same zone is notable, but "Zone C" is also the area with the most prominent geological features (the large granite clearing), which naturally attracts analytical attention.

### Mitigating Factors

- The three evidence lines use genuinely different source material (poem structure, Ch 23 image, Ch 8 diagram)
- The stanza ordering argument gained strength from the Feb 15 campsite discovery — an externally verifiable fact, not an interpretation
- The necklace theory's 13-bead / 13-marker correspondence is a structural observation, not vocabulary matching
- The macro-level Rocky Face hypothesis (318 acres, Alexander County, Hiddenite proximity) remains very strong regardless of which specific zone is correct

### Honest Assessment

Zone C is the strongest target, but the analysis should not create a tunnel vision that prevents thorough searching of other zones if Zone C fails to produce results.

3. **Look BEHIND every prominent rock** — the side facing AWAY from the trail approach. "Peering behind a rock to find a treasure box" (Ch 6, p. 69).

4. **Get LOW** — "lie on the ground to read" (Ch 9, p. 91). Look at ground level behind rocks. The treasure or a marker may only be visible from below standing height.

5. **Walk fully around each formation** — the hiding spot has "no discernible entrance" (Ch 9, p. 88). It won't be obvious from the trail approach. You must circumnavigate.

6. **Look for natural granite cavities** — the treasure is "secreted away within walls" (Ch 2, p. 30). Look for crevices and natural pockets in the granite where something could be deliberately placed.

7. **Time visit for mid-morning** — when mica sparkle is visible ("shimmering circles of gold"). Low-angle direct sunlight on granite surfaces may reveal which specific rock face to investigate.
