### Recontextualized Findings

**The Chivor Discovery as Treasure Hunt Template:**

The Restrepo story isn't just historical background—it's a **template for finding the treasure**:

1. **Single clue approach**: Restrepo had "just one solitary clue" (the vista). Joy's Serenade provides a similar focused approach—the haiku (3 lines + 18 syllables = 318) points to a specific marker.

2. **Vista = key to discovery**: Restrepo found the lost mine by locating the correct vantage point. Rocky Face Mountain summit provides exactly this—a specific elevated position with panoramic views of surrounding "pike" (peaks).

3. **Hidden for centuries, then found**: Chivor was "hidden for 200 years" until someone followed the clue. The author implies the treasure similarly awaits someone who follows the clues.

4. **"Between two jagged grey peaks"**: This description could parallel views FROM Rocky Face summit, where pointed granite peaks are visible in multiple directions.

**Sparkle vs. Shine Distinction:**

The author explicitly states "Emeralds don't sparkle. They **shine**." Meanwhile, the back cover haiku says "**sparkle** even as you pine." This distinction may be intentional:
- **Sparkle** = granite with mica (Rocky Face geology)
- **Shine** = emeralds (not the location indicator)

The back cover points to a **sparkling** location (granite outcrop), not a shining one.
