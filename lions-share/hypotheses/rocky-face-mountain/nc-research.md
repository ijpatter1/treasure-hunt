### 1. Rocky Face Mountain - COMPLETE DETAILS

**Location & Access:**
- **Address:** 3451 Rocky Face Church Road, Hiddenite, NC 28636
- **Coordinates:** 35.8847, -81.1228
- **Hours:** 8am-5pm daily
- **Phone:** 828-632-1093
- **Size:** 318 acres
- **Summit elevation:** ~1,800 ft

**History:**
- Rocky Face Quarry operated 1922 to early 1940s
- Rock type: Granite gneiss
- Train engine hauled granite on spur track to Hiddenite (~8 mi)
- Used for Army rappelling training in 1975
- Became unofficial dumping ground for decades
- Park opened in 2012 after land gifted to county
- Added to NC Natural Heritage Registry in 2003

**Geological Features:**
- Dome-shaped mass of granite
- Southern edge of Brushy Mountains
- 100-foot sheer cliff face at quarry
- Large expanses of exposed granite on trails
- Low Elevation Granitic Dome ecosystem (rare)

### 2. KEEVER'S ONION - CRITICAL BOTANICAL DISCOVERY

**Allium keeverae (Keever's Onion)**
- **Conservation Status:** G2 rank (IMPERILED - very rare)
- **Distribution:** Found ONLY in Alexander and Wilkes Counties, NC
- **Specific Origin:** Plants originated on Alexander County's Rocky Face Mountain
- **Habitat:** Calcitic seepages on granite domes
- **Bloom:** May, 15" tall clumps with **light pink flower clusters**
- **Cultivar:** 'Rocky Face' variety named after this specific location
- **Source:** Specimens shared by late plantsman Dr. Larry Mellichamp

**Significance:** This extremely rare plant is endemic to Rocky Face Mountain, making the location botanically unique and supporting the "flowers near treasure" clue. The **pink flowers** align with the book's "blue and pink" color reference.

**Source:** [Plant Delights Nursery](https://www.plantdelights.com/products/allium-keeverae-rocky-face)

### 10. BRUSHY MOUNTAINS GEOLOGY

**Overview:**
- 45-mile-long isolated "spur" of Blue Ridge Mountains
- 4-8 miles wide
- Divides Yadkin and Catawba rivers
- Highest peak: Pores Knob (2,680 ft) in Wilkes County

**Geology:**
- Inner Piedmont geologic province
- Metamorphic rocks: gneiss and schist
- Quartzite outcrops create dome-like summits
- Monadnocks (isolated erosion-resistant mountains)
- Once as high as Alps/Himalayas, eroded over millions of years

**Rocky Face Mountain:**
- Southern edge of Brushy Mountains
- Alexander County
- Granite gneiss dome

**Source:** [Wikipedia - Brushy Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brushy_Mountains_(North_Carolina))
