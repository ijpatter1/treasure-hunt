## 1. The one strong, VERIFIED new fact: Bakersville (Ch. 3)

Accurate source text, `pages/metadata/page-040-041.json` → p40 (verified verbatim):
> *"Meanwhile, about **6,000 nautical miles east of Japan and forty miles northeast of Asheville, North Carolina**, lies another small town tucked away into the mountains. This place is named **Bakersville**, and the people who live there enjoy scenic green mountains not too dissimilar to Hakone, Japan."*

And p41 (verbatim): *"Seth Gould is this blacksmith's name, and he spent more than a year designing and crafting the five treasure boxes…"* / *"I made sure he knew I'd grown up in North Carolina and that I had lots of family who lived not too far from him."*

Why this matters:
- **Bakersville is named outright, with a bearing** ("40 mi NE of Asheville") — a literal geographic reference, not a pun. It's the county seat of **Mitchell County**, gateway to **Roan Mountain**.
- It's tied to the treasure's **physical creation**: Seth Gould made **all five boxes, including the Lion's Share** (Ch. 3). The author also states he has **family living "not too far from" Bakersville**. → the box's maker *and* the author's family anchor to this town.
- The book gives Bakersville a **named-town spotlight** and **never does so for any place in Alexander County** (the spent Rocky Face area). That's the case for treating Mitchell County as a fresh region.
- The author explicitly likens Bakersville's **"scenic green mountains"** to Hakone, Japan (famous for mineral baths) — a small extra "nature/water" tint to the area, though not a specific site.

**Two association chains converge on this region:**
1. **Box → maker → place:** Lion's Share box → Seth Gould → **Bakersville, Mitchell Co.** (the strongest kind of link — the physical container, not a gem's foreign origin).
2. **Birthstone/gem biography (Ch. 20 + Ch. 4):** January garnet + childhood public gem-digging → western-NC gem belt → **Spruce Pine / Emerald Village, Mitchell Co.** — the *other* great NC gem district, in the same county, **unsearched.**

> ⚠️ **The honest caveat the refuters kept raising:** the book's *most explicit* location pointers — Ch. 4 "only some miles up the road was the **Hiddenite** emerald mine" and Ch. 21 "Antiquities of **Alexander** / Make It Make Sense" — point back at **Alexander County** (= the spent Rocky Face area), **not** at Mitchell County. Bakersville is named only as the **box-maker's** town. So the Mitchell-County read trades the book's strongest *explicit* pointer (Alexander, already searched) for a strong *associative* one (Bakersville, new). That's the central bet — and its central risk.

## 3. The Roan extension (inference, not vetted)

- **"Much easier task"** (Ch. 4): Carver's Gap → Round Bald is ~0.7 mi, easy, roadside — a leisurely picnic walk.

**But:** the book **never names Roan, Carver's Gap, or the balds**; Roan is one of the **most heavily trafficked stretches of the entire AT** (strains "where few have seen it"); a 25-lb box on a famous open bald would be exposed; and it took **Hurricane Helene damage (Sep 2024)** — confirm access. Roan was **not validated/scored/refuted** by the workflow. Treat it as *one plausible spot in the Bakersville region*, not a vetted pin.

## 5. Honest recommendation & one-day plan

**If you travel, go to the Bakersville / Mitchell County region and run a falsification loop across 2–3 spots in a day** — don't bet the day on Roan alone:

1. **Roan balds via Carver's Gap** (~36.106, -82.110): walk the AT 0.7 mi onto Round Bald. Search the **wood-to-bald transition boulders** and **off-trail rock clusters** (the quiet spots hikers walk past), not the photographed crest. Peer **behind/under** freestanding rocks and at the base of any lone spruce. Weight rocks **near rhododendron/iris stands**.
2. **Roan High Bluff / Rhododendron Gardens** (Roan Mtn Day Use Area, ~2 mi further): the literal "pass by similar flowers" node — world's largest natural Catawba rhododendron garden + a freestanding-rock overlook. Run the same behind-a-rock sweep.
3. **A Bakersville-adjacent gem-belt/public spot** (e.g., a Spruce Pine / Blue Ridge Parkway overlook in Mitchell Co.): closest to the *named* town; quick terrain check of roadside boulders.

**Timing:** **mid-to-late June** (rhododendron in bloom validates the flower cue on arrival; also past Roan's snow/cloud season). **Do not dig** (not buried).

**Fast falsifier:** if the behind-a-rock + flowers signature comes up empty at the transition boulders + one overlook in ~3 hrs, the "open bald" reading is wrong — shift the *same day* to the lower Bakersville/gem-belt spots, and on the next pass reconsider whether the book's explicit **Alexander County** pointer (a *new, unsearched* overlook there, not Rocky Face) deserves priority over the Bakersville association.
