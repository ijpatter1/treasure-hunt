Lesson: Joy's Serenade & the Reader's Invitation

Front matter · book pages 1-17

Summary

The front matter (pages 1-17) frames the entire treasure hunt and lays out the author's ground rules. Page 1 is the copyright page: text copyright 2024 by Jon Collins-Black, cover and interior layout by Richard Ljoenes, most photography by Jamie River, and a notable credit that "SOME IMAGES WERE DONATED BY THE ARKENSTONE IN DALLAS, TEXAS." It was published by Treasure Books, Inc., distributed by Baker & Taylor, ISBN 979-8-9899831-3-5, LCCN 2024904217, printed in China; website www.treasureinside.com.

Page 2 is the dedication "TO KIMBERLY, AIDEN & LONDYN AND TREASURE HUNTERS EVERYWHERE," followed by a Saint Augustine epigraph: "Where your pleasure is, there is your treasure: where your treasure, there your heart; where your heart, there your happiness." Pages 3-4 are the Table of Contents, listing the Introduction (p.9), How To Read This Book (p.16), Chapters 1-23 with their titles, subtitles, and page numbers, and the back matter: Additional Treasure Items (p.199), Final Poem (p.205), Postscript (p.207), Acknowledgments (p.210). The TOC confirms there is NO explicit "Part One"/"Part Two" label printed, though the text refers to them.

Pages 8-15 are the Introduction. Page 8 is a full-page photo of a custom treasure box (gold/brass frame, black leather/velvet interior). Page 9 opens with the headline claim: "I'VE HIDDEN A TREASURE SOMEWHERE IN THE UNITED STATES THAT'S WORTH MILLIONS OF DOLLARS. AND IT'S WAITING FOR YOU TO FIND IT." The trove holds gold coins and bullion, precious metals and gems, plus dozens of remarkable, mostly rare, some one-of-a-kind items of historical significance. It names six historical figures who created or owned items in the treasure: "Picasso, Carnegie, Washington, Tiffany, Thoreau, and Onassis."

Page 10 explains the structure: the fortune was separated into five custom-made treasure boxes "secreted away" in "five different locations within the United States," spread out so at least one box might lie in "close proximity to you." Four boxes are similar in size, each worth comfortably in the six figures, and each has one dedicated chapter in Part Two. The fifth box is "substantially larger" and holds "the lion's share of our valuable trove"; it has NO dedicated chapter — its clues "may be found anywhere inside this book," including the Introduction, and "almost every chapter in Part One offers at least one important detail."

Page 11 is the author's motivation: he is drawn to joy "like the proverbial moth to the fiery flame," the project took "the better part of the last five years," and "an idea riding like a wild cowboy upon a lightning bolt of inspiration" sparked it. He concludes "we are all treasure hunters" and "Our lives are one perpetual treasure hunt."

Pages 12-13 give historical and process context. Page 12 lists treasure-hunt-driven history: Alexander the Great (Europe/Asia for 1000+ years), the disciples of Jesus Christ, Muhammad and the Muslim Middle East, the Spanish quest for riches in the Americas, the American Revolution, women's suffrage, and the civil rights movement. He argues "the fundamental treasure everyone of us seek are all the same" (love, food/water/shelter, control, purpose, belonging). Page 13 reveals he was a full-time dad to two toddlers during Covid writing children's books, with no collecting experience; acquiring items felt "almost frictionless," "as if an invisible hand was guiding my task." He enlisted "five researchers," and research "was like panning for gold," waded "out into a stream of time," turning over "tens of thousands of pages of documents." The crucial clue statement: "The objects in our treasure became my co-authors. Their stories created the structure of this book and provided the topics and themes of its chapters," and "some of the clues to finding the largest treasure box are found within the histories of treasure items themselves."

Pages 14-15 close the Introduction with the signature "Yours in the hunt, JON COLLINS-BLACK" and a red sidebar note "This is a picture of some of our treasure" beside a full-page treasure-spread photo (gold chest, coins, ingots, a visible "Atlanta 1996" Olympic medallion, jewelry, gemstones, basketball trading cards, belt buckle, a large gold nugget). Pages 16-17, "How To Read This Book," instruct the reader to read once through thoughtfully, note "anything that makes your Spidey sense tingle," let thoughts marinate, and "research it further" if a topic is captivating ("It's not outside the realm of possibility that was my exact intention"). He confirms there is no cipher: "I'm not a genius, so creating some complex cipher or grand secret code is beyond my scope." Finally he stresses physical search — "boots on the ground" — "Mother Nature beckons you," closing with "Take a friend. Bring a picnic. Find a treasure." and the Cesare Pavese epigraph "The only joy in the world is to begin."

The Treasure(s)

The front matter is where the prize itself is introduced. The headline object is the Lion's Share box — the substantially larger fifth box that contains the majority of the trove's value. Unlike the other four boxes, it has no dedicated chapter; its clues are scattered through the whole book, including the Introduction, with "almost every chapter in Part One" offering at least one important detail.

Two photographs anchor the section. Page 8 shows a custom-made gold/brass treasure box with a black leather/velvet interior. Pages 14-15 show a treasure-spread photo whose visible contents include gold coins and bullion, gold bars/ingots, a large gold nugget, jewelry, gemstones, basketball trading cards, a belt buckle, and an "Atlanta 1996" Olympic medallion — a concrete confirmation of items detailed in later chapters.

People

Jon Collins-Black — the author and creator of the hunt, who signs the Introduction "Yours in the hunt." He frames the project as a five-year effort sparked during the Covid era and tells the reader exactly how to approach the clues (see Themes below).

Saint Augustine — credited with the dedication-page epigraph on treasure, heart, and happiness. The triadic quote is a popular distillation echoing Matthew 6:21, used as a thematic anchor for the book.

Cesare Pavese — the Italian poet quoted on the closing page ("The only joy in the world is to begin"). The author chose him deliberately; per the "research it further" invitation, Pavese is a candidate worth a look, though his footprint is entirely Italian.

The Introduction also names six historical figures tied to treasure items, each the subject of a later chapter and each carrying potential "clues by association": Pablo Picasso (Ch. 22 pendant), Andrew Carnegie (Ch. 18 emerald), George Washington (Ch. 17 jelly glass), Louis Comfort Tiffany (Ch. 14 furnace), Henry David Thoreau (Ch. 14 fire), and Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis (Ch. 10 brooch). Alexander the Great leads the Introduction's history-of-treasure-hunting list and reappears in Ch. 21.

Production credits name Richard Ljoenes (cover and interior design) and Jamie River (most photography) — low-priority for location but noted for completeness.

Places & Geographies

The front matter itself names only one specific real-world US place outright, but the people it introduces open a wide map of associated US geographies (developed in Research Findings below). Surfacing every concrete US location found:

  • Dallas / Richardson, Texas — home of The Arkenstone fine-mineral gallery, the only specific US business named in the front matter (image donor and an actual source of treasure-box minerals).
  • Columbus, Ohio — where Arkenstone founder Rob Lavinsky's collecting career began.
  • Sweet Home Mine / Alma, Park County, Colorado (Mosquito Range, Pike-San Isabel National Forest) — The Arkenstone's signature US mineral locality; accessible public land, but a thematic association only.
  • Near Los Angeles, California — the author's confirmed current home; California is also tied to his earlier acting/music career.
  • Rocky Mountain Fenn-search corridor — New Mexico, Colorado, Wyoming, Montana — region the author personally explored; his "Forrest Fenn Box" is in one of these four states (explicitly NOT Yellowstone).
  • The Ozarks and the Appalachian Trail (Eastern US) — regions the author references in interviews relative to other boxes.
  • St. Augustine, Florida — oldest continuously inhabited European-established US settlement, named for the saint's feast day; public-land sites include Castillo de San Marcos National Monument, Fort Matanzas National Monument, and Anastasia State Park. A place-name coincidence, flagged for cross-referencing.
  • Beverly Hills, California and Washington, DC (Smithsonian) — Picasso jewelry provenance points (DC piece is by Paloma, not Pablo).
  • Carnegie / Pittsburgh / West Chester / Johnstown, Pennsylvania and Cumberland Island National Seashore, Georgia — Carnegie geography (the emerald itself is Colombian).
  • Mount Vernon & Woodlawn / Audley, Virginia — Washington jelly-glass provenance estates.
  • Corona (Queens), Laurel Hollow / Oyster Bay, and Green-Wood Cemetery (Brooklyn), New York; Winter Park, Florida (Morse Museum) — Tiffany geography.
  • Concord, Massachusetts — Fair Haven Bay, Walden Pond State Reservation, Walden Woods, Wright Woods: the "Thoreau's Fire" site, real accessible public/conservation land. Also Mount Katahdin / Baxter State Park, Maine and Cape Cod, Massachusetts.
  • Red Gate Farm, Aquinnah (Gay Head), Martha's Vineyard, Massachusetts — Jackie Onassis's former estate, now public conservation land near Moshup Trail and Squibnocket Pond. Also Newport RI, Hyannis Port MA, McLean VA, NYC, Boston, and Arlington National Cemetery VA.

Themes & the Lesson

"Joy's Serenade & the Reader's Invitation" is the reader's operating manual for the whole hunt. The author tells you HOW clues work: they are subtly placed (not obvious), spread across nearly every Part One chapter and even the Introduction for the Lion's Share box, and — critically — embedded "within the histories of treasure items themselves." He explicitly disclaims any cipher or grand secret code, so clues should be read literally and pursued by RESEARCHING the people, places, and objects each chapter raises ("research it further... that was my exact intention").

The Saint Augustine and Cesare Pavese epigraphs reinforce the thematic frame: treasure is where your heart and joy are, and the real value is in the act of beginning the hunt. The practical close — "boots on the ground," "within close proximity," "Mother Nature beckons" — confirms the prize sits in an accessible natural/outdoor US setting reached on foot. In short: read literally, chase the item histories, trust intuition, and expect a real, physically reachable place.

Research Findings

The Arkenstone, Dallas, Texas (Gallery of Fine Minerals)

The Arkenstone is a world-renowned fine-mineral and gem dealership founded by Dr. Robert (Rob) Lavinsky, who began collecting at age 12 and earned a Ph.D. in Biochemistry/Molecular Genetics at UC San Diego. He built one of the first online mineral marketplaces (TheArkenstone.com in 1994, renamed iRocks.com because buyers thought "Arkenstone" meant Arkansas). The company established its headquarters in the Dallas, TX area in 2002 and has expanded to roughly 20,000 sq ft, with a mailing address in Richardson, TX and a second office in Shanghai. The name is a Tolkien reference — the Arkenstone of Thrain, the "Heart of the Mountain," the great glowing gem and most coveted single treasure of the dwarven hoard beneath the Lonely Mountain.

Crucially, The Arkenstone did more than donate images: press releases and iRocks.com confirm it actually SOURCED several physical mineral/gem treasures placed in the five caches, including an emerald formerly owned by Andrew Carnegie (Ch. 18), a large octahedral diamond crystal, and a 100+ carat tanzanite. The pages reviewed do not state which box received which specimen, nor do they give mine-level localities for the book's pieces (tanzanite is Tanzanian; the Carnegie/Colombian-type emerald is South American — neither a US locality). The most defensible US geo-association is thematic, not the book's specific stones: the gallery is iconically linked to the Sweet Home Mine near Alma, Park County, Colorado, surrounded by accessible Pike-San Isabel National Forest land.

Jon Collins-Black

Jon Collins-Black is the author/creator of "There's Treasure Inside" (hunt launched Nov 12, 2024), in which he hid five treasure boxes (~$2M+ total), the largest being the "Lion's Share" (worth over $1M). The boxes were custom-forged by blacksmith Seth Gould over about a year. Per his own rules (corroborated by Business Insider), none are on private land, none are buried, and reaching them requires no dangerous acts. He is consistently described as a web/crypto entrepreneur (early Bitcoin adopter) and musician who currently lives near Los Angeles, California. Public profiles give no confirmed hometown and no Montana/Texas ranch — the "wild cowboy/lightning bolt" Western imagery appears to come from the book, not documented biography.

The single strongest geographic thread is his deep personal connection to Forrest Fenn's treasure hunt: he personally searched for Fenn's chest, wrote "An Ode to Forrest Fenn," paid ~$46,000 for the olive jar containing Fenn's autobiography, and created a "Forrest Fenn Box" placed somewhere in New Mexico, Colorado, Wyoming, or Montana — explicitly NOT in Yellowstone. He tells searchers not to rule out National Forests or National Parks, mentions taking 5+ trips to place boxes, references driving "past the Ozarks," and an "Appalachian Trail box" indicates an Eastern US placement for at least one box.

Cesare Pavese

Cesare Pavese (1908-1950) was an Italian poet, novelist, translator, and critic from Santo Stefano Belbo, Piedmont. He grew up between Turin and the rural Langhe hills, moved in antifascist circles, was sent to internal exile in Calabria, won Italy's Strega Prize in 1950, and died by suicide in a Turin hotel that August. The book's quote, "The only joy in the world is to begin," is widely attributed to him (likely from his diaries, "Il mestiere di vivere") but lacks a cited primary source in the materials reviewed.

Pavese's strongest non-Italian dimension is his deep tie to AMERICAN literature: he wrote his University of Turin thesis on Walt Whitman and became Italy's leading translator of Melville, Faulkner, Steinbeck, Dos Passos, Lewis, and Stein. He has no US birth/life/death connection. The only concrete US-physical association is the Cesare Pavese Collection at UMass Amherst's W.E.B. Du Bois Library — a weak, coincidental clustering in western Massachusetts near the Melville Arrowhead house in Pittsfield.

Saint Augustine

The dedication-page epigraph is attributed to Augustine of Hippo (354-430 AD), the early-Christian theologian and bishop in Roman North Africa, author of "Confessions" and "City of God." The wording is a popular distillation rather than a sourced passage, echoing Matthew 6:21 ("For where your treasure is, there will your heart be also"). The strongest concrete US angle is the place-name: St. Augustine, Florida — the oldest continuously inhabited European-established settlement in the contiguous US, founded September 8, 1565 by Pedro Menendez de Aviles, who named it for the saint's feast day (his ships sighted Florida on August 28). In and around that town sit public lands: Castillo de San Marcos National Monument (NPS), Fort Matanzas National Monument (NPS), and Anastasia State Park (the fort's coquina "King's Quarry"). No "Saint Augustine" mountain town was found outside Florida.

Pablo Picasso

Pablo Picasso (1881-1973) is the subject behind Chapter 22 ("Picasso's Pendant: A Love Story"). The Lion's-Share-relevant angle is his lesser-known jewelry — gold medallion/pendant series made with François (and later Pierre) Hugo at the Atelier Hugo in Aix-en-Provence, France, derived from his silver compotier designs and kept "as a private treasure." Two distinct US-located objects must not be conflated: (1) the Pablo/Hugo gold medallions, sold to the Feingarten Galleries in Beverly Hills, California and held by Gail Feingarten Oppenheimer until 2021; and (2) the "Picasso Kunzite Necklace" at the Smithsonian National Museum of Natural History in Washington, DC — which was designed by Picasso's DAUGHTER Paloma, not Pablo. Picasso's own jewelry-making geography (Vallauris, Antibes, Aix-en-Provence) is entirely in France.

Andrew Carnegie

Andrew Carnegie (1835-1919), the Scottish-American steel magnate and philanthropist, is tied to Chapter 18 ("Andrew Carnegie's Emerald," theme "The Science of Giving"). The treasure item is a Colombian emerald from Carnegie's collection, sourced via The Arkenstone — so the stone's geology yields no US clue; the value is in Carnegie's US geography. His footprint is unusually concentrated: the borough of Carnegie, PA (Andrew Carnegie Free Library & Music Hall, Carnegie Park); the Carnegie Museum of Natural History in Pittsburgh, whose Hillman Hall of Minerals and Gems displays emeralds; West Chester, PA (the Jefferis mineral collection he donated in 1904); the Johnstown Flood National Memorial, PA (NPS); his NYC mansion, now the Cooper Hewitt; and Cumberland Island National Seashore, Georgia (NPS) — the Carnegie family island with the Dungeness ruins, though access is pedestrian-ferry-only. He funded 1,679 US public libraries nationwide.

George Washington

George Washington is the namesake/owner of the jelly glass featured in Chapter 17 ("George Washington's Jelly Glass," subtitle "Share Your Story") — a small (~4 in.) cut-glass dessert vessel in the "English Rose" pattern, English/Irish-made circa 1790-1800, part of the Washington dessert service at Mount Vernon. It descended from Martha Washington via her will to granddaughter Eleanor "Nelly" Parke Custis Lewis and her husband Lawrence Lewis (Washington's nephew), then through later heirs and auction houses (Birch's Sons, Philadelphia 1891; Parke-Bernet, New York 1950) to M.S. Rau Antiques in New Orleans. Mount Vernon holds a comparable jelly glass (object W-522/E). The Lewises built Woodlawn Plantation near Mount Vernon and later moved to their Audley estate in Clarke County, Virginia — giving the glass two ownership-estate geographies beyond Mount Vernon itself.

Louis Comfort Tiffany

Louis Comfort Tiffany (1848-1933), American artist and designer and son of Tiffany & Co.'s founder, is the named maker tied to Chapter 14 ("Tiffany's Furnace & Thoreau's Fire"). His production geography centers on New York: in 1892/93 he built a glasshouse in Corona, Queens (initially the Stourbridge Glass Company, renamed Tiffany Furnaces in 1902) — the literal "furnace" of the chapter title. His 84-room estate Laurelton Hall stood on ~580-600 acres in Laurel Hollow (Oyster Bay), Long Island, overlooking Cold Spring Harbor; it burned in 1957, and its salvaged windows now anchor the Charles Hosmer Morse Museum of American Art in Winter Park, Florida. Tiffany is buried in Green-Wood Cemetery, Brooklyn (Section 65). These associations are overwhelmingly urban/private; the most "outdoor/public" candidate is the Laurel Hollow / Cold Spring Harbor shoreline, but it is a weak fit.

Henry David Thoreau

Henry David Thoreau (1817-1862), the Concord, Massachusetts naturalist and transcendentalist, is tied to Chapter 14's "Thoreau's Fire" episode. On April 30/May 1, 1844, Thoreau and Edward Sherman Hoar stopped to cook a chowder; their fire escaped on the east shore of Fair Haven Bay and burned an estimated 100-300 acres of Concord woodland, earning Thoreau the "woods burner" label. A common (speculative) reading holds this guilt helped push him toward Walden Pond a year later. His core geography is densely place-linked: Walden Pond, Fair Haven Bay/Fairhaven Hill, "the Cliffs," and the surrounding woods — much of it now public/conservation land (Walden Pond State Reservation, Walden Woods, Wright Woods). Beyond Concord, "The Maine Woods" recounts his 1846 attempt on Mount Katahdin (Baxter State Park), plus Cape Cod travels and an 1861 Minnesota trip.

Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis

Jacqueline Lee Bouvier Kennedy Onassis (1929-1994) is the named owner of the Chapter 10 item — an Art Deco bow brooch of carved rock-crystal panels framed by calibre-cut sapphires and old European-cut diamonds, in its original fitted red leather box from A la Vieille Russie (Fifth Avenue, NYC). It sold via Sotheby's in 2005 in "Property From The Kennedy Homes," so its provenance runs through her US residences. Her geography is rich: raised between Merrywood (McLean, VA) and Hammersmith Farm (Newport, RI); adult homes at 1040 Fifth Avenue (NYC), the Kennedy compound (Hyannis Port, MA), and Red Gate Farm on Martha's Vineyard; buried at Arlington National Cemetery, VA. Most notable for this hunt: after her death, Red Gate Farm in Aquinnah (Gay Head), Martha's Vineyard was acquired (2020-2021) by the Martha's Vineyard Land Bank and Sheriff's Meadow Foundation and converted into conservation land OPEN TO THE PUBLIC, between Moshup Trail and Squibnocket Pond.

Treasure-Hunt Signals

Signal The reader's operating manual is the single most important takeaway. The author states plainly that Lion's Share clues "may be found anywhere inside this book," that "almost every chapter in Part One offers at least one important detail," and — most actionably — that "some of the clues to finding the largest treasure box are found within the histories of treasure items themselves." Combined with his explicit denial of any cipher ("creating some complex cipher or grand secret code is beyond my scope"), this directs the search method: read literally, research the item histories, and chase the real US geographies they touch. This is solid guidance, not speculation.
Signal "Boots on the ground" + "Mother Nature beckons" confirms an outdoor, accessible US setting. The closing instructions ("Take a friend. Bring a picnic. Find a treasure.") reinforce the hard constraints we already hold: public land, near a road, not dangerous, reached on foot. Solid framing, not a coordinate.
Signal The Arkenstone (Dallas/Richardson, TX) is the only specific US business named — but as a hide pointer it is weak. The connection is genuine and load-bearing (it physically supplied treasure minerals, not just photos), yet the gallery is private urban property that violates the public-land rule, and the stones it supplied (tanzanite, Colombian emerald, diamond) are FOREIGN localities. The most defensible US association is thematic: the gallery's iconic link to the Sweet Home Mine near Alma, Park County, Colorado (accessible Pike-San Isabel NF). Treat Alma/Sweet Home as SPECULATION unless another chapter independently emphasizes rhodochrosite, Colorado, or the Mosquito Range.
Signal "Arkenstone" name = a deliberate mountain-treasure metaphor. Respecting the no-cipher rule, this is thematic flavor only: the Arkenstone is literally "the most precious gem at the heart of a mountain hoard," which resonates with the "Lion's Share" (largest/most valuable box) and a recurring mountain-treasure motif. Evocative, not a location.
Signal Author's familiar regions: the Rocky Mountain Fenn corridor (NM/CO/WY/MT) and an Eastern/Appalachian theme. His documented, first-hand exploration of the Fenn-search region is real geography he is comfortable in — but that connection is tied most directly to his separate "Forrest Fenn Box," so applying it to the Lion's Share is SPECULATION. The "Appalachian Trail box" reference confirms at least one Eastern placement, consistent with prior NC interest, without naming which box. Useful as candidate regions to cross-reference, not standalone hides.
Signal "Saint Augustine" → St. Augustine, Florida is a clean literal place-name hook, low-to-moderate confidence. The epigraph most plausibly functions thematically (treasure → heart → happiness), and the quote attribution is loose. But the place-name is a strong literal US geography hook and a one-box-per-state candidate for FLORIDA: Castillo de San Marcos NM, Fort Matanzas NM, and Anastasia State Park are all public land with road access and no dangerous terrain. Caveat: high-traffic NPS forts are surveilled; a quieter park edge (e.g., Anastasia's wooded/dune sections) is a better fit. Log it, but confirm another chapter points to Florida before investing.
Signal Jackie Onassis's Red Gate Farm (Aquinnah, Martha's Vineyard, MA) is the cleanest "clue by association" fit on this page. The named owner of the Chapter 10 brooch has a former estate that is now exactly the kind of accessible public conservation land the author favors — trails near Moshup Trail / Squibnocket Pond, near a road, no digging, no danger, in a single-box state. SPECULATION flag: this is associative, not textual; we have NOT confirmed the book points to Martha's Vineyard or that MA holds a box. Recommend checking Chapter 10 and any Joy/poem text for "vineyard," "Aquinnah," "Squibnocket," "Moshup," or "dunes/heath."
Signal Thoreau's Fire → Fair Haven Bay / Walden Pond State Reservation, Concord, MA is a strong, constraint-compliant public-land target. These are real, accessible US public lands directly tied to the "item history" of Chapter 14, fitting public-land/near-road/no-danger constraints. SPECULATION caveat: whether MA hosts a box is unconfirmed (prior failed search was NC). Mount Katahdin (Baxter State Park, ME) is geographically real but likely too strenuous to fit the "no cliff scaling" rule for a summit hide; lower Maine North Woods would fit better but is weak without corroboration.
Signal Carnegie and Washington point to real estates/parks but no pinpoint. Carnegie yields road-accessible Johnstown Flood National Memorial (PA) and ferry-only Cumberland Island National Seashore (GA — weaker on the "within ~3 miles of a road" framing). Washington yields Mount Vernon/Woodlawn (VA, mostly paid/historic sites, not federal public land). Both are association-level signals that could corroborate a PA, GA, or VA region if other chapters agree — not solves. Avoid building any letter-game from "Washington"; the value is literal place-names only.
Signal Picasso, Tiffany, and Pavese are mostly cautionary / low-value as US geography. Picasso's biography is French; his only real US provenance points (Beverly Hills CA; Smithsonian DC — and the DC piece is Paloma, not Pablo) are urban and fit the rules poorly. Tiffany's sites are urban/private (Queens factory, Long Island estate, Brooklyn cemetery, indoor FL museum). Pavese has zero US footprint; his only US thread is the American authors he translated (Whitman, Melville), which is thematic speculation. Do not manufacture motif-to-toponym mappings. For Picasso especially, read Chapter 22 directly for any literal place name before acting.
