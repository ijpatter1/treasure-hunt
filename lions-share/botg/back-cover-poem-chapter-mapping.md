# Back Cover Poem — Chapter-by-Chapter Mapping & Illustration Catalog

## The Haiku (Back Cover)

```
Be solid, have grit;          (5 syllables)
sparkle even as you pine.     (7 syllables)
Here lies a joy divined       (6 syllables)
```

**Total: 18 syllables = Marker 318**
No terminal punctuation after "divined" — noted as potentially intentional.

## Overall Assessment

The back cover poem functions as a compressed summary of the entire book's clue structure:

- **BE SOLID** → Rock/granite (Ch 21 solid gold oak wreath, physical rock at location)
- **HAVE GRIT** → Granite/determination (Ch 4 Hiddenite childhood, Ch 10 gritty fight)
- **SPARKLE** → Emeralds/gems (Ch 4 Chivor/Hiddenite, "emeralds don't sparkle, they shine")
- **EVEN AS YOU PINE** → Pine trees at location + longing/yearning for treasure
- **HERE LIES** → Resting place of treasure (Ch 14 "resting places...colors of nature")
- **A JOY DIVINED** → A treasure discovered (Ch 21 "joys we will ever divine", Joy's Serenade)

Every significant word in the haiku finds its strongest textual resonance in chapters connected to:
1. The Hiddenite/NC area (Ch 4, Ch 14, Ch 20)
2. Oak/nature symbolism (Ch 21)
3. The divine/transcendentalist outdoor spirit (Ch 14, Ch 21)
