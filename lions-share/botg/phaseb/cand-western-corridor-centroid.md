# Phase B — Discriminator-Search Dossier: Western-Corridor-Centroid Sub-Area

**Agent:** discriminator-search (NC sub-area = **western-corridor-centroid**).
**Date:** 2026-05-29.
**Sub-area definition:** The connecting corridor / approximate centroid of the tight western three
ledger anchors — the **Brushy Mountains foothills & Blue Ridge escarpment between Hiddenite,
Statesville, and Bakersville** (Pisgah NF units, NC state parks / natural areas, and game lands within
this band). Descriptive centroid of the three anchors (per geometry.md) = **~35.90 N, 81.38 W**, which
lands in the upper Catawba / Caldwell / Burke Piedmont-to-escarpment transition SE of Grandfather Mtn.

**Frame (inherited):** "Triangulation" is a THEORY. The four anchors bound a ZONE; they do not produce
a centroid X. This dossier looks INSIDE the corridor for accessible-public-land SPOTS where the
**most region-agnostic discriminators co-locate at ONE point**, and scores each honestly.

## Discriminator checklist (scored per spot)

Group P (terrain/flora) and Group Q (hard filters), restated as the co-location test:
1. **Sit-on / look-behind ROCK** at the spot (a notable boulder/ledge to sit on or look behind).
2. **WATER in view / nearby** ("magic in the water") — NOT crossed (no swift current / no ford).
3. **ELEVATION / VISTA that opens onto LOWER COUNTRY** (the Ch4 vantage-as-key landform — must look
   out over markedly lower ground, ideally Piedmont, with an open outlook).
4. **OAKS** / shady oak forest at the spot.
5. **BLUE + PINK spring wildflowers** plausible in that habitat.
6. **DARK-ish sky** (away from major urban glow).
7. **PUBLIC land** (accessible; not private; not inside a hazardous wilderness-cliff zone).
8. **Within ~3 mi of a road.**
9. **NOT buried, NOT dangerous** (no cliff scaling, no water crossing, no swift current, not underwater).
10. **Carryable / single-day-walkable** to the spot (box >25 lb must be portable to it).
11. **Snow in winter** (searchability filter — region with real winters).
12. **Bear + venomous-snake habitat plausible** (region-consistency, not a positive locator).

## CANDIDATE 1 — Chestnut Knob Overlook, South Mountains State Park (Burke Co.)  ← TOP CANDIDATE

- **Approx coords:** ~35.60 N, 81.62 W (Chestnut Knob overlook; Jacob Fork access trailhead 3001 South
  Mountain Park Ave, Connelly Springs). **BOX CAVEAT:** sits ~0.18 deg SOUTH of the corridor's stated
  south edge (35.78 N), i.e. **just OUTSIDE the strict western-three bounding box, ~12–14 mi S of the
  descriptive centroid.** geometry.md itself flags South Mountains SP as "just S of the corridor" but
  "very strong all-around discriminator match." Included — and now scored as the sub-area's TOP
  candidate — because the live data shows it is the **single best discriminator-stacking POINT** in or
  adjacent to the corridor, with the box-edge caveat kept explicit.
- **What's there (confidence: HIGH — live-confirmed):** South Mountains SP is NC's largest state park
  (~20,900 ac) of rugged monadnock mountains carved out of the Blue Ridge by erosion, on the southern
  Piedmont edge; highest point **Buzzard's Roost ~3,000 ft**, valley floor <1,200 ft along Jacob Fork.
  Classic "view opens onto lower country" monadnock looking SE onto the Piedmont. Chestnut Knob
  overlook is ~2,291 ft, reached via Chestnut Knob Trail (~2.3 mi from the Jacob Fork trailhead; ~900
  ft climb).
- **Discriminator co-location AT the Chestnut Knob overlook (live-confirmed where noted):**
  - Rock (sit-on/look-behind): **MET** — multiple sources describe a **"boulder outcropping"** and a
    **"rocky slab"** at the overlook that is "a great spot to take a break and enjoy the view." A
    discrete, sittable rock at the exact vantage. (AllTrails/Backpacker/LyonsRoar.)
  - Water in view: **MET (this is the correction)** — from the Chestnut Knob slab you can see
    **High Shoals Falls and the Jacob Fork River Gorge**. The earlier draft wrongly said the falls
    were out of view; live sources state the overlook view includes "High Shoals Falls, Jacob Fork
    Gorge, Raven Rock, Hugo Rock, and Crowders Mountain." So **water IS in the view from the same
    point as the vista.** (Note: seasonal — sources warn the falls can be "mostly obscured by trees in
    summer"; best leaf-off in spring, which also aligns with the spring-wildflower window.)
  - Vista onto lower country: **MET (strong)** — "grand views of the South Mountains and the
    Piedmont"; on a clear day **the Charlotte skyline** is visible — an unambiguous "view that opens
    onto lower country."
  - Oaks: **MET** — chestnut-oak / oak-hickory forest dominates (the name "Chestnut Knob"); park-wide
    oak/hickory/pine.
  - Blue+pink spring wildflowers: **PLAUSIBLE/MET** — rich southern-Appalachian-foothill flora;
    rhododendron/azalea (pink) + spring blue ephemerals plausible. **VERIFY exact blue species
    (bluets/dwarf iris/birdfoot violet).**
  - Dark sky: **MET (good)** — interior of NC's largest park, away from city glow (note Charlotte's
    distant dome is visible to the SE but the park interior is dark).
  - Public land: **MET** — NC State Park.
  - Within 3 mi of road: **MET** — Chestnut Knob overlook is ~2.3 mi by trail from the Jacob Fork
    paved trailhead/parking → within the ~3-mi-of-road filter. **VERIFY straight-line distance to the
    nearest drivable road is also <3 mi (likely, given park road geometry).**
  - Not dangerous / carryable / day-walkable: **MET** — established graded hiking trail (steep but no
    cliff scaling / no water crossing); easy single-day round trip.
  - Snow in winter: **MET** — gets winter snow.
  - Bear+snake habitat: **MET** — both present.
- **Why it now leads the sub-area:** with the corrected live data, Chestnut Knob is the ONLY point
  found that **co-locates the two flagship discriminators — water in view (High Shoals Falls/Jacob Fork
  Gorge) AND an open vista onto lower country (Piedmont/Charlotte) — PLUS a confirmed sit-on rock
  (boulder slab), oaks, dark interior sky, safe graded walk-in, and snow.** That is the deepest
  single-point stack in the sub-area.
- **Remaining gaps / caveats:**
  - **Box edge:** south of the strict 35.78 N corridor line (~12–14 mi). It is on-zone in *spirit*
    (the same Piedmont-edge monadnock family, adjacent to the corridor) but not inside the strict
    bounding box — flagged honestly, not hidden.
  - **CURRENT CLOSURES (Helene/storm):** High Shoals Falls Trail & waterfall viewing areas closed;
    Chestnut Knob Trail itself was listed "temporarily closed" by Burke County tourism. **VERIFY trail
    status before a trip.** This is a timing issue, not a permanent disqualifier.
  - Falls visibility is seasonal (leaf-off best) — favors a spring visit (also the wildflower window).
- **Discriminators MET (at the overlook point):** sit-on rock, water-in-view, vista-onto-lower-country,
  oaks, blue+pink flora (plausible), dark sky, public land, <3mi road, not-dangerous/carryable, snow,
  bear+snake. (11/12, with flora as the only "plausible-not-confirmed".)
- **Discriminators FAILED / caveated:** strict corridor box (south of 35.78 N); confirmed BLUE flora
  species pending; current storm closures (timing).
- **Public-land + 3-mi:** YES / YES.
- **Fit score: 78/100.** Raised from the earlier (erroneous) 62. The live data shows water + vista +
  rock co-locate at one accessible, safe, snowy, dark, oak-clad public point. Only real deductions: it
  sits just south of the strict box, blue-flora species unconfirmed, and current storm-closure timing.
  **Strongest single-point discriminator stack in the western-corridor-centroid sub-area.**

## CANDIDATE 2 — Wilson Creek Gorge area overlooks, Pisgah NF (Caldwell Co.)

- **Approx coords:** ~35.95 N, 81.71 W (Wilson Creek / Brown Mtn Beach Rd corridor; the designated
  Wild & Scenic river section). **This is INSIDE the corridor latitude band (~35.95 N) and longitude
  (~81.7 W) — squarely in the Caldwell-County Blue-Ridge-front part of the western corridor.** Strong
  on-zone fit geographically.
- **What's there (confidence: HIGH on existence, MED on best-point — VERIFY):** Wilson Creek is a
  designated **Wild & Scenic River** in Pisgah NF (Grandfather Ranger District), a road-accessible
  gorge with the paved/gravel **Brown Mountain Beach Rd / FR 981** running alongside, a visitor
  center, and multiple pull-offs and overlooks (e.g., the **Wilson Creek Gorge overlook**). Big
  granite boulders line the creek.
- **Discriminator co-location:**
  - Rock (sit-on/look-behind): **MET (strong)** — the gorge is full of large, sittable granite
    boulders and slabs; this is one of the best "notable rock" matches in the corridor.
  - Water in view: **MET (strong)** — the creek IS the feature; water in view everywhere.
    **CAVEAT (Group Q):** Wilson Creek has **swift current / rapids / waterfalls** in the gorge.
    "Water in view" is MET, but the "no swift current / not dangerous" filter pushes hard against
    spots AT the rapids. Favor a bench/overlook ABOVE the water, not a streambank in the whitewater.
  - Vista onto lower country: **WEAK/PARTIAL** — Wilson Creek is a GORGE; you are largely DOWN IN it.
    The dominant view is up-canyon / down-canyon and at the water, NOT an open vista that "opens onto
    lower country (Piedmont)." Some rim overlooks exist but the signature experience is in-gorge.
    **FAIL on the Ch4 "view opens onto lower country" semantics at the water-level points.**
  - Oaks: **MET** — oak-hardwood slopes above the gorge.
  - Blue+pink spring wildflowers: **PLAUSIBLE/MET** — rosebay/Catawba rhododendron (pink) abundant
    along the creek; spring ephemerals plausible. **VERIFY blue species.**
  - Dark sky: **MET (good)** — adjacent to the **Brown Mountain** area (famous dark NF country; the
    "Brown Mountain Lights"); low urban glow.
  - Public land: **MET** — Pisgah NF.
  - Within 3 mi of road: **MET (very strong)** — the road runs right along the creek; many points are
    within a few hundred feet of a road.
  - Not dangerous / carryable / day-walkable: **PARTIAL** — easy access, BUT the in-gorge whitewater /
    slick rock is exactly the "swift current / not dangerous" hazard the rules warn on. A box hidden
    at a roadside bench above the water would be fine; at the rapids it fails the hazard filter.
  - Snow in winter: **MET.**
  - Bear+snake habitat: **MET.**
- **The tension:** Wilson Creek MAXES water + rock + road-access + dark sky, but it is the **inverse**
  of the Ch4 vantage landform — you are inside a gorge looking at water, not on a height looking out
  onto lower country. It satisfies "magic in the water" superbly and fails the "view opens onto lower
  country" badly. Two of the strongest discriminators pull in opposite directions here.
- **Discriminators MET:** rock (strong), water-in-view (strong), oaks, blue+pink flora (plausible),
  dark sky, public land, <3mi road, snow, bear+snake.
- **Discriminators FAILED / strained:** vista-onto-lower-country (gorge, not vista), not-dangerous
  (swift water/whitewater at the water-level points — mitigable by choosing a bench above the water).
- **Public-land + 3-mi:** YES / YES (very strong).
- **Fit score: 64/100.** Best water+rock+road+dark-sky stack in the corridor, on-zone latitude — but
  the Ch4 vantage discriminator is essentially absent at the water, and the hazard filter complicates
  the water-level points. High on "magic in the water," low on the vantage.
