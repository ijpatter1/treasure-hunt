# Phase B Provenance-to-Ground: apple-asia-faberge cluster (NC-UNCONSTRAINED)

Agent task: trace each item's real-world US geography to a SPECIFIC, ACCESSIBLE US PUBLIC-LAND site
(made / found / displayed / memorialized / discovered) that could be a "clue by association" to the hide.
Hard gate: a qualifying tie must land on accessible public land within ~3 mi of a road. Private
collections, private/hazardous mines, and urban museum *buildings* do NOT qualify (context only).

Verdict up front: **0 qualifying public-land ties. reinforces-NC = no.** All three items are
foreign-origin objects whose only US footprints are private collections / urban museum buildings /
foreign auction houses. Per the agreed rules, item ORIGINS abroad are decoy/context, and a museum
building or private collection is not a hide site. Nothing in this cluster lands on accessible US
public land, and nothing points to North Carolina.

## 1. 120-carat sapphire (Mike Scott / Apple)

**Geography / provenance.**
- The item is associated with Michael "Mike" Scott (1945-2025), Apple Computer's first CEO
  (1977-1981), who became one of the world's foremost colored-gemstone collectors. The mineral
  *scottyite* is named for him.
- Scott's sapphires are overwhelmingly of **foreign origin** — documented stones in his collection
  come from **Sri Lanka (Ceylon)** and **Myanmar (Burma)** (e.g., a noted ~400 ct golden Sri Lankan
  sapphire, a 1,730 ct Burmese ruby crystal). No source identifies the specific 120 ct sapphire as
  US-mined; the weight of evidence is Asian origin. (Origin abroad = decoy/context per the rules.)
- US footprint of Scott's collection: it was **exhibited at the Bowers Museum, Santa Ana, CA**
  (world-class gem exhibit opened 2008) and elsewhere as a traveling/loan exhibit. That is an urban
  museum *building* in California — explicitly disqualified as a hide site, and on the wrong side of
  the country from NC.
- *Scottyite* type locality is the **Wessels Mine, Kalahari manganese fields, South Africa** — not
  US land at all.

**Lands on accessible US public land? NO.** Origin is Asian; the only US presence is a private
collection shown inside a California museum building. No accessible-public-land site.

**Distinctiveness:** Low as a geographic pointer — a private gem with a Silicon Valley owner and an
Asian mine origin. Highly distinctive as an *object*, but its geography routes to CA / Sri Lanka /
Myanmar, none of which is a public-land hide.

**Reinforces NC? NO — points elsewhere (CA / Asia).**
- Self-critical note checked: North Carolina *does* have real sapphire/corundum country (Cowee
  Valley, Macon Co.; Buck Creek / Corundum Knob, Clay Co.) and bills Franklin as a "gem capital."
  This is a tempting bridge, but it is a generic-association reach, not provenance: there is no
  evidence the Scott sapphire is NC-mined, and the NC corundum localities are mostly fee-dig
  commercial / private "gem mines," not free accessible public land. This does NOT clear the bar and
  must not be used to manufacture an X. (Same failure mode as the CA "Red Ledge" source-mine lead.)

## 2. Tuyet Nguyet — 100 gold rings (Arts of Asia)

**Geography / provenance.**
- Tuyet Nguyet (1934-2020), Vietnamese-born journalist, founded *Arts of Asia* magazine (1970) and
  was based in **Hong Kong** with her husband, English architect Stephen Markbreiter (designer of
  Hong Kong buildings incl. the Mandarin Oriental).
- Her ~100 gold rings are **ancient Asian** pieces (Java, Khmer/Angkor 9th c., Burma, Vietnam,
  Tibet, 1st-19th c.), assembled over ~40 years.
- US footprint: essentially **none on the ground.** The collection's public events were **auctions
  at Sotheby's Hong Kong** (e.g., "Golden Splendour," 2021) and scholarly coverage in *Arts of Asia*
  (Hong Kong magazine; July-Aug 2017 article by Susan Ollemans). The rings also appear in the Paris
  Musées / a Paris museum catalog context. No US public-land site is associated with making, finding,
  displaying, or memorializing these rings.

**Lands on accessible US public land? NO.** Everything is Hong Kong / Paris / ancient Asia.

**Distinctiveness:** As geography, irrelevant to the US — there is no US node to test.

**Reinforces NC? NO.** No US tie of any kind, public or private. Does not touch North Carolina.

## 3. Fabergé magnifying glass

**Geography / provenance.**
- A House of Fabergé object, **St. Petersburg, Russia**, ~1890. The well-documented exemplar (gold-
  mounted nephrite magnifying glass) is by workmaster **Michael Perkhin** (Fabergé's chief
  workmaster, also responsible for many Imperial eggs). Origin is wholly Russian.
- US footprint: Fabergé works live in US **museum buildings / private estates** — Virginia Museum of
  Fine Arts (Lillian Thomas Pratt collection, Richmond VA), Hillwood Estate (Marjorie Merriweather
  Post, Washington DC), Cleveland Museum of Art. These are urban museums / a private estate, all
  disqualified as hide sites; none is tied to *this* magnifying glass specifically, and the JCB item
  itself surfaced via a **Sotheby's auction** (2021), not a US public-land event.

**Lands on accessible US public land? NO.** Russian origin; US presence only inside museum
buildings / private estates / an auction house.

**Distinctiveness:** As a geographic pointer, low — a Russian luxury object with no US ground tie.

**Reinforces NC? NO.** No NC connection; nearest US Fabergé nodes are VA / DC / OH (all museum
buildings, none qualifying, none NC).

## Cluster conclusion

These three are textbook **foreign-origin decoy/context** items (Asia / Hong Kong / Russia). Their
only US manifestations are a private gem collection shown in a California museum, foreign auction
houses (Sotheby's HK / London), and East-Coast museum buildings/estates (VA, DC, OH) — every one of
which fails the accessible-public-land gate. **No qualifying ties; nothing reinforces the NC zone.**
The lone NC-shaped temptation (NC sapphire country for the Scott stone) is a generic association
without provenance and on fee-dig/private land, so it is explicitly rejected rather than promoted to
an anchor.
