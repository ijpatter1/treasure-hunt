## 1. The structural problem: Orange County fails the controlling discriminator by geography

The Ch4 landform — **"a view that opens onto lower country"** — requires high ground standing
directly over markedly lower ground with an open outlook (escarpment edge or a monadnock crest over a
distinctly lower floor). The Group P stack also wants real **elevation with a high vantage**, and
Group Q includes a **"gets winter snow"** searchability filter implying a region with real winters.

Orange County is **eastern Piedmont**, rolling, with very little local relief:
- General terrain ~400–700 ft elevation; broad, gentle, forested rolling country.
- It is the *low country* in the statewide gradient — there is nothing nearby for a vista to "open
  onto" that is markedly lower. A view from any Orange County high point looks out over more
  rolling forest at essentially the same elevation, not down an escarpment onto a distinct lower
  basin.
- Winter snow is light and infrequent in the eastern Piedmont vs. the western anchors' mountains
  (the snow filter weakly disfavors this sub-area).

This is exactly why `geometry.md` §3 says: "do NOT search Orange County's flat Piedmont on the
strength of a college reference — Orange County lacks the escarpment relief the Ch4 vantage
discriminator demands." The on-the-ground discriminator pass **confirms** that geometry conclusion;
it does not overturn it.

## 2. Candidate spots evaluated (best of a weak field)

I still tested the few public-land natural areas that exist in/near the sub-area, scoring each at a
single best point. All are accessible public land within ~3 mi of a road (the one filter the area
*does* pass), but they fail the relief/vista discriminators that matter.

### C1 — Occoneechee Mountain State Natural Area (Hillsborough, Orange County)
- **Approx coords:** ~36.07 N, 79.12 W (Overlook Trail area). NOTE this sits at ~36.07 N,
  slightly NORTH of the anchor latitude band (~35.78–36.01 N) used in geometry — a minor mismatch.
- **What it is (CONFIRMED via web):** The **highest point in Orange County** (~867 ft, 221-ac NC
  State Natural Area), a Piedmont **monadnock** that rises **~350 ft from the Eno River** just SW of
  Hillsborough; >3 mi of trails, a short **Overlook Trail** giving a view of the **town of
  Hillsborough** and surrounding forest from the top of a pre-Civil-War (abandoned ~1908)
  pyrophyllite **quarry**, plus two small fishing ponds. CAVEAT (confirmed): the true summit has a
  **cell tower and is county property technically NOT part of the park**, and the natural area is
  small and "hemmed in by private property" — so usable public ground is limited.
- **Discriminators MET (at the overlook point):**
  - Accessible PUBLIC land — YES (NC State Natural Area). [Group Q pass]
  - Within ~3 mi of a road — YES (parking lot at trailhead off Virginia Cates Rd / US-70). [pass]
  - Not buried / not underwater / carryable / single-day-walkable — YES (short trails). [pass]
  - Oaks — YES (oak-hickory + the rare hardpan/cedar communities; oaks present). [partial Group P]
  - Water in view (not crossed) — PARTIAL: the **Eno River** runs at the mountain's base and a
    **quarry pond** sits near the summit trail; "water in view" plausible at the right spot. [partial]
  - Notable sit-on / look-behind ROCK — PARTIAL: exposed quarry rock faces and outcrops exist, but
    these are cut/blasted quarry walls (often fenced/closed for safety), not a natural "sit-on/
    look-behind" boulder. The quarry cliffs also flirt with the "no cliff/ledge danger" Group Q
    filter. [weak/ambiguous]
- **Discriminators FAILED:**
  - Elevation/vista that opens onto lower country (the **controlling Ch4 landform**) — WEAK/FAIL.
    There IS a real Overlook Trail with a confirmed view of the town of Hillsborough and surrounding
    forest from atop the old quarry, and ~350 ft of relief above the Eno — the BEST vantage in the
    sub-area. But it "opens onto" a town + same-elevation rolling forest, NOT down an escarpment onto
    distinctly lower country; the relief (~350 ft) is an order of magnitude short of the western
    escarpment landform the Ch4 discriminator evokes. Closest Orange County gets, still a fail on the
    "markedly lower country" semantics. [Group P, decisive but not absolute fail]
  - Blue + pink spring wildflowers as a *notable* co-located feature — NOT distinctive here (typical
    Piedmont spring ephemerals exist but no signature blue+pink display comparable to the Roan/
    Catawba-rhododendron habitat the discriminator evokes). [fail/weak]
  - Dark sky — FAIL. Occoneechee sits immediately adjacent to **Hillsborough** and the I-85
    corridor, ~10 mi from Durham — light-polluted eastern-Triangle skies. [fail]
  - Winter snow — WEAK (light/infrequent eastern-Piedmont snow). [weak]
- **Public-land + 3-mi check:** PASS (public, road-accessible).
- **Fit score: 30/100.** Best-in-sub-area, but fails the controlling vista discriminator and dark-sky,
  and the "rock" is a hazardous quarry face rather than a natural sit-on rock.

### C2 — Eno River State Park (Few's Ford / Bobbitt Hole area; Orange–Durham line)
- **Approx coords:** ~36.07 N, 79.01 W (Few's Ford access).
- **What it is (CONFIRMED via web):** 4,319-ac NC state park straddling the Eno River across Orange
  & Durham counties, 24 mi of trails, oak-hickory bluffs, rocky river shoals. Multiple **named
  natural rock outcrops** confirmed: **Buzzard Rock** (large outcrop, Pea Creek Trail), **Gebel
  Rock** (outcrop high above the river, Laurel Bluffs Trail), the **Bobbitt Hole** outcrop ("stands
  sentinel" over an 18-ft-deep pool), and a rock outcrop overlooking the river's best rapids
  (Buckquarter Creek Trail). Cabe Lands Trail ends at a bluff overlooking the Eno.
- **Discriminators MET:** Public land (YES); within ~3 mi of road (YES — five access areas); oaks
  (YES, mature oak-hickory bluffs); **water in view, not crossed** (YES — the Eno River, shoals and
  the deep Bobbitt Hole are the centerpiece, viewable from bluff trails without crossing); **notable
  sit-on / look-behind ROCK** (YES, multiple confirmed NAMED natural outcrops — the area's *strongest*
  natural-rock match, materially better than Occoneechee's hazardous quarry faces).
- **Discriminators FAILED:** Vista that opens onto lower country (FAIL — river-bluff relief is only
  ~80–150 ft; you look across/down to the river, not out onto a lower *country*; no panoramic
  escarpment outlook); dark sky (FAIL — Durham/Hillsborough glow); blue+pink signature wildflower
  display (WEAK); winter snow (WEAK).
- **Public-land + 3-mi check:** PASS.
- **Fit score: 28/100.** Strong water + confirmed named natural rock (best in sub-area on those two),
  but no vantage/vista at all (it's a river valley, the opposite landform), light-polluted, no
  snow-region winters. The two strong discriminators do not rescue the missing controlling vista.

## 3. Honesty gate / bottom line for this sub-area

- **No spot in Chapel Hill / Orange County clears the discriminator bar.** The decisive failure is
  the **Ch4 "view that opens onto lower country"** landform: Orange County *is* the lower country in
  the statewide gradient, so there is no escarpment/monadnock-over-lower-basin vantage anywhere here.
  Dark-sky also fails across the sub-area (Triangle light dome). Winter snow is weak.
- **The strongest individual feature** in the sub-area is **water + natural rock at Eno River State
  Park** and the modest **highest-point monadnock at Occoneechee** — but neither co-locates the
  controlling vista, and Occoneechee's "rock" is a hazardous quarry face, not a natural sit-on rock.
- **Provenance gives this sub-area NO credit** (Jordan/UNC is biographical, no terrain hook).
- **Recommendation:** Treat `chapel-hill-orange` as **eliminated for pinpoint search**, consistent
  with `geometry.md`'s outlier ruling. It remains useful only as the **latitude consistency-check**
  (~35.9 N) that it always was. Search effort belongs in the western three anchors' Piedmont-to-
  escarpment corridor (Brushy Mountains / Blue Ridge front), where the vista/elevation/dark-sky/snow
  discriminators actually co-locate.

**Top fit score in sub-area: 30/100 (Occoneechee) — below any reasonable BOTG threshold.**
