Rob Lavinsky / The Arkenstone (iRocks.com) is a Dallas, TX–based **private** fine-mineral dealership; the specimen passed through a private commercial channel, not a public site.
- **Public-land tie:** **NO.** Source mine is in Colombia (foreign, private, hazardous-type commercial emerald mine). The US handler (Arkenstone) is a private business. Nothing here lands on accessible US public land.
- **The real NC hook is THEMATIC, not provenance:** Ch4 is titled "The 96 Carat Chivor Emerald" but the chapter's importance to the convergence engine is the **author's childhood emerald mine in Hiddenite / Alexander County, NC** (a ledger anchor), NOT the Colombian Chivor stone itself. The Chivor name is the decoy "famous foreign emerald"; the *emerald* theme is what points to Hiddenite. So this item REINFORCES the NC zone only by association with the chapter's Hiddenite anchor — but the Colombian provenance produces NO independent US public-land site.
- **Distinctiveness:** Low as a ground-truth pointer (foreign source). The emerald *theme* → Hiddenite is already captured as a ledger anchor.
- **Reinforces:** NC zone **thematically** (emerald → Hiddenite/Alexander Co), but provenance itself points abroad. Net qualifying public-land tie: 0.
- **Sources:** General gemological knowledge on Chivor (Boyacá, Colombia); The Arkenstone / iRocks.com (Rob Lavinsky, Dallas TX private dealer). Book Ch4 = "The 96 Carat Chivor Emerald," tied to author's Hiddenite childhood mine.
