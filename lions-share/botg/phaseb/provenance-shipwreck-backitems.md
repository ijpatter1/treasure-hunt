## 2. Octahedral Diamond (back-matter)

- **Geography:** A natural octahedron is the classic diamond crystal habit; the item itself is a loose natural diamond crystal. No single foreign source mine is uniquely implied.
- **US public land? YES (qualifying).** **Crater of Diamonds State Park**, Murfreesboro, Pike County, **Arkansas**, on Arkansas Hwy 301 — the ONLY diamond-producing site in the world open to the public on a finders-keepers basis. Octahedral crystals are among the forms found there. Accessible public land, directly on a state highway (within 3 mi of road). Famous finds: Uncle Sam (40.23 ct, largest US diamond), Star of Arkansas, Strawn-Wagner.
- **Distinctiveness:** High — it is literally the unique US public place where one can search for and keep diamonds, making "octahedral diamond -> public land" an unusually clean association.
- **Reinforces NC?** NO — points to **southwest Arkansas**. Unrelated to the NC zone.
- **Discriminator check:** Plowed 37.5-acre field atop an eroded lamproite volcanic pipe; low elevation, no high vantage, marginal/occasional snow. Weak match to the hide's terrain/flora discriminators. Counts as a real public-land tie but pulls away from NC.
- **Sources:** en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crater_of_Diamonds_State_Park ; arkansasstateparks.com/parks/crater-diamonds-state-park.
