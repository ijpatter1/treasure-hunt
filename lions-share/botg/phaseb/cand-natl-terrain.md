# Phase B Candidate Dossier — natl-terrain (NATIONAL / NC-UNCONSTRAINED)

**Agent role:** Anti-NC-bias check. Search ANYWHERE in the US for accessible public
land that distinctively satisfies the FULL discriminator stack — explicitly including
non-NC regions — to test (not just confirm) the NC #1 ledger ranking.

**Discriminator stack tested at each spot:** water at/near hide · shady oaks · sit-on /
look-behind rock · ELEVATION with a vista that "opens onto lower country" · blue + pink
spring wildflowers · dark sky · accessible PUBLIC land · ≤3 mi from a road · NOT buried ·
not dangerous (no water crossing / swift current / cliff scale) · gets winter snow ·
bear + venomous-snake habitat plausible · single-day-walkable carry of a >25 lb box.

**Tooling note:** WebSearch / WebFetch worked after the deferred-tool load; an earlier
batch also used `curl` against Wikipedia REST/summary APIs. Facts below are sourced.

## HEADLINE FINDING (the honest meta-result)

The terrain/flora discriminators — water + oaks + sit-rock + vista-onto-lower-country +
blue&pink spring bloom + dark sky + ≤3mi road + snowy winters + bear/snake — are **NOT
distinctive enough to single out one spot anywhere.** They describe, almost verbatim, the
*entire* temperate forested-upland belt of the eastern US: the southern/central
Appalachians (NC, VA, TN, WV, GA), the Cumberland Plateau (TN/KY), and the Ozarks (AR).
Every one of those regions has:

- spring-ephemeral **blue** Virginia bluebells (*Mertensia virginica*) + **blue** wild
  phlox (*Phlox divaricata*) alongside **pink** redbud — these species span "eastern
  North America," so the blue+pink spring palette is NOT an NC signature (VERIFIED);
- oak-hickory forest, sandstone/quartzite **bluffs and crags** that overhang valleys
  ("opens onto lower country"), free-flowing rivers/springs, black bear, and
  copperhead/timber-rattler habitat;
- real snowy winters (USDA zone 6–7).

So on terrain/flora alone, **no non-NC spot pinpoints, and no non-NC spot is
disqualified either.** The stack is a **pass/fail FILTER, not a magnet that points only
at NC** — and that is the core anti-bias takeaway: a terrain match found *inside* NC must
NOT be read as confirmation, because it would match equally in five other states.

### IMPORTANT CORRECTION (initial NC-bias claim reversed mid-research)

My first pass asserted "NC has ZERO certified dark-sky parks," which would have argued
against NC. **That is WRONG.** NC has a certified IDA International Dark Sky Park:
**Mayland Earth to Sky Park & Bare Dark Sky Observatory** — and it is in **MITCHELL
COUNTY, NC (Bakersville/Burnsville area)**, which is *exactly one of the four ledger
anchors* (Ch3 box-maker Seth Gould, Bakersville). So the "dark sky" discriminator, far
from cutting against NC, **co-locates with a ledger anchor in NC.** This is a genuine
convergence FOR the NC zone, not against it. (See darksky.org Mayland listing.)

The dark-sky criterion therefore does NOT favor non-NC regions; if anything it tightens
the NC Mitchell-County anchor. The honest national verdict stands on terrain/flora
*generality*, not on dark sky.

## TOP NON-NC CANDIDATES (best terrain-stack fits anywhere — anti-bias comparators)

### 1. Whitaker Point / Hawksbill Crag — Upper Buffalo Wilderness, Buffalo National River, AR
**Approx:** ~35.89 N, -93.43 W (Newton County, AR). Trailhead ~13 mi S of Ponca off AR-21
via gravel road.

The single cleanest *terrain-stack* embodiment I found anywhere, NC included.

- **Public land:** YES — Upper Buffalo Wilderness, Ozark–St. Francis National Forest
  (USFS, 12,108 ac). VERIFIED. (Note: it abuts the NPS Buffalo National River corridor.)
- **≤3 mi road:** YES — gravel road off AR-21; 3.0 mi round-trip, easy day hike. VERIFIED.
- **Vista onto lower country:** YES, textbook — the crag is a sandstone overhang
  extending ~30 ft, ~200+ ft above the Lower Fork of Whitaker Creek; overhangs the
  valley. VERIFIED. "Arkansas' most-photographed geological feature."
- **Sit-on / look-behind rock:** YES — Hawksbill Crag itself is *the* iconic rock; trail
  also passes "huge boulders." VERIFIED.
- **Water:** YES — Buffalo watershed; one of few undammed rivers in the lower 48 (135 mi
  free-flowing), spring-fed Ozark karst. "Magic in the water" fits. VERIFIED.
- **Oaks:** YES — Ozark oak-hickory; "towering oak and hickory" near the bluffs.
- **Blue + pink spring bloom:** YES — wild blue phlox documented as an Ozark spring
  signature; redbud (pink), bluebells, spring beauty along the Buffalo corridor Mar–May.
- **Dark sky:** YES — Buffalo National River = IDA International Dark Sky Park (2019);
  Bortle 2 sites in Searcy Co. VERIFIED. (Crag is in/adjacent to that dark-sky region.)
- **Snowy winters:** YES but LIGHT — Newton Co. averages only ~6–8 in/yr snow (well below
  US avg). Passes the "gets winter snow" filter, but it is the *weakest* snow of the set.
- **Bear / venomous snake:** YES — Buffalo NR has black bear + elk; timber rattlesnake +
  copperhead favor exactly these rocky bluffs/denning sites. VERIFIED.
- **DANGER caveat:** ⚠️ The crag is an unrailed clifftop — a fall is "several hundred
  feet and would likely be fatal." The *approach* is not dangerous, but a hide must be
  *near* the crag, not on its lip, to satisfy "not dangerous / no cliff scaling."

**Beats NC zone?** On raw terrain distinctiveness, EQUAL-to-better than the best NC spot
and the cleanest single "vista-onto-lower-country + iconic sit-rock + undammed water +
dark sky" combo anywhere. BUT it has **ZERO ledger support** (no in-text NC anchor points
to Arkansas). Loses decisively on the ledger; wins on terrain. This is the key anti-bias
datapoint: terrain does NOT uniquely favor NC.

### 2. Shenandoah NP / Blue Ridge, VA (Hawksbill, Bearfence, Stony Man, Whiteoak)
**Approx:** ~38.55 N, -78.36 W. Skyline Drive corridor, Page/Madison/Rappahannock Cos.

- **Vista onto lower country:** YES — *defining* feature: Blue Ridge crest with the
  Virginia Piedmont's rolling hills E and the Shenandoah Valley W; ~75 named overlooks
  directly off Skyline Drive, "many just steps from your car." VERIFIED.
- **Public land + ≤3 mi road:** YES — NPS land threaded by Skyline Drive.
- **Water / oaks / sit-rock:** YES — Whiteoak Canyon falls + many streams; oak-dominated
  forest; greenstone/granodiorite outcrops (Bearfence rock scramble, Stony Man) as
  sit-rocks. VERIFIED.
- **Blue + pink spring bloom:** YES, strongly — VA is the namesake of *Virginia bluebells*
  (sky-blue spring ephemeral, along the South Fork Shenandoah); wild blue phlox + redbud/
  dogwood. VERIFIED.
- **Dark sky:** YES — among the darkest skies on the East Coast; NPS-promoted stargazing.
  (Park itself not IDA-certified, but four VA *state* parks are.)
- **Snowy winters:** YES — Blue Ridge crest snows reliably (stronger than the Ozarks).
- **Bear / snake:** YES — one of the densest black-bear concentrations in the US; timber
  rattlesnake + copperhead present. VERIFIED.
- **Not dangerous:** YES for overlooks/trails; avoid the canyon-edge spots.

**Beats NC zone?** Terrain-equal to NC, but **no ledger anchor** points to Virginia.
Strong anti-bias comparator; not a lead.

### 3. Big South Fork NRRA + Pickett CCC State Park — Cumberland Plateau, TN/KY
**Approx:** ~36.5 N, -84.7 W. Scott/Fentress/Pickett Cos TN & McCreary Co KY.

- **Vista / sit-rock:** YES — sandstone bluff overlooks (East Rim, Angel Falls), arches,
  rockhouses overhanging gorges = vista-onto-lower-country + dramatic sit-rocks.
- **Water:** YES — free-flowing Big South Fork of the Cumberland; many creeks/springs.
- **Public land + ≤3 mi road:** YES — NPS recreation area + TN state park/forest (19,200
  ac), many overlooks roadside. VERIFIED.
- **Dark sky:** YES — Pickett CCC Memorial SP = IDA International Dark Sky Park (2015),
  adjacent to Big South Fork. VERIFIED.
- **Blue+pink bloom / oaks / snowy / bear+snake:** YES — Appalachian-plateau flora; black
  bears recolonizing; timber rattler + copperhead; mixed-oak forest; real plateau snow.

**Beats NC zone?** No — full stack + certified dark sky, but no ledger tie. Comparator.

### 4. Cohutta Wilderness, GA/TN (Chattahoochee NF; Grassy Mtn vista)
**Approx:** ~34.9 N, -84.6 W. Murray/Fannin/Gilmer Cos GA + Polk Co TN.

- **Public land + ≤3 mi road:** YES — largest USFS wilderness in the East (36,977 ac
  core, 100k+ ac complex); Grassy Mountain fire-tower trail is road-accessible.
- **Vista onto lower country:** YES — Cohuttas rise sharply ~3,000 ft from the Great
  Valley; fire-tower panorama over the wilderness + Fort Mountain. VERIFIED.
- **Water / oaks / blue+pink bloom:** YES — Jacks/Conasauga Rivers; oak-pine reclaiming
  forest; spring blue cohosh, dogwood (pink/white), flame azalea, lady's slippers. VERIFIED.
- **Bear / snake:** YES — black bear, copperhead, timber rattlesnake explicitly. VERIFIED.
- **Snowy winters:** YES — N. Georgia Blue Ridge gets winter snow at elevation.
- **Dark sky:** Not IDA-certified; rural-dark only.

**Beats NC zone?** No — full terrain stack but no certified dark sky and (critically)
it sits just S of the NC anchors, so it is "near-NC," not a truly independent national
alternative. Logged as a boundary comparator.

## VERDICT (return-level)

1. **The terrain/flora discriminators do NOT, on their own, select NC over the rest of
   the eastern uplands.** Buffalo River AR, Shenandoah VA, Big South Fork TN/KY, and
   Cohutta GA each match the FULL terrain stack at least as well as the best NC spot.
2. Therefore the stack is a **pass/fail FILTER, not a pointer.** A terrain match found
   inside NC is NOT confirmation — it would replicate in 5+ other states.
3. **BUT the one criterion I initially thought cut against NC — dark sky — actually
   co-locates with an NC ledger anchor:** the Mayland Earth to Sky IDA Dark Sky Park is
   in **Mitchell County (Bakersville anchor)**. So dark sky does not favor the non-NC
   comparators; it modestly *reinforces* the NC Mitchell-Co anchor.
4. **No non-NC spot should displace the NC zone** — none has ledger support. Buffalo
   River / Hawksbill Crag is the strongest *terrain* embodiment found anywhere and is the
   best evidence for staying humble about NC terrain matches, but it dead-ends on the
   ledger and is also the snow-weakest of the set.

**Honesty gate:** Nationally, no spot "clears the bar" as a stand-alone X on terrain
alone. The bar is genuinely cleared only where LEDGER + terrain (+ now dark sky) coincide
— i.e., inside the NC zone, especially Mitchell County. Stay region-level on terrain; let
the NC ledger anchors carry the localization. The anti-bias check did NOT find a non-NC
spot that beats the NC zone.
