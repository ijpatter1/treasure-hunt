## 4. Case FOR / Case AGAINST

### Case FOR (honest best argument)
- The book *does* literally contain the words "Midwest United States" (Ch3 p.44) — the only region whose tag is a verbatim phrase from the text.
- Several real US-internal provenance threads touch the Midwest (Ohio, Illinois, Missouri, Michigan, Iowa, Indiana), so the region is *mentioned* more than, say, a random state.
- The rocky fringes (Ozarks/Driftless) could physically satisfy sit-rock + vista + oaks + water, so the Midwest is not terrain-*disqualified* everywhere.

### Case AGAINST (why it is illusory)
- **The seed is self-disconfirming.** The single literal "Midwest United States" is explicitly the **3rd Faberge Egg that is NOT in our treasure** — JCB's own "look at the tiny detail" teaching story. Building a hide-region on it inverts the text.
- **No convergence — pure scatter.** The six Midwest fragments name six different states with no shared point. Convergence requires distinct sources landing on ONE place; here they land everywhere and nowhere.
- **No author biography.** JCB's validated life-geography is **NC** (Hiddenite/Alexander, Statesville/Iredell, Chapel Hill, family near Bakersville/Mitchell) and **CA** (LA, SW of Mount Wilson). **Zero** Midwest biographical anchor for the author.
- **All Midwest hits are non-hide provenance / 3rd-party bios** (Rob Lavinsky's Ohio, a non-treasure Illinois fluorite, Rudolph's St. Louis waypoint, exhibit-tour libraries, an archival photo copy at Purdue). None ties a *box* to the Midwest.
- **The Ohio bearing actively points away.** "400 miles northwest near Columbus" exists to locate the author's **NC** childhood region — it is evidence FOR NC, not Ohio.
- **Terrain anti-fit** on the most specific discriminators (sit-rock + vista).
- **No prior boots-on-ground** here, so it is not *refuted by search* — but absence of refutation is not support. Prior field effort was NC (Rocky Face / Bakersville).
