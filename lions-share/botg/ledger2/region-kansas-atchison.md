## 4. The case FOR and the case AGAINST

### Case FOR (honest best argument)
- Earhart is a **named, prominent treasure item** with a famous, specific, accessible US birthplace
  (Atchison — birthplace home + Earhart museum). "Go to the birthplace of the person behind the item" is a
  move some treasure designers use, and Atchison is a tidy boots-on-the-ground target.
- It passes several generic hard-filters/discriminators (water, snowy winters, public land within 3 mi,
  no dangerous water crossing required).
- Chapter subtitle "Explore More" is mood-consistent with an out-of-the-way pilgrimage.

### Case AGAINST (why it is illusory) — dominant
- **The book never says it.** Grep-confirmed zero mentions of Kansas/Atchison anywhere in the ledger.
  This is not even a lone *in-text* association; it is a fact imported from outside the book.
- **The Earhart channel actively points elsewhere.** Of all the geography the chapter DID name, none is
  Kansas; it spreads across Hawaii, Mexico City, DC, NJ, Indiana, Massachusetts, Canada, and Ireland —
  and the most evocative Earhart locus (the Pacific disappearance) is explicitly NOT a US hide.
- **Not the author's biography.** The RULING that elevates biography to a real channel applies to **JCB's
  own** geography (NC, Chapel Hill, CA), where distinct anchors converge. Earhart's birthplace is an item
  footnote, and item origins are generally region-agnostic/foreign-context — the hide is not where the
  person/object came from.
- **Terrain argues against.** Low-relief river country contradicts the strong Ch4 "vista between two peaks
  / plains below" discriminator and the Ch7 snow-capped-mountain illustration; probable bear mismatch.
- **Seeded-prior bias only.** Atchison is in the set solely because it was seeded. Nothing in the graded
  triage independently elevated it (it scores no region-pointing hits).
- **No prior boots-on-ground here** (the documented BOTG failure is Rocky Face, NC). So Atchison is
  "un-refuted" only trivially — nobody went because no evidence sent them — not because evidence supports it.
