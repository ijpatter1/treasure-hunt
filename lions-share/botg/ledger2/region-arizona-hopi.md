That visual density is seductive — but **every one of those names flows from ONE underlying source: Charles Loloma (and his great-niece Verma Nequatewa "Sonwai"), the Hopi jeweler(s) whose pieces are in the treasure.** It is a single item-provenance/biography thread (ledger Group I). Under the strict independence-grouping rule, a swarm of names from one source = **one** convergence point, not many. There is **no second, separately-sourced anchor** pointing to Arizona. So despite looking like the book's most "Arizona-heavy" chapter, the convergence count is **1**, and it carries real discriminator problems (desert, not water+oaks) plus a likely access hard-filter problem (sovereign tribal land ≠ public land).

## 4. The case FOR and the case AGAINST

### Case FOR (honest best argument)
1. **It is the densest US-placename concentration in the book.** Ch8 names six-plus specific Arizona places (Oraibi, Hotevilla, Scottsdale, Canyon de Chelly, Grand Canyon/Tutuveni, the mesas) — far more than any single other chapter. A reader could argue JCB chose a Hopi-jeweler chapter *precisely* to seed a place, and that "almost every chapter has a morsel" means this morsel is Arizona.
2. **Real terrain-method texture.** Two lines carry genuine hide-grammar that recurs elsewhere in the book: Drye's "edge of Hopi ... you see the vastness" (the Ch4 "find the vista, the hide is not far" pattern) and Sonwai's "stones on the mesas ... bird's eye view" (the Ch19/Ch20 perspective-from-above motif, and the Group-P "sat upon a rock" token). The dark-sky / starlight match (Poem st.6) is excellent.
3. **The chapter subtitle is "Confirmation Bias"** and explicitly warns the hunter against fixation — a meta-cue some read as a wink that this chapter matters.

### Case AGAINST (why it is illusory)
1. **Textbook lone-association: one source, many names.** Every Arizona name is the Loloma/Sonwai item-provenance/biography thread (Group I). The independence guard collapses them to ONE point. A pile of names from a single source is *not* convergence — it is the exact failure mode the analysis was built to catch.
2. **It's the JEWELER's geography, not the author's, and not a stated hide.** The chapter is "curated by the history and stories of an item." Item/artist origin does not locate the US hide. JCB has **zero** personal biographical connection to Arizona (his life-geography is NC + CA).
3. **The same thread leaks across seven jurisdictions.** Group I also "points" to CA (Treasure Island/Alcatraz), NM (Gallup, Santa Fe), NYC (MoMA), NJ (Princeton), Paris, and the Netherlands. A pointer that aims everywhere aims nowhere.
4. **Discriminator failure on the most important tokens.** The strongest Group-P signal is water ("magic in the water"), plus shady oaks and blue-and-pink wildflowers — all of which the high desert fails. Vista/rock/dark-sky fit, but those are shared across the whole Colorado Plateau and don't select AZ.
5. **Probable public-land hard-filter conflict.** Hopi and Navajo lands are sovereign tribal territory, not "public land," and access is permit/guide-restricted — colliding with the Postscript's strict public-land/private-property rule. The mesa and Canyon de Chelly interiors are effectively off-limits to a casual "bring a picnic" search.
6. **The Butterfly-Dance bridge is extra-textual.** The only thing that elevates Hopi beyond "jeweler's hometown" is decoding the poem's "Dancers on the land" to the Hopi Butterfly Dance — but the repo's own assumptions.md admits that link is "not mentioned in the book," and the literal-only rule forbids the decode. Strip that out and Arizona is purely item-provenance.
7. **Prior boots-on-ground note:** No pin or BOTG attempt was ever aimed at Arizona/Hopi (prior field effort was NC/Rocky Face, Alexander Co — FAILED; strongest prior text lead was Bakersville/Mitchell Co NC). Arizona is unrefuted only because it was never seriously pursued as a hide region, not because it survived scrutiny.

## 5. Honest convergence verdict

**THIN — single-group, item-provenance, with active discriminator and access strikes against it.**

Arizona/Hopi *looks* like the book's strongest US-place chapter because of sheer name density, but that density is an illusion created by one source (the Loloma/Sonwai treasure jewelry). Independent pointing groups = **1**, and it is the weakest channel (item-provenance / artist-biography), not author biography or a stated hide direction. The region additionally **fails the water/oaks/wildflower half of Group P** and **likely fails the public-land hard filter** (sovereign tribal land). Its only genuine merits — sit-rock, vista, dark-sky — are region-agnostic discriminators shared with the entire Colorado Plateau. Recommend ranking **low**: it is a vivid item-provenance chapter, not a converged hide region. Do not commit field effort here unless a genuinely independent second anchor (e.g., something concrete in the children's book "Our Unbreakable Thread," Group S) surfaces for Arizona.
