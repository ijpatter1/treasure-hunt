## 3. DISCRIMINATOR FIT (region-agnostic terrain/flora/access — Group P/Q)

Western NC Blue Ridge / gem belt (Mitchell, Mitchell-adjacent Yancey/Avery, Alexander foothills). Inferences marked [inf].

- **Water (sapphires "found in streams ... smoothed by water"; poem "Begin where waters meet"):** STRONG fit. The NC Blue Ridge is dense with creeks, gem-bearing streams (Mitchell/Yancey is the heart of US gem mining), and confluences. [inf: many named "where waters meet" candidates.]
- **Oaks:** GOOD fit [inf] — oak-hickory and cove hardwood forests blanket the southern Appalachians.
- **Sit-rock / "peering behind a rock" / boulders:** GOOD fit [inf] — abundant granite/gneiss outcrops, balds, and sitting boulders throughout the Blue Ridge. Mitchell/Avery has Roan Highlands rock outcrops.
- **Elevation / vista / "view opens onto the plains/lower ground below" / "framed between two peaks":** STRONG fit. The Chivor "vantage-as-key" discriminator (Ch4) maps cleanly to Blue Ridge overlooks where the view drops away to the Piedmont/lower valleys — exactly the eastern escarpment edge near Bakersville/Roan and the foothills above Hiddenite/Alexander. The "framed between two jagged grey peaks" image has many Blue Ridge analogues [inf].
- **Blue & pink spring wildflowers:** GOOD fit [inf] — Catawba rhododendron (pink/purple), flame azalea, bluets/phlox bloom across the Roan/Mitchell highlands in late spring; the Roan rhododendron gardens are famous.
- **Dark sky:** MODERATE fit [inf] — rural Mitchell/Yancey is fairly dark, though not a designated Dark Sky park; less dark than the Western US deserts.
- **<=3 mi from a road, public land, not buried, no dangerous water:** GOOD fit [inf] — Pisgah NF, Roan/Appalachian Trail corridor, state natural areas, and gem-belt public parcels provide accessible public land within 3 mi of roads. No mandatory water crossings. Note: the Hiddenite emerald mine itself is a *commercial/private* operation — the public-land requirement pushes any NC pin to NF/state land, NOT the mine.
- **Snowy winters:** FITS the "bear/snake habitat + snowy winters" discriminator [inf] — the Blue Ridge gets real snow. BUT this cuts AGAINST the author's stated "I am not a fan of the cold" preference (Intro/Postscript). Western NC winters are cold and snowy; this is a genuine tension (see case-against).
- **Bear/snake habitat:** STRONG fit [inf] — black bear and timber rattlesnake/copperhead are native to the NC Blue Ridge.

**Discriminator verdict:** NC scores HIGH on water, vista/escarpment, rock, flora, oaks, public-land access, bear/snake. It scores POORLY on the warm-weather / "not a fan of the cold" preference and only moderately on dark-sky. The terrain fit is excellent, but terrain fit is region-AGNOSTIC by definition — it does not by itself select NC over the southern Appalachians generally, and several discriminators also fit the Western US.

## 4. The case FOR / the case AGAINST

### The case FOR (honest best argument)
- **Real, named, verifiable NC places appear in the book** — Bakersville and Hiddenite are explicit, not inferred. That is rare; most chapters give only item-provenance abroad.
- **Two-to-three genuinely distinct anchors converge on western/central NC:** the box-craftsman (Bakersville), the author's childhood gem-digging (Hiddenite/Alexander), and his college (Chapel Hill). Under the biography-counts ruling, that is real convergence, not a lone association.
- **The Chivor "vantage-as-key" discriminator (Ch4) is the book's most explicit hide-pattern teaching, and it sits inside the very chapter that names Hiddenite** — JCB pairs "find the overlook where the view opens onto the plains below; the prize is not far from the vista" with his NC childhood. The Blue Ridge eastern escarpment (Roan area down to the Brushy Mountains above Hiddenite) is a textbook "view opens onto the lower country below" landform. This is the strongest internal reason to take NC seriously: the method-teaching and a biographical anchor co-locate.
- **Discriminator stack (water/confluence, sit-rock, rhododendron pink + bluet blue wildflowers, oaks, bear/snake, accessible public NF land within 3 mi of road) all physically fit** the southern Appalachian Blue Ridge.

### The case AGAINST (why it might be illusory)
- **Lone-association risk on the strongest anchor.** Bakersville's ONLY connection to the treasure is that the boxes were *made* there — there is no statement that anything was *hidden* near Bakersville. A craftsman's town is provenance, not a hide pointer. Author deliberately seeded "interesting backstory" places; Bakersville may be exactly such a decorative-but-true detail. Counting it as a *hide* convergence point is the central inflation risk.
- **Biography-only weakness.** NC-2 (childhood) and NC-3 (Chapel Hill) are pure biography. Per the ruling, biography counts — but biography points to where the author *is from*, which a careful hider might deliberately AVOID for the hide (or deliberately use — undecidable). It is suggestive, not dispositive.
- **Generic terrain.** The discriminator fit is excellent but NON-discriminating among regions: the southern Appalachians broadly (NC/TN/GA/VA) and even parts of the Western US satisfy "vista onto lower ground," "creeks," "oaks," "rock," "bear/snake." Terrain cannot pick NC; it can only fail to exclude it.
- **The cold preference cuts against NC.** "I am not a fan of the cold" (Intro + Postscript). Western NC has genuinely cold, snowy winters. This is a real, author-stated preference that mildly DISFAVORS the snowy Blue Ridge versus warmer regions (CA, AZ).
- **Already partially refuted on the ground.** Persistent project memory records **Rocky Face Mountain (Alexander County, the Hiddenite anchor's recreational area) was BOOTS-ON-GROUND SEARCHED and FAILED.** So the NC-2 childhood anchor has already produced one negative pin. Bakersville/Mitchell has NOT yet been searched. This means part of the NC convergence is spent: the Hiddenite/Alexander lead is weakened (not destroyed — the search may have looked in the wrong spot, but it is a negative data point).
- **Homonym false-positives must be excluded** (Alexander-the-Great in Ch21 is NOT Alexander County). Stripping such coincidences keeps the NC count honest at 3, not higher.
- **The competing strongest hits are elsewhere.** The author's CURRENT home base (Los Angeles/California) and the most-repeated independent geography in the book (CA: Apple/Bay Area, Gold Rush American River, Mt-Wilson orbit, Treasure Island) form a competing convergence; the "not a fan of cold" preference favors CA over NC.

## 5. HONEST CONVERGENCE VERDICT

**MODERATE.**

Rationale: NC clears the bar for genuine multi-anchor convergence (3 distinct sources: Bakersville-box-craftsman, Hiddenite/childhood, Chapel Hill/college) and the terrain/discriminator fit is excellent — and uniquely, the book's clearest hide-method teaching (Chivor vantage-as-key) is embedded in the same chapter as a NC biographical anchor. That keeps NC well above "thin." But it is held back from "strong" by three honest deductions: (a) the single strongest named anchor (Bakersville) is craftsman-PROVENANCE, not a stated hide locale (lone-association risk); (b) two of three anchors are biography-only and one of those (Alexander/Hiddenite via Rocky Face Mtn) has ALREADY been searched and FAILED on the ground; (c) the author's explicit "not a fan of the cold" preference mildly disfavors the snowy Blue Ridge. Bakersville/Mitchell County remains the un-refuted, highest-quality NC sub-lead and the rational next-trip candidate if NC is pursued; Hiddenite/Alexander is downgraded by the prior negative pin.
