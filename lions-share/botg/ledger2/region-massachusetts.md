All sourcing is from Chapter 14 (Tiffany's Furnace & Thoreau's Fire), with one decoy/attribution touch in Ch4 (Robert Frost) and one explicitly-rejected Emerson touch in Ch6.

## 1. Pointing signals, organized by independence-group

### Group MA-1 — Thoreau journal-pages provenance → Concord / Walden Pond / Fairhaven Bay
**Channel: item-provenance (single underlying source).**

This is the ONLY genuine region-pointing source for Massachusetts. Every Concord/Walden/Fairhaven row in the ledger flows from the SAME fact: two original pages of Henry David Thoreau's 1851 journal are in the treasure, and Ch14 narrates their Concord/Walden context. Per the independence rule, these collapse to ONE point — they are repeated framing of a single item's provenance, not separately-sourced anchors.

Constituent rows (all Ch14 p.127–129):
- p.127: *"Thoreau wrote Walden as a summary of the two years he spent in a small cabin he constructed on Emerson's property next to Walden Pond in Concord, Massachusetts."* — region-pointing, graded **strong** in triage (concrete placename).
- p.127: *"On April 30th, 1844, Thoreau went fishing with a friend in Fairhaven Bay."* — Fairhaven Bay, Concord MA. (moderate)
- p.127: *"the largest forest fire ever recorded in Concord" / "Over three hundred acres of woodland burned."* — Concord woodland fire (moderate).
- p.127 terrain color tied to the same story: dry spring, low lake levels, meadow of tall dry grass beside the lake and trees, "a matchbook he'd bought at the local store," "flames had burned through the field to the nearest growth of trees." (discriminator/weak — these describe 1844 Fairhaven Bay, not a directive.)
- p.129: *"Two original pages of Henry David Thoreau's journal are included in our treasure" / "written in 1851."* — the item itself; the reason all the above appears.

**Quality of the group: MODERATE as a placename, but provenance-only.** The placename is concrete and explicit (rare — most item-origins in the book are abroad and the hide is in the US). BUT it is item-history, the SAME class as "Tiffany Iris vase → Met in NYC" or "gold nugget → American River CA": context for the object, not a stated hide directive. The triage author graded the strongest single row "strong" on placename concreteness, but flagged the role as item-provenance, and the raw-ledger grader rated every Concord row only **weak** precisely because it is item-context. I side with the raw grader: as a HIDE pointer this is weak-to-moderate.

## 3. Discriminator fit (Group P/Q region-agnostic terrain/flora/access)

Concord, MA is genuinely a strong terrain MATCH for the discriminator profile — but note that a good discriminator fit does NOT add convergence points; it only tells us a region is *compatible*, not *pointed-to*.

| Discriminator (from poem st.4–7, Ch14, postscript) | Concord/Walden/Fairhaven fit |
|---|---|
| **Water at/near site** ("magic in the water") | EXCELLENT — Walden Pond, Fairhaven Bay (a widening of the Sudbury/Concord River), Concord River. Quintessential water feature. |
| **Shady oaks** ("you may find shady oaks") | GOOD — eastern MA oak-hickory & oak-pine woods; oaks abundant around Walden. (inference) |
| **Sit-rock** ("sat upon a rock") | MODERATE — glacial erratics common in New England; Walden has the famous cairn but that is man-made. Natural boulders present. (inference) |
| **Elevation / vista / "go down…how high"** ("how high you ever want to be") | WEAK — Concord is low, gentle relief (~150–300 ft). No real "go down to know how high," no framed-between-peaks vista, no "Plains-of-the-Llanos-below" overlook (the strong Ch4 vantage discriminator). This is the worst-fitting discriminator for MA. |
| **Blue & pink spring wildflowers** (Ch14 irises "surround each box") | MODERATE — blue flag iris (native, blue) grows in MA wetlands; New England spring has blue (lupine, iris) and pink (mountain laurel, lady's slipper) blooms. Plausible but generic — this discriminator fits almost every temperate-US region. (inference) |
| **Dark sky / "flame of starlight"** | POOR — Concord is in the dense Boston metro light dome; the Milky Way is not visible the way Ch19's rural-childhood line describes. A meaningful argument AGAINST. (inference) |
| **<=3 mi from road, public land, not buried** (hard-filter) | EXCELLENT — Walden Pond State Reservation is public land, heavily roaded, trails everywhere, casual picnic access. Easily satisfies all hard-filters. |
| **Snowy winters** ("don't search in winter…snow") | GOOD — New England has snowy winters; consistent with the postscript caution. (inference) |
| **Bear / poisonous-snake habitat** (postscript) | WEAK — black bears are scarce in eastern MA/Concord; copperheads/timber rattlers are rare and localized in MA. The postscript's bear-spray/venomous-snake framing fits the southern Appalachians far better than suburban Concord. A mild argument AGAINST. (inference) |

**Net discriminator read:** Concord nails *water + oaks + easy public access + snowy winters*, but FAILS the *elevation/vista*, *dark sky*, and *bear/snake* discriminators — three of which the book emphasizes (the Ch4 vantage-onto-plains discriminator is one of the strongest in the whole book; the dark-sky and bear/snake cues are explicit). The terrain is compatible-but-flat and light-polluted, which actively argues against it.

## 4. The case FOR and the case AGAINST

### Case FOR (honest best argument)
- The placename is **explicit and unusually concrete**: "Walden Pond, in Concord, Massachusetts" and the named "Fairhaven Bay." Most item-origins in the book are foreign (Sri Lanka, Cambodia, Colombia, Russia, Egypt) — a clearly-named *US* item-history place stands out, and the How-To-Read instruction says to research captivating item histories further.
- Ch14 spends real page-space on the Concord landscape (the 1844 Fairhaven Bay fire, the meadow/lake/forest-edge), more topographic detail than a bare provenance line would require — arguably "subtly placed" terrain.
- Walden is the literary archetype of "solitude in nature next to water," and the author explicitly says *"No book embodied this concept more than Thoreau's Walden."* The water + oaks + secluded-pond profile genuinely matches the poem's water/oaks/seclusion tokens.
- Hard-filters are trivially satisfied: Walden Pond State Reservation is public, road-adjacent, picnic-friendly — exactly the "take a friend, bring a picnic" access ethos.

### Case AGAINST (why it is likely illusory)
- **Lone association, single channel.** This is the textbook anti-convergence failure mode: ONE item's provenance, repeated within one chapter, dressed up as multiple rows. Independent-group count = 1. No biography, no second item, no placename corroboration. The book has FIVE boxes hidden in five places; a single item-origin placename is at least as likely to point to one of the *other four* boxes (or to nothing — item origins are mostly decoy-abroad).
- **Provenance ≠ hide.** The Tiffany vase in the SAME chapter points to NYC (Met) and DC (White House) and Paris; if we let item-provenance pick the hide, Ch14 alone "points" to four different places. Provenance is the weakest pointer class for the hide, which is in the US but not necessarily where an object came from.
- **Biography points elsewhere.** The author's own life-geography — the validated strong channel — is NC (childhood Hiddenite/Alexander, parents' Statesville church, Chapel Hill, "I grew up in North Carolina") and CA (20+ years' residence, Mount Wilson to his NE). JCB has NO Massachusetts biographical tie. The thing that makes a region strong here is absent.
- **Discriminator failures on emphasized cues.** Flat relief (fails the strong "go down…how high"/framed-vista discriminator), Boston light-dome (fails "flame of starlight"/dark-sky), and minimal bear/venomous-snake presence (fails the postscript's wildlife framing) all point away from suburban eastern MA.
- **Decoy literary cluster.** Frost (Ch4) and Emerson (Ch6) both got flagged/rejected upstream as over-reads. A New-England-literary halo (Thoreau + Frost + Emerson) is exactly the kind of seductive-but-shallow theme the "Confirmation Bias" chapter (Ch9) warns against; converging on it *feels* literary but is association, not evidence.
- **No prior BOTG, but no corroboration either.** No physical search or pin attempt of Massachusetts is recorded in the repo (checked botg/). The only repo trace is one analytic note (`poem-chapter-connections.md`) mapping Walden imagery to poem tokens ("magic in the water," "shady oaks") — that is the discriminator-match observation, not independent pointing evidence, and it has never been promoted to a hypothesis or tested. So MA is neither refuted nor supported by fieldwork; it simply has not accumulated weight.

## 5. Honest convergence verdict

**THIN.**

Massachusetts rests on a single independent source (the Thoreau journal-pages item-provenance), and that source is the weakest pointer class (item-history; the hide is in the US). It has good *discriminator compatibility* on water/oaks/access but fails the emphasized elevation-vista, dark-sky, and bear/snake discriminators, and has **zero biographical convergence** — the channel that distinguishes the genuinely strong regions. The Frost and Emerson touches are rejected decoys, not added anchors. Concord is a literary-association magnet, which is reason for extra suspicion, not confidence. It is real (a concrete US placename) but uncorroborated and single-channel — clearly secondary to the multi-anchor North Carolina cluster.
