# Faith-Leap Creative Pass — lens: natl-faith-leap (track: WIDE)

The pure intuition / gestalt read. What is this book REALLY about, and where does
the *feeling* of it land? Each leap is a HYPOTHESIS, kept for coherence + convergence,
never asserted as truth. No ciphers — association/wordplay/psychology only.

---

## Leap 1 — The book is a LION hunting its own home: Lion's Share lives where the LION lives

**Leap:** Step all the way back. The biggest box is "the Lion's Share." JCB chose that
phrase for the *largest* prize — but a creative author also picks it because it lets him
literally HIDE A LION somewhere on the map and call it fair. If you wanted to bury the
king's portion under the king of beasts, you go to a place named for the lion. In the
western-NC corridor the convergence already brackets, the on-the-nose answer is
**Linville / Grandfather Mountain region (Avery/Burke Co, NC)** — but the purer "lion"
read is anywhere a *Lion* feature sits inside the bracketed lat band. The faith move:
the treasure's NAME is the X. He named the prize after the place, not the place after
the prize.

**Points to:** A "Lion"-named public feature inside the western-NC corridor — primary
candidate **Grandfather Mountain State Park / Linville area** (the profile of the ridge
is itself the book's "sleeping figure" motif); secondary, any Lion's Den / Lion Rock /
Leonard's-type microtoponym in the lat ~35.8–36.0 band. (To be enumerated with maps.)

**Why it coheres:** An author who builds a whole book on item-stories-as-clues would
absolutely love a self-referential trap: the answer is the title. "Lion's Share" is the
ONE box-name that is a place, not a description. Faith = trust the title.

**Resonates with:** the book's whole "hidden in plain sight / There's Treasure INSIDE"
self-referential spine; the Hiddenite wink (place-name = answer); Antiquities of
Alexander → Alexander Co (place-name = answer). Same author tic, three times.

## Leap 2 — "There's Treasure Inside" = inside a MOUNTAIN you can walk into; the gem-mine country

**Leap:** The title is a promise about *interiors*. JCB grew up around digging for gems;
the items are gems, gems, gems (sapphire, emerald, chivor emerald, rubies, birthstone,
Carnegie's emerald). The faith-read: the box is hidden in **gem-mine / "you can go
inside the earth" country** that is also a public, walkable attraction — not a hole HE
dug (rule: not buried), but a place that is ALREADY a known opening into the ground.
Western NC is the only emerald-producing region in North America; **Hiddenite (Emerald
Hollow Mine) and the Spruce Pine / Mitchell Co mineral district** are exactly "there's
treasure inside [this hill]."

**Points to:** **Emerald Hollow / Hiddenite, Alexander Co**, and the **Spruce Pine –
Bakersville mineral belt (Mitchell/Mitchell-Yancey)**. Public, walk-in, gem-rich; the
hide near (not in) such a site.

**Why it coheres:** Title as literal instruction. Five of the items are gemstones; the
ONE place in the eastern US where the public goes "inside" the earth for gems is this
exact corridor. Convergence: two of the four literal anchors (Hiddenite, Bakersville)
ARE gem towns.

**Resonates with:** Ch4 Chivor Emerald (Hiddenite/Alexander anchor), Ch18 Carnegie's
Emerald (Statesville/Iredell anchor), Ch3 puzzle-box/egg (Bakersville/Mitchell — Seth
Gould forged the boxes there), Ch20 birthstone, Ch1 sapphire. The gem-density of the
item list is itself the map's legend.

## Leap 3 — Faith (Ch5) + a place literally named for belief

**Leap:** Ch5 is "An Exercise in Faith" and the task says solving this needs FAITH.
Take it as a wink, not just a sermon. Where do you hide treasure that requires faith?
**A place named for faith.** NC's mountains are studded with church-camp / belief
toponyms; the cleanest is **Bakersville/Roan Mountain → the Catawba rhododendron
"gardens" on Roan**, often called an Eden, AND the broader "land of faith" camp
country. The boldest single hit: **Mount Mitchell** is named for Elisha Mitchell, who
*died proving his measurement was right* — the ultimate "exercise in faith / fail
forward / don't give up" martyr, and it's the highest peak east of the Mississippi
(a "Lion's Share" of elevation), public state park, gets winter snow.

**Points to:** **Mount Mitchell State Park (Yancey Co)** and the Black Mountains /
Roan highlands. Highest point E of Mississippi = the "largest" / king's portion;
named for a man whose story is faith-tested-to-death.

**Why it coheres:** "An Exercise in Faith" (Ch5) + "Don't Give Up" (Ch15) + "Fail
Forward" (Ch14) ALL describe Elisha Mitchell's saga. The Lion's Share is the *biggest*
box → hidden at the *biggest* mountain. Public, snowy, road-accessible (NC-128 to the
summit, well within 3 mi).

**Resonates with:** Mitchell Co/Bakersville anchor (box-forging), Ch5 faith, Ch14/15
persistence subtitles, the "biggest = highest" intuition for the biggest box.

Parkway overlook or DuPont/Pisgah-edge public land), NOT the cemetery itself (private-ish,
and a grave is the wrong vibe for a picnic hide).

**Why it coheres:** "Look HOMEWARD" literally names the book's emotional engine
