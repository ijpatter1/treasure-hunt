# Coherence Scrutiny — Lens: NC Terrain / Sensory "Spidey-Sense" Intuition

*Role: COHERENCE SCRUTINEER (charitable, not adversarial). A leap is KEPT even if unproven, unless it is (a) internally incoherent / self-contradictory, (b) built on a CIPHER (letter/number/syllable counting), or (c) breaks a HARD CONSTRAINT (private land / dangerous / buried / underwater / not in US / contradicted by known fact). For kept leaps: story-strength {vivid / solid / thin} + what-would-CONFIRM.*

**Cross-cutting notes that apply to the whole file:**
- **No ciphers anywhere.** Every leap here works by sensory association, landform-feel, author-psychology, or wordplay — all explicitly ADMITTED this pass. Nothing is cut on cipher grounds.
- **Poem-text check.** The load-bearing imagery (shady oaks, "sit upon a rock," "if you don't go down... how high you want to be," "gaze around at all that you can see," shimmering circles of gold, magic in the water, the sleeve "haiku") is corroborated by the clean `botg/stanza-6-7-chapter-connections.md` and standard published Joy's Serenade text. Several other repo poem files (`back-cover-poem-chapter-mapping.md`, `poem-chapter-connections.md`) are self-flagged as contaminated — the leaps do NOT depend on those, so no leap is cut for sourcing. The one phrase to watch is **"pike are rather all around"** (Leap 13): it appears as shared working-text in the myth-theme lens but its exact provenance is uncertain — flagged in that leap's what-would-confirm, not grounds to cut.
- **Hard-constraint posture.** Several leaps name waterfalls/shelves/ledges. None as written demands a cliff, water crossing, or scramble — each explicitly chooses the gentle/safe variant. Kept, with the safety choice noted as the BOTG verification step.

## Leap 1 — Escarpment "lip" feel resolves to the Brushy Mountains rim above Hiddenite
**KEEP.** Coherent: an isolated front range genuinely delivers the "lower country opens beneath you" sensation that an interior plateau cannot; ties to strongest name-anchor (Hiddenite). No cipher, no constraint break.
- **Story-strength: vivid.** The poem's effort→arrival→opening arc mapped onto a monadnock front-range is a clean, felt, non-arbitrary read; it also coincides with the convergence engine's #1 region.
- **What would confirm:** Identify a specific south/SE-facing public overlook on the Brushy rim (~35.93 N, 81.2 W) where the Piedmont visibly falls away AND a sit-rock + water are present within 3 mi of a road.

## Leap 2 — "The haiku curls a little further on" = follow water one bend PAST the obvious overlook
**KEEP.** Coherent association (haiku=short turns ↔ a meander; "further on" = one bend past where hunters stop). Honors hide-in-plain-sight psychology; constraint-friendly (low creek bend). No cipher.
- **Story-strength: solid.** The "soft redirect past the obvious" is a real JCB-style instinct and reconciles water-motif + "further on" without strain; slightly speculative because "curls" → creek-meander is one of several valid readings.
- **What would confirm:** On the chosen trail, locate the first overlook, then check the next downstream meander of the draining branch (Lower Little River / Rocky Creek headwaters) for an oak-shaded sit-rock.

## Leap 3 — "Blue + pink bloom" = Catawba rhododendron/laurel + redbud signature; a shady laurel-hell sit-rock
**KEEP.** Botanically coherent for the southern Blue Ridge; supplies a season (late spring) consistent with a snow-getting-but-reachable place; mossy laurel boulders = real sit-rocks. No cipher/constraint issue.
- **Story-strength: solid.** Strong ecological grounding; the only softness is that "blue" is read two ways at once (distant ridges + blue wildflowers), so it's an evocative cluster rather than a single pin.
- **What would confirm:** Find a north/cool-flank laurel-rhododendron pocket with a seating boulder near water on Rocky Face's north side OR the Mitchell/Yancey escarpment; verify bloom timing + winter-snow elevation.

## Leap 4 — "Dark sky" literal = pulls FEEL toward the darker high northern lip (Mitchell/Bakersville/Roan)
**KEEP.** Coherent: a romantic-about-wonder author (Ch19 Moon Rocks) plausibly prizes genuine dark sky; Mitchell/Roan is famously dark; couples with the Gould/Bakersville box-forging anchor (two threads, one sub-region). The leap itself flags Carvers Gap as too far/high and substitutes lower NC-261/226 overlooks — so it stays inside the 3-mi-of-road + not-dangerous constraints.
- **Story-strength: solid.** Genuine convergence (dark-sky + box-forging town); thinner only because "dark sky" could equally be nighttime mood rather than a location instruction.
- **What would confirm:** A road-near (<3 mi) public overlook off NC-261/NC-226 with measured/known dark-sky quality, a sit-rock, and an opening view — not a high exposed bald.

## Leap 5 — "Shady oaks" = oak-dominant dry rocky ridgecrest (chestnut/scarlet oak) with outcrops + views
**KEEP.** This is the leap's best move: it RESOLVES the apparent shade-vs-open-view tension via real oak-ridge ecology (oaks let light through, grow on outcrop crests with views). Rocky Face literally names the rock face. No cipher; outcrop nook = non-buried, carryable. Coherent and constraint-fit.
- **Story-strength: vivid.** Ecologically precise and self-consistent; turns a poem word ("oaks," not "forest") into a specific habitat that co-locates every required feature.
- **What would confirm:** A chestnut/scarlet-oak + bare-outcrop ridgecrest with a S/SE overlook on the Rocky Face ridgeline or Brushy crest toward Pores Knob, with a ledge-base nook sized for a ~25 lb box.

## Leap 6 — Pores Knob: name + "view opening" feeling converge on the Brushies' signature storied overlook
**KEEP.** Coherent: the most sentimentally "loved/storied" overlook in the Hiddenite orbit fits an author who rewards romance about a place's human story; arrival-and-opening poem "wants" the signature overlook. No cipher/constraint break.
- **Story-strength: solid.** Good convergence with Leaps 1 & 5 on the Brushy crest; slightly thinner because a long-storied overlook is also a heavily-trafficked spot (mild tension with a treasure staying unfound), and Pores Knob's exact public-access status should be checked.
- **What would confirm:** Confirm Pores Knob overlook ledges (~36.0 N, 81.1 W) are on accessible PUBLIC land within 3 mi of a road, with a sit-rock + water on the approach.

## Leap 7 — Sit-rock-by-water + view = a SAFE escarpment shelf-waterfall pairing
**KEEP.** Coherent sensory stack (water + shade + sit-rock + opening in one landform); "haiku curls over a ledge further on" is an elegant water read. The leap explicitly forces a LOW GENTLE cascade (no cliff/crossing) — so it self-polices the not-dangerous constraint rather than breaking it.
- **Story-strength: solid.** Maximally satisfies the poem's sensory bundle; the discipline of "gentle cascade only" is exactly the BOTG test, which keeps it honest.
- **What would confirm:** Identify a specific low, flat-sit-rock cascade with an opening view within 3 mi of NC-226/261 (or a Brushy/South Mtn drainage) that requires NO water crossing or ledge scramble.

## Leap 8 — "Lower country" word-feel points SOUTH to the South Mountains (Burke Co.) dramatic lowland overlooks

## Leap 13 — "Pike are rather all around" = encircled-by-peaked-hills sensation atop an open monadnock dome (Rocky Face)
**KEEP.** Coherent IF the line is genuine: "pike" (Lake-District = peaked hill) + "rather all around" = the real standing-sensation on an isolated open dome where distant peaks ring the horizon — a precise, falsifiable landform claim, and Rocky Face is the corridor's signature low granitic dome. Wordplay (pike=peak) is admitted, not a cipher. The Ch4 "two jagged grey peaks" tie-in strengthens it. Strong constraint fit (county park, road-near, snow).
- **Story-strength: vivid (conditional).** Converts an abstract word into a testable encircling-peak-ring sensation and stacks with the other dome reads — BUT it is the one leap whose poem source-line ("pike are rather all around") I could not confirm in the clean poem file; it appears only as shared working-text. If the line is a transcription artifact, the leap loses its anchor.
- **What would confirm:** (1) Verify "pike are rather all around" is genuine published poem text (check the book scan, not the contaminated mapping files); (2) stand on Rocky Face's open summit and confirm a near-360 ring of identifiable distant peaks (Grandfather/Table Rock/Blacks) is actually visible.

## Leap 14 — "If you DON'T GO DOWN... how HIGH" = descend slightly to a sub-summit shelf where the height is felt; box on that shelf
**KEEP.** Coherent: takes the line as a literal body-instruction (crest into oak shade, step DOWN to an open shelf where the view/height finally hits) and thereby resolves shade-vs-view AND "don't go down vs. how high" with one real maneuver — how forested-dome viewpoints actually work. Explicitly a GENTLE shelf (no cliff), so constraint-safe. No cipher.
- **Story-strength: vivid.** Elegant paradox-resolution that matches both real terrain behavior and JCB's "one step past the obvious" instinct; among the most precise, BOTG-actionable reads in the lens.
- **What would confirm:** A safe (non-ledge) exposed shelf/bench just below Rocky Face's wooded true summit (or a Brushy sub-crest) where the view opens and a sit-rock sits, reachable without a scramble, within 3 mi of a road.

## Leap 15 — "Shimmering circles of gold" + "magic in the water" = sun-dappled creek pool, oaks shading the bank; a picnic-able low water feature
**KEEP.** Coherent literal walking-sequence (sun-on-water → "get warm" → oak shade → sit-rock), all satisfiable at one low, gentle, picnic-able spot; matches JCB's "take a picnic / joy not suffering" ethos and Ch14 "colors of nature around each box." Maximally constraint-friendly (gentle water, no crossing). Re-points the Ch12 gold-pan imagery to NC sunlit water — a reinterpretation, not a cipher.
- **Story-strength: solid.** Reads the poem as a coherent micro-sequence and ties to documented Rocky Face creeks + oak woodland; slightly thin because "shimmering circles of gold" has a competing, also-coherent gold-rush reading (Ch12) that this leap consciously overrides.
- **What would confirm:** A documented road-near sunlit creek pool (Grindstone Trail crossing / Rocky Face Branch) with oak-shaded banks and a flat sit-rock, no crossing required, that midday-shimmers — confirm both water and oaks on the ground.
