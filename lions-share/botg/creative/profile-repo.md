# Jon Collins-Black: Author Profile (from the book itself)

**Purpose:** A psychological / biographical portrait of the author of *There's Treasure Inside*, built ONLY from what the book reveals (chapters/*.md, front/back matter, introduction, how-to-read, postscript, acknowledgements, Joy's Serenade). This is the CREATIVE pass: it is meant to help later agents *think like JCB* and abduce where he would hide the Lion's Share. Quotes are verbatim from the repo's verified transcriptions.

This file is HALF of a two-part profile. It focuses on: life-geography, values/personality, aesthetic & habits of mind, humor & delights, and his relationship to nature/the outdoors. Everything here is a hypothesis-input, not a proven fact about location.

---

## PART A — LIFE-GEOGRAPHY (where his soul-map is drawn)

This man's emotional center of gravity is **western North Carolina**, even though he has lived in **Los Angeles** for 20+ years. The book is, structurally, a Californian writing a love letter to a Carolina childhood. Geography is the single richest seam for "where would he hide the most personal box."

### Childhood — Northern Iredell County / Statesville, NC (the formative ground)
- Born ~1972–1973 (he was 9 when Jordan was a UNC freshman in 1981; "forty plus years ago" framing puts him at ~47+ at writing).
- Born **January** ("my January birthstone was garnet"). His birthstone shame is a recurring emotional motif (Ch 20).
- At **age 3**, his parents gifted him **20 acres of woodland**; his father then spent ~10 years **building a log home himself** on it (Ch 14). This is enormous for profiling: his idea of "home" is *self-built, on wooded acreage, in the country.*
- Grew up in the **countryside** where "I could see the Milky Way every night" — a thing he openly **misses** now living "near a large city" (Ch 19). Nostalgia for dark rural sky is a live nerve.
- As a boy he climbed onto the **roof of his parents' minivan** with a Christmas telescope to stargaze and "dream of touching the moon" (Ch 19). Forty years later he literally put a moon rock in the treasure — *he closes his own childhood loops*. This is a key behavioral tell: **he plants a wish in childhood and resolves it in the treasure.**
- At **age 7** he dug for crystals in a **"red dirt embankment"** near the house and only ever found **dark smokey quartz**, which he resented as "disappointment and unrewarded toil" (Ch 4, Ch 20) — *another loop he closes* by putting a magnificent cut smokey quartz in the trove decades later.

### The gem-country wink — Hiddenite / Alexander County, NC (genesis of the obsession)
- "Although I knew that **only some miles up the road was the Hiddenite emerald mine**…" (Ch 4). His love of gems and "treasure" was born here, next to a town literally named for a hidden green gem in a state with the most diverse gem geology in the East.
- For the CREATIVE pass this is a flag, not a coincidence: a **hidden-treasure author who grew up gem-digging beside a town named "Hidden-ite," writing a book called *There's Treasure Inside*.** He is the kind of mind that would savor exactly that pun. (See "Antiquities of ALEXANDER" / Alexander County in Ch 21 for a parallel name-wink.)

### Extended family — Bakersville / Mitchell County, NC (the high-mountain kin)
- He chose **Seth Gould**, a Bakersville blacksmith, to forge all five boxes, and pointedly told Seth "**I'd grown up in North Carolina and that I had lots of family who lived not too far from him**" (Ch 3). He routes the *physical vessels of the entire project* back through his family's mountain ground. The boxes themselves are Carolina-made.

### Faith roots — Statesville / Huntersville church (the moral spine)
- Parents **met in seminary** and **started a church in Statesville, NC** focused on service: homeless shelter, soup kitchen, battered-women's shelter, Habitat for Humanity; father on the boards of the local Food Bank and Red Cross (Ch 18). He was **required to volunteer** from a young age.
- Father was a **pastor** who **traded pulpits with local Black ministers**; Jon's first spiritual awakening was at **Rev. James Henderson's Black Baptist church** (Ch 6). Denomination is **Baptist** ("dry somber Baptist hymnals," Ch 9).

### College — UNC Chapel Hill / Orange County, NC
- Lifelong **Tar Heels** fan from age ~6, watched games with mom and dad; was 9 when Jordan was a UNC freshman and saw the 1982 title shot (Ch 13). The Chapel Hill / UNC identity is deep and tribal.

### Adult home — Los Angeles area (La Cañada / Pasadena corridor)
- "California for over 20 years" (Ch 12); now "near a large city" (Ch 19).
- "Mount Wilson Observatory… is located **not far from my home, just to the northeast**" (Ch 18) → places his home **southwest of Mt Wilson**, i.e. the La Cañada Flintridge / Pasadena / Glendale foothills.
- Walks a **"massive rose garden" of "several acres"** in a "local park" with Kimberly (Ch 20) — consistent with **Descanso Gardens** in La Cañada (a heritage-oak woodland park with a large rose garden). Present-day daily landscape: **foothill oak woodland + rose garden + observatory mountain.**

### Geographic synthesis for hypothesis-makers
- **Soul-geography = western NC Piedmont→Blue-Ridge escarpment** (Hiddenite/Alexander, Statesville/Iredell, Bakersville/Mitchell, Chapel Hill/Orange). The book's *personal* anecdotes cluster here; the California references are present-tense logistics, not romance.
- His landscape vocabulary, repeated unprompted: **woodland, red dirt, crystals, dark night sky, hills/peaks, oaks, rose garden, "behind a rock."** A hide that "feels like home" to him would be **wooded, rocky/gemmy, with a high open view of sky, oaks present, near family ground, leisurely-accessible.**

## PART B — VALUES & PERSONALITY (what he steers by)

### 1. Joy as a deliberate, hard-won discipline (his stated prime mover)
- "Like the proverbial **moth to the fiery flame, I am drawn to joy**. I simply want to experience as much joy as I can, and there is no greater high I get than when I am sharing joy with others" (Intro).
- Joy was NOT natural to him: for "much of my life… joy was a rather obscure concept… Joy existed somewhere on the other side of a dense wall, comprised of the stress and anxiety of my own relentless daily expectations." He "decided it was time to tear that barrier down and actively quest" for it — "through humor, music, writing, parenting, and spirituality." (Intro)
- The entire treasure hunt is, by his own account, "one continuous act of joy." He named the central clue poem **Joy's Serenade**; the back-matter epigraphs he chose are all joy quotes (Mother Teresa: "Profound joy of the heart is like a magnet that indicates the path of life"; Maya Angelou: "We need joy as we need air"). **Joy is his north star, and he says so repeatedly — a man who hides his treasure where joy is.**

### 2. Generosity as identity — "the science of giving" (Ch 18)
- Raised by service-driven pastor parents; required to volunteer. He frames the whole give-it-all-away project as natural to him, against his editor's protest that giving extravagant things away "goes against human nature."
- Reveres **Carnegie's** "Gospel of Wealth" ("The man who dies thus rich dies disgraced"). He explicitly wants finders to "**share their joy with others**." Giving is not a stunt for him; it's inherited theology.

### 3. Family-centered to the core (Kimberly, the kids, parents)
- Met **Kimberly in 2013**, started a family in **2017**, married only months before publishing (Ch 22). Children **Aiden and Londyn** (back matter). His love-story chapter (Picasso/Jacqueline) is openly autobiographical: persistence + devotion.
- Kimberly is his creative partner: they assembled the **3,000-year-old faience necklace together** during weekly **"creative nights"** (Ch 23). The treasure isn't just *for* his family — it's *made with* them. The dedication voice ("Thank you for seeing me, knowing me, and having me anyway") is tender and self-deprecating.
- He explicitly frames the hunt as something to do **"with a loved one,"** "take a friend, bring a picnic." His ideal finder is *not* a lone obsessive — it's two people on a nice day out. That tells you about the *kind of place* he'd choose: pleasant, shareable, picnic-able.

### 4. Faith / spirituality — broad, sincere, non-dogmatic
- Baptist upbringing, but his adult spirituality is **syncretic**: a **Sanskrit tattoo** on his left forearm — "I welcome the good with the bad, as they both come from the one true source" (Ch 10); **10 years of meditation** (Ch 6); reverence for MLK and Gandhi as childhood heroes (Ch 6).
- He devotes a chapter to **faith as method**: "Faith is akin to having an **ethereal treasure map** guiding each step of our way"; "**Trust in what you know. Have faith.**" (Ch 5). For the creative pass this is permission *from the author* to act on intuition.
- He sees "**an invisible hand… orchestrating each step**" of the project and wonders aloud about "**larger forces at play**" and "serendipity" (Intro). He is comfortable with mystery and meaning; he is not a cold rationalist — even though he warns hard against **confirmation bias** (Ch 8, 9) and insists there's **no cipher/code** (How to Read; Postscript).

### 5. Humility, vulnerability, self-deprecation
- "**I'm not a genius**, so creating some complex cipher or grand secret code is beyond my scope" (How to Read).
- He volunteers his **most embarrassing moment** (scoring a layup for the *other* team in his one junior-high appearance, Ch 13) and his childhood **money anxiety** / fear of failure (Ch 14) and a lean adult stretch when he'd "**grab a muffin or two in a grocery store… pretending to be a customer**" (Ch 12). He is unguarded; he likes being likable; he wants the reader on his side.

### 6. Underdog-love and persistence as worship
- His **favorite story in the book** is **Wilma Rudolph** (polio→3 golds), and he lingers on the **Nigerian 1996 underdog** team and Jordan being cut from varsity. He is moved by people who **don't give up** and who **defy expectations**. He repeatedly reassures the reader they *can* do this: "**I believe you can find it.**"

## PART C — AESTHETIC & HABITS OF MIND (how to read him)

### 1. Detail-obsession: "Joy Is in the Details" (Ch 3)
- Whole chapter built on a **Sherlock-Holmes magnifying glass** and a **Fabergé egg lost in plain sight for 87 years**, with quote "**With a keen eye for details, one truth prevails!**" His explicit instruction: "**noticing details will help you find our treasure.**" He prizes the small, the overlooked, the hidden-in-plain-sight.

### 2. Hidden-in-plain-sight is his core trick
- The Fabergé egg (Ch 3) and the unsigned-vs-signed Amelia photo (Ch 7) are both *the same object as everyone else has, but one tiny mark makes it the treasure.* He loves the idea that the special thing sits unremarked among ordinary ones. **Expect the Lion's Share to be near something common/overlooked, distinguished by a small detail.**

### 3. Wordplay and name-winks (NOT ciphers — associations)
- He confirms his own method: "if you find a topic… captivating… and your instinct is to go research it further, I invite you to do so. **It's not outside the realm of possibility that was my exact intention.**" (How to Read). This is the license for thematic/associative reading.
- Demonstrated winks: **Hiddenite** (hidden gem); **"Antiquities of Alexander"** ↔ Alexander County (Ch 21, his confirmed solve-guidance chapter "Make It Make Sense," which tells you to "**look beyond just ancient myths and legends and consider more**" — i.e. the myth is a vehicle, the *word* is the point); "**Golden lions guarded a golden bridge**" near the **Lion's Share** name (Ch 2); "**Treasure Island**" murals (Ch 8). He *delights* in a name that does double duty.
- Caveat he himself sets: **no letter/number/syllable counting, no code** (Postscript: "No code… take what I say here as literally as you possibly can"). Wordplay/association = yes; arithmetic cipher = no.

### 4. Puzzle-craft and craftsmanship as love language
- All five boxes are **handmade puzzle boxes by Seth Gould** requiring **7 actions** to open, instructions inside (Ch 3). He fell for **Hakone Japanese puzzle boxes** on a 2018 trip. He and Kimberly play **escape-room games and make puzzles** on creative nights (Ch 23). He admires master-makers across the book (Tiffany's 8 years to perfect glass; Art Smith; Loloma; Perkhin). **He respects slow, careful, made-by-hand work — and he made the hunt the same way.**

### 5. Perspective / aerial / "bird's-eye view" as a recurring key
- Ch 8 (Loloma/Sonwai flew over Hopi mesas — "**It's a bird's eye view**"), Ch 7 (Amelia's "From an Airplane" poem; the snow-mountain airplane illustration), Ch 19 (the astronaut **Overview Effect**), Ch 20 ("**Where you stand determines what you see**… a change in where you stand changes everything"). He returns again and again to **seeing a place from above / from a new vantage**. A high open viewpoint matters to him both emotionally (childhood roof + sky) and as method.

### 6. He closes childhood loops in the treasure (behavioral signature)
- Moon dream → moon rock. Resented smokey quartz → showpiece smokey quartz. Garnet birthstone shame → rare green tsavorite garnet. **He resolves personal childhood disappointments by placing their redeemed form in the trove.** If the *hide location* follows the same logic, it would be a place tied to a childhood ache redeemed — the red-dirt digging ground, the woodland, the dark-sky country.

### 7. Documentation / story-keeping
- Reveres **Thoreau's journals** and **Washington's papers**; urges finders to "**document your adventures… journal these**" and "**share your stories with one another**" (Ch 14, 17). He values the *record* of the journey, not just the prize.

## PART D — SENSE OF HUMOR & WHAT DELIGHTS HIM

- **Playful, casual, breaks the fourth wall:** "if you are eagerly waiting for me to **shut the hell up and get to the part about… clues**, then let's get to it!" (How to Read). "Thank you, Richard for making this book so **damn attractive**" (acknowledgements). He's warm, a little irreverent, never pompous.
- **Self-mocking:** "Maybe I've lost my mind. (**Maybe I have! But I don't think so.**)" (Intro); the wrong-basket layup; the Easter-song-at-Christmas toddler story (loudly singing "**Here Comes Peter Cottontail**" during his father's candlelight vigil, Ch 9). He enjoys telling stories where *he* is the comic figure.
- **Nerd-joy, openly owned:** "**I'm a bit of a nerd**" — Star Trek: TNG was his favorite show ages 16–22, **Picard his hero** (Ch 19). Mike Scott's Apple badge **#007** gets a James-Bond wink (Ch 1). Detective Conan's "one truth prevails" (Ch 3). He enjoys fandom, easter eggs, and clever little references.
- **Delighted by serendipity and coincidence:** the meteorite that landed **on a doghouse** (Ch 19); items appearing "**seemingly out of nowhere, the very next day**" as if by "**an invisible hand**" (Intro). He treats lucky alignment as magical and meaningful, not random.
- **Whimsical framing of the hunt:** "**Take a friend. Bring a picnic. Find a treasure. Now what could be better than that?**" His tone toward the hunt is light, generous, anti-grim. He wants finders **having fun**, not suffering.
- **Music delights him deeply:** Joy's Serenade is built on musical metaphor (note, tune, coda, conductor's baton, harmonies); Kimberly's favorite song "**Make Your Own Kind of Music**" (Cass Elliot) was the last song at their wedding and threads into the final chapter "**Sing Your Own Special Song**." Music = joy = the through-line.

## PART E — NATURE & THE OUTDOORS (his feelings, in his words)

This is the most location-relevant value cluster, so it gets its own section.

- He frames nature almost **devotionally**: "**Mother Nature beckons you. Don't ignore her. Go say hi.** I can think of no better reason to turn off your television, pocket your smart phone, and go breathe some of our planet's fresh air" (How to Read).
- He sells the hunt as a **sensory outdoor pleasure**: even failing leaves you with "**the delightful sights, smells, and sounds of nature**" and time "**spending time outdoors with a loved one**" (Ch 10). "This hunt is designed to bring you **joy, not suffering**."
- **Transcendentalist DNA:** his high-school favorite authors were **Emerson and Thoreau** (Ch 14); he keeps the Emerson line "**What lies behind us and what lies before us are tiny matters compared to what lies within us**" from childhood (Ch 6). Walden/woods/self-reliance/nature-as-teacher is baked in.
- **Explicit nature-color clue + reverence in one breath (Ch 14):** "I can't help but think of the **resting places of the boxes** I've hidden and the **colors of nature that surround each of them.** Perhaps you'll pass by **similar flowers** along your way." He *associates each hide with the natural colors around it* — he thinks of the boxes as nestled in specific natural palettes.
- **Landscape features he names as places to explore (Ch 7):** "**Go where others have not.** We never know what's past the **next tree line**, over the **next rise**, or around the **next corner**." Tree line / rise / corner = his mental image of where treasure waits.
- **"Peering behind a rock to find a treasure box"** (Ch 6) — his own offhand metaphor for the hiding method; rocks/boulders are in his mental picture of a hide.
- **Joy's Serenade nature imagery** (the Lion's Share poem): shady oaks, "magic in the water," elevation ("if you don't go down you may never know how high you ever want to be"), "the sky smiles on you," "flame of starlight," "sat upon a rock," "nature's wide embrace." His ideal hide-place, as he imagines it poetically: **elevated, oak-shaded, near water, with a big open sky, a sitting-rock, secluded ("where few have seen it") but embraced by nature.**
- **Dark sky longing** (Ch 19): he misses seeing the Milky Way; "flame of starlight" + "if you stay to see the night" in Joy's Serenade suggests a place with a **good night sky / stargazing quality** — emotionally precious to him.
- Hard real-world constraints he sets on the outdoor place (Postscript, etc.): public land, ≤3 miles from a road, not buried, not dangerous, no water-crossing/cliff/ledge, not underwater; **gets winter snow** ("do not search in winter… blanket of snow"); reachable and searchable **in a single day** by one person (LeElaine & Kimberly D. "how much ground one searcher can cover in a single day"); pleasant enough for a **picnic**. So: a **temperate, snow-getting, wooded, accessible, scenic, family-friendly public natural area** — which fits the western-NC mountains/foothills far better than the LA basin (LA gets essentially no snow at picnic elevations).

## PART F — DISTILLED "THINK LIKE JON" HEURISTICS (for downstream agents)

1. **Follow joy, not difficulty.** He'd pick a place that *delighted* him, that he'd happily picnic at — not a grim or extreme spot. Beauty and pleasantness are selection criteria.
2. **It's personal and probably nostalgic.** The Lion's Share is "the lion's share of [his] soul"; he closes childhood loops. Favor places tied to his own formative ground / aches redeemed (woodland, red-dirt gem country, dark-sky country, family mountains).
3. **Hidden in plain sight, marked by a small detail.** Expect the box near something ordinary/overlooked, identifiable by a tiny distinguishing mark — his signature trick.
4. **Read names and themes associatively, never arithmetically.** Hiddenite, Alexander, lions, "Treasure Island," "Make It Make Sense" — he plants winks. No letter/number ciphers (he forbids them).
5. **Think from above.** Bird's-eye / vantage / "where you stand changes everything" recurs; a hide with a high open view, or one best understood from an aerial perspective, is on-brand.
6. **Nature has a palette he pictured.** He associates each box with "the colors of nature that surround" it and "similar flowers" — blue/pink wildflower country, oak shade, water nearby.
7. **Faith + abduction are sanctioned by the author.** Ch 5 ("ethereal treasure map," "have faith") and Ch 21 ("Make It Make Sense… consider more") explicitly invite intuitive, best-story inference. The creative pass is doing what he asked.
8. **Convergence over cleverness.** He warns against confirmation bias (Ch 8, 9: "let them guide you in the direction they want you to take," "look both ways before crossing"). One clever read = maybe; multiple independent reads landing on the same small area = a lead.
