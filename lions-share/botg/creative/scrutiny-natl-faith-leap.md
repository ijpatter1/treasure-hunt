# Coherence Scrutiny — lens `natl-faith-leap`

Charitable coherence review. Each leap is KEPT unless it is (a) internally incoherent /
self-contradictory, (b) built on a CIPHER (letter/number/syllable counting), or (c)
breaks a HARD CONSTRAINT (private land, dangerous, buried, not-in-US, refuted by a known
fact). Story-strength rated {vivid / solid / thin}. "what-would-confirm" is a thing to
GO CHECK, never a reason to reject.

Source: `botg/creative/leaps-natl-faith-leap.md` (13 leaps + convergence notes).

## Leap 1 — Lion's Share lives where the LION lives (title-as-place; Grandfather/Linville)
**KEEP.** Coheres cleanly: "name the prize after the place" reuses the same author tic
as Hiddenite and Alexander (three-times pattern = the convergence backbone, not a
one-off). No cipher (pure association). The Grandfather candidate is public, road-near,
snowy — no hard-constraint break. The ONLY soft worry is the leap admits it has no
enumerated "Lion"-named feature yet ("to be enumerated with maps"), but absence of the
pin is a thing-to-check, not an incoherence.
- **Story-strength: solid.** (vivid as a thesis; "solid" because the specific landform is still unfilled)
- **What-would-confirm:** Run a GNIS / OSM query for "Lion / Lion's Den / Lion Rock /
  Leonard's / Lyon" toponyms inside lat 35.8–36.0 N, lon 81–82.2 W. A real Lion-named
  PUBLIC feature in-band would convert this from thesis to pin.

## Leap 2 — "There's Treasure Inside" = gem-mine interior country (Hiddenite / Spruce Pine belt)
**KEEP.** Strong internal logic: title-as-instruction, item list is gem-dense, and the
hide is *near* (not in, not dug) a known public walk-in mine — explicitly respects the
"not buried" rule. Two of the four literal anchors (Hiddenite, Bakersville) ARE gem
towns, so this isn't a fresh unsupported claim, it re-derives the corridor from the item
theme. No cipher. Emerald Hollow / Hiddenite is public pay-to-dig land; the hide would
be on adjacent public land, which must be verified (the mine itself is commercial/private).
- **Story-strength: vivid.**
- **What-would-confirm:** Confirm a PUBLIC (state/federal/county) parcel within ~3 mi of
  Emerald Hollow or in the Spruce Pine mineral district — the hide can't be on the
  commercial mine's private acreage. Check Hiddenite-area county natural areas.

## Leap 3 — "An Exercise in Faith" → a place named for faith / Elisha Mitchell martyr (Mount Mitchell)
**KEEP.** The faith-named-place move is association, not cipher. The Elisha-Mitchell read
is genuinely strong: a man who *died proving his measurement* maps onto Ch5 "Faith" +
Ch14 "Fail Forward" + Ch15 "Don't Give Up" — three subtitles, one story = real coherence,
not a stretch. Highest peak E of Mississippi = "Lion's Share of elevation" is a clean
"biggest box → biggest mountain" intuition. Public state park, snowy, NC-128 to summit
(well within 3 mi). No hard-constraint break.
- **Story-strength: vivid.**
- **What-would-confirm:** Identify the specific *off-summit* public spot (summit is
  crowded/developed — the hide wants a quiet, walkable, non-dangerous pocket within the
  park). Cross-check whether any in-book text actually names Mitchell/measurement vs.
  this being pure thematic resonance.

## Leap 4 — Ch21 "look beyond ancient myths" → Alexander the PLACE not the myth (Alexander Co / Rocky Face)
**KEEP — strongest single leap in the file.** It applies JCB's OWN confirmed solve-method
(Ch21 = "Make It Make Sense," look past the myth) literally. Not a cipher: it's
substituting the modern referent of the same word, the identical move that "unlocks"
Hiddenite. Double-hit (Ch4 Chivor→Hiddenite AND Ch21 Alexander→Alexander Co) on one

- **Story-strength: vivid.**
- **What-would-confirm:** Map the UNSEARCHED ~80% of Rocky Face Mtn Recreational Area
  against the 3-mi-of-road + walkable + winter-snow filters; pick the highest-coherence
  sub-acre for a re-walk. This is the top BOTG re-target.

## Leap 5 — "Sleeping lion" silhouette → ridge that LOOKS like a lion / Rocky FACE
**KEEP (with a caution flag, not a cut).** Coheres as a name-and-shape double (Lion = face
= "Rocky FACE") and it usefully reframes the prior near-miss as "right mountain, wrong
acre." Stacks with Leaps 1 and 4. The honest weakness: "many monadnocks have leonine
profiles" is pareidolia — almost any ridge can be seen as a lion, so the shape half is
weak; the NAME half ("Rocky Face" carrying "face") carries the real weight. Not a cipher,
no constraint break, so it survives — but rate the shape-claim as thin.
- **Story-strength: thin** (name-pun solid; the literal "looks-like-a-lion" landform claim is the weak part).
- **What-would-confirm:** A specific named "Lion / Sphinx / Profile / Face" ROCK feature
  on the Brushy Mtns escarpment (photo or GNIS entry), not a subjective "kinda looks like."

## Leap 7 — Ch14 fire/furnace → burn-and-rebirth landform (Linville Gorge) OR quartz source (Spruce Pine)
**KEEP, but split the two branches.**
- Spruce Pine branch: **KEEP, vivid.** "World's purest quartz feeds the world's furnaces"
  + boxes forged in fire by a blacksmith in the SAME belt (Mitchell Co) = tight
  re-convergence on the forge town. Strong coherence.
- Linville Gorge branch: **KEEP but flag a HARD-CONSTRAINT caution.** The leap itself says
  "rim ... road-rimmed ... within 3 mi" and "not dangerous" — fine AS WRITTEN (rim
  overlooks, not the gorge interior). But the constraint says NO cliff/ledge/dangerous
  terrain; Linville Gorge interior is exactly that. So keep ONLY the rim/overlook reading;
  any descent-into-gorge version would break the danger constraint and must be cut.
- No cipher in either branch.
- **Story-strength: vivid (Spruce Pine) / solid (Linville rim, danger-bounded).**
- **What-would-confirm:** For Spruce Pine, a public parcel near the quartz district within
  3 mi of road. For Linville, restrict candidate points to RIM overlooks reachable by
  short non-technical walk (Wiseman's View, etc.) and verify no exposure.

bounded to safe rim/established-overlook readings to stay inside the no-danger constraint;
(2) Leap 5's "looks-like-a-lion" shape claim is pareidolia — lean on the NAME, not the silhouette.
