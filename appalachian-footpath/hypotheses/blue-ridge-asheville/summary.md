## Current Status

Initial hypothesis formed based on author's explicit childhood connection to the Blue Ridge Mountains near Asheville, NC. The theory proposes that the treasure map depicts a specific AT section in this region that can be identified through geographic overlay analysis.

---

## Strongest Evidence

1. **Direct childhood connection**: Author explicitly states he "had fairly close access to the trail as a child" in the Blue Ridge Mountains near Asheville, NC - this is the only specific geographic connection mentioned in the chapter.

2. **"Magical place" statement**: Author describes realizing "this would be a magical place to leave a treasure" about the AT, in direct context of discussing his childhood experiences near Asheville.

3. **Thematic alignment with dedication**: The Saint Augustine quote "Where your pleasure is, there is your treasure" directly connects the author's stated childhood pleasure hiking this section to where the treasure might be.

## Biggest Challenges

2. **AT spans 14 states**: The trail offers thousands of potential hiding spots; personal connection doesn't guarantee that's where he chose to hide it.
