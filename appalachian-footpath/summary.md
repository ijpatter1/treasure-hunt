## Chapter Summary

Chapter XXVII presents the Appalachian Footpath treasure box, the most ornate of the five treasure boxes created by Jon Collins-Black. The author reveals his childhood connection to the Appalachian Trail near Asheville, North Carolina, where he grew up hiking through the Blue Ridge Mountains. This personal history inspired him to choose "somewhere along The Appalachian Trail" as the hiding location for this treasure.

Unlike the previous three Part Two chapters (which used a poem, fictional story, and games as their clue mechanisms), this chapter takes a "more traditional" approach: a hand-drawn treasure map. The author describes watching his son Aiden draw in his room as the inspiration for creating this map. He acknowledges that the path may be "less obvious" than ideal but states that "all that's left for you to do is follow this map."

The box itself is unique among the five treasures as the only one featuring both a combination lock and puzzle box mechanism. Seth Gould created the distinctive blue steel coloring using a technique he originally demonstrated on a simple bolt. The box contains more items from the book's main chapters than any other Part Two box, along with a laminated clue to help find the Lion's Share.
