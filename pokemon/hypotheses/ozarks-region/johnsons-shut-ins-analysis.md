## Overview

Johnson's Shut-Ins State Park is a 9,432-acre public recreation area in the St. Francois Mountains region of the Missouri Ozarks. The park features unique billion-year-old volcanic rock formations along the East Fork Black River, creating Missouri's most famous natural swimming destination.

## Key Location Features

### The "Shut-Ins" Phenomenon
- River is "shut in" by 1.4-1.5 billion year old rhyolite rock
- Creates natural chutes, pools, and cascades
- Smooth, water-carved rock surfaces

### Rock Characteristics
- **Type:** Rhyolite (volcanic igneous rock)
- **Age:** ~1.4-1.5 billion years old
- **Color:** "Light gray through pink and red to dark purplish-red"
- **Texture:** Dense, fine-grained, smooth from water erosion

**Critical Note:** The p18 photo description mentions "smooth, rounded boulders" in "pinkish/purple tones" - this closely matches rhyolite coloration.

### 2005 Flood Event
- Taum Sauk reservoir breach sent 1.3 million tons of water through park
- Created visible boulder field near visitor center
- Boulders "carried down the mountain by the flood"
- This boulder field could match p18 photo

### Trails & Access
- 2.3-mile Shut-ins Trail (most popular)
- 3-mile Black River Trail System
- 1.4-mile Scour Trail
- Multiple access points within 3 miles of road
